# Oak lesson authoring guide (AUTHORING)

For every agent or person who writes or reviews Oak content. It applies `docs/design/contracts.md` (schemas, budgets, IDs, unlocks, grading) and `content/STYLE.md` (the code dialect). If this guide and those disagree, they win; fix this guide.

Start here, in this order: this guide, STYLE, your unit's spec (`content/specs/Uxx.md`), then the exemplar lessons `content/core/U01/L01` ("Heartbeat"), `content/core/U02/L01` ("High & Low"), for U03 on `content/core/U03/L02` ("Degrees in Code"), which also shows the Wave 3 shapes of §16, and for U05 on `content/core/U05/L01` ("Stacking Thirds"), which shows how chords are written, graded and made into a song (§17). All of them pass every check with the real grader; copy their shape, not their words. U06.L02 and U07.L08 are the U06 and U07 exemplars. From Wave 5: `content/core/U08/L01` ("Roots Down Low") shows a bass line written and graded against the chords (§18); U09.L02, U10.L01, U11.L05 and A1.L01 are the U09, U10, U11 and side-lesson exemplars. From Wave 6: `content/core/U12/L01` ("Major 7 & Minor 7") shows seventh chords written by name, built, heard and graded (§19); U13.L01, U14.L01, U15.L02, U16.L01 and U17.L02 are the exemplars of their units. From Wave 7: `content/advanced/J1/L01` ("Swing Feel & the Ride") is the advanced tracks' exemplar and the worked example of the corrections-pass standard (§20.2): every graded step checks what is heard, pins a wrong answer that uses the right function, and passes three other right answers; J2.L01, J3.L01, J5.L01, R1.L01, R3.L01 and A2.L01 are the exemplars of their units, written by the spec writers from the unit specs.

## 1. How content gets made

Content is authored **just in time**: always at least two units ahead of the learner, never the whole course up front. One unit goes through this pipeline:

1. **Unit spec** (content lead). `content/specs/Uxx.ts` says, per lesson: the one idea, the sound-first hook and its code, terms and the step that introduces each, can-dos, the tools the code uses, the step list with exercise and answer kinds and minutes, the Make with its one required rule and Band, the SRS links, the realistic mistakes the tests must pin, and how the lesson connects backward and forward. It also specs the unit's checkpoint items and capstone briefs. `content/validate/specs.test.ts` checks every spec against the registries (skill order in every legal order, unlock-before-use, terms, can-dos, families, budgets, the Band's meter, the hook evaluates and lints). `bun scripts/content/spec.ts` renders it to `content/specs/Uxx.md`, the document you read.
2. **Author.** One agent per lesson, from the spec, this guide, STYLE and the exemplars. Loop on `bun run content:check <lessonId>` until every section is PASS (§12).
3. **Music-theory review** (adversarial, §15.1).
4. **Playtest** (an agent that solves each step using only what earlier lessons taught, §15.2).
5. **Listen QA** on `/dev/listen`: a human hears new or changed **shown examples and reference solutions** only (§15.3).
6. **Merge**: green checks, then one commit.

Drills, fact cards and ladder levels L1–L4 are generated by code. You write lessons, checkpoints' authored items (§10), capstone briefs (§11), Band layers and repertoire entries.

A lesson is two files in `content/core/Uxx/Lyy/` (or `content/advanced/J1/L03/`):

- `lesson.ts`: the typed truth. Metadata, steps, every piece of code, every exercise, grading, tests.
- `lesson.mdx`: prose only. One `<Step id="S3">` block per step. No code fences, no numbers that grading depends on, no unlock or skill declarations.

`bun run content:new <lessonId>` scaffolds both files from the syllabus row with code that already lints in that lesson. Replace everything in it.

## 2. The authoring checklist

Work through it in order. Each line names the check that catches a slip.

1. Read your lesson's section of `content/specs/Uxx.md` and the lesson before and after it (connects).
2. Copy the metadata: `id`, `title` and `introduces`/`practises` exactly as in the syllabus (check 3, Map); `key`, `meter`, `bpm` from the spec; `terms` with the step that introduces each; `canDo`.
3. Write the hook first and listen to it (`/dev/listen` or evaluate it). It must sound like music within seconds: a groove, a line over the Band, never a single test tone.
4. Write the steps in the spec's order. Every code string starts with the tempo line, uses only tools unlocked here (check 4), evaluates without errors and is not silent (check 6). Factor repeated code into constants and helpers, as the exemplars do (`groove(bpm)`, `mel(notes)`).
5. For every graded code step: the reference solution, one equivalent answer written differently that passes, the unchanged starter failing with `code.unchanged`, and at least two realistic mistakes with different feedback codes (§9). Run `bun run content:check <id> --verbose` and copy the codes the grader really emits; never guess them.
6. Hints are four rungs: nudge (a question), pointer (where and which concept), partial (half the answer, or the same idea elsewhere), `"auto"` (check 8).
7. The Make: exactly one required rule that is the lesson's idea, a Band in the lesson's meter (§8), tests for the starter, a minimal sketch, a sketch beyond the brief and a sketch that fails with `rubric.unmet` (checks 12, 14).
8. Write `lesson.mdx`: short prose per step, the term in bold once in the `<OnPass>` of the step that introduces it, nothing that names a term earlier (check 14).
9. Budget: expert minutes add up to at most 9.25, the split at `wrapAt` holds, at most 3 graded steps, at most 12 steps (check 11). `content:check` prints the numbers.
10. `bun run content:check <id>`: every section PASS. Read the `advice:` lines; fix them or be sure they are plain English.
11. Theory: re-derive every musical claim in prose, hints and feedback (`bun run theory …`, or evaluate the code and read the events). Then hand over to review.

## 3. The lesson template

Every lesson is **Predict → Run → Investigate → Modify → Make** (PRIMM), ordered **sound → code → symbol**, and ends with a saved sketch. The default order of step kinds is:

`listen → predict → run → investigate → see → modify → (ear | sing | tap) → make`

| PRIMM stage | Step kind | Exercise kind (contracts §5.8) | What the learner does | Graded? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sound first | `listen` | `ListenEx` (or a passive block: `passive`, §16.5) | plays a loop, just listens | no (completes on one full loop) |
| **Predict** | `predict` | `PredictEx` (`hum`, `choose` or `count`) | hums or picks what the code will sound like, before running it | never gates; logged |
| **Run** | `run` | `RunInvestigateEx` with no questions | runs the code, sees the prediction against the result | no |
| **Investigate** | `investigate` | `RunInvestigateEx` | toggles layers, clicks the token or note that does X | light (predicate targets) |
| symbol | `see` | none | sees the staff or the term for what was just heard | no |
| explain | `concept` | none | reads a card of ≤ 120 words with 1–2 audio examples | no |
| **Modify** | `modify` | `CodeModifyEx`, `CodeFillEx`, `ErrorHuntEx` | changes the code to hit a target | **yes** |
| Modify (variant) | `parsons` | `ParsonsEx` | orders chord or bar blocks | yes |
| retrieval | `quiz` | `FactEx`, `KeyboardEx`, `StaffReadEx` | answers one fact | yes |
| ear | `ear` | `IdentifyEx`, `DictationEx` (generator stimulus) | a mini-set that introduces a drill family | yes → SRS |
| voice | `sing` | `SingEx` (must declare `fallback`) | sings a pitch, degree or echo | yes → SRS; never gates |
| pulse | `tap` | `TapEx` | taps along, echoes or reads a rhythm | yes → SRS; never gates |
| dictation | `dictate` | `DictationEx` (by ear: `byEar`, §16.4), `TranscribeStepEx` | writes down what was heard | yes |
| writing | `notate` | `StaffWriteEx` (notes with a key, or a key signature: §16.1, §16.2) | writes notes or a key signature on the staff | yes |
| judgement | `compare` | `IdentifyEx` (two audio cards) | A/B: which ends more conclusively? | light |
| **Make** | `make` | `CodeMakeEx` | writes a 1–8 bar sketch over the Band and saves it | one required rule; never blocks saving |
| metacognition | `reflect` | none | rates a can-do statement | no |

Rules of the template:

1. **Sound → code → symbol inside every concept.** The learner hears or plays the thing, then reads or writes its code, and only then meets the staff view or the term. A term never appears before its sound.
2. A lesson teaches **one idea**. Its title names the idea; its `objective` is one sentence the learner could say afterwards.
3. `predict` comes before the first `run` of new material and asks for an inner-hearing guess (hum it, or choose).
4. Repeat Modify rather than adding a new concept. Two small Modify steps beat one large one.
5. The **last step is `make`**. From U01 on it plays over a Band layer (§7).
6. The warm-up and the Coda are engine-driven. Do not author review or wrap steps. Do not author a first drill set or first sing for a new family inside the lesson unless the budget still holds: by default the lesson **seeds** the family (`srs: [{ family, mode: 'seed' }]`) and the engine runs its blocked-intro sets in tomorrow's warm-up.
7. Mark a step `testOut` only when it is pure code mechanics (a mini-notation operator, `setcpm`, labels) that a programmer may already know (contracts §9.5).
8. Choose `wrapAt`: the step after which the learner has made something that stands alone. It is where a 15-minute session stops for the day.
9. A `concept` or `see` step has no exercise and therefore no pass text: introduce terms in a step that has one.
10. Tap, sing and listen steps are never among the graded checks (`graded` unset); `ear`, `quiz`, `modify` and `dictate` steps are.

## 4. Budgets (content CI enforces all of them)

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Lesson content at the 20-minute target (New + Make) | **13 minutes** after scaling |
| Scaling | every `step.minutes` is your **expert** estimate, in steps of 0.25; CI multiplies by **1.4**. Your own numbers must add up to ≤ **9.25** |
| Steps | ≤ **12** |
| Graded checks (`graded: true`) | ≤ **3** (an `AllEx` counts once; the Make's required rule does not count) |
| New terms | ≤ **3** (an antonym pair introduced together counts as one) |
| Panels per step in U00–U04 | at most **2 of {roll, staff, keyboard}** |
| Make | ≈ 2.5–3 expert minutes, exactly **one** `required` rule |
| Split for the 15-minute target | scaled part A (up to `wrapAt`) ≤ 8.5 min; scaled part B ≤ 9.5 min |
| Optional stretch steps (`optional: true`, 25-minute target only) | ≤ 5 scaled minutes in total |
| Prompt length | ≤ 60 words; concept cards ≤ 120 words |

Typical expert minutes: listen 0.5; predict 0.5–1; run 0.25–0.5; investigate 1; see 0.5; concept 1; modify 1–1.5; quiz 0.5–1; ear mini-set 1.25–2; sing 1.5; tap 1.25–1.5; make 2.5–3. If the lesson does not fit, cut scope or ask for a split in contracts §2; never shave the estimates.

Each unit from U02 on contains at least one **"Play from the page"** step: a `modify` or `dictate` step whose starter is staff-only (no code, no roll), answered in code.

## 5. Voice and microcopy

Write to one adult programmer who is new to music. Second person, short sentences, musical verbs, no hype. Calm, precise, never cute, never shaming.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| "Press Play and nod along." | "Let's dive into the wonderful world of rhythm!" |
| "Not quite: bars 2 and 4 differ. Listen to bar 2 again?" | "Wrong!" / "Oops!" / any exclamation mark on a failure |
| "That's it: 5 4 3 2 1. The tune walks home." (specific) | "Great job!" (generic praise) |
| "Hear how 7 leans up to 1." (sound first) | "The leading tone resolves to the tonic by semitone." (term first) |
| "degree 5 (G)" (degree first, letter in brackets) | "G, the fifth" |
| "Change **one number** so the tune ends at home." | "Modify the terminal scale index." |
| "Hints are free. They just tell Oak to bring this back sooner." | anything that frames a hint, a rest day or a slow day as a loss |

From the exemplars, before and after the edit that made them pass:

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Tap along for two bars: one tap with every kick and every snare. Space bar or any key." | "Tap along: one tap with every kick and every snare, eight taps in all. Any key works." | "bar" is U01.L01's third term, introduced three steps later; even "Space bar" counts. Say what to do in counts the learner already has. |
| Step title "One loop, one bar" | "Count the loop" | Titles are shown before the step: the same rule applies to them. |
| "Pick a tempo, fill the four beats, and let the band play along." | "Pick a tempo, give each of the four beats a sound, and let the band play along." | "fill" is U01.L07's term. The check advises on later terms; rephrase unless it is plain English. |
| Grader text "Bar 1, 3: right note, an octave higher." on the step that introduces the octave | `feedbackText: { "melody.octave": "Bar {bar}, {beatLabel}: the right letter, but {dir} than the target. Check the number after the c." }` | Catalog feedback may use words the lesson has not taught yet. Override it with the lesson's own image; keep the `{bar}` placeholders. |
| "Until now every sound was a drum." | "For nine lessons every sound you wrote was a drum." | The learner heard a piano in U00.L01. Every claim true, including the small ones. |
| "At 60 the drums hit once a second." (the groove had eighth hats) | "At 60 the kick and snare land once a second; at 120, twice a second." | Check the claim against the events, not your intention. |

Pass text names what the learner just did, then the term in bold if one is introduced: "That steady beat you tapped is the **pulse**." "The same line, one **octave** higher: every `c` moved up to the next `c`."

Rules:

1. Degrees are written 1-based with ♭/♯ (`1 2 ♭3 ♯4`); in code the bridge is always stated the same way: "`n` counts from 0, so degree 5 is `n` 4".
2. Singing copy uses **numbers** ("sing one, three, five") until U03.L08 introduces syllables; after that, syllables with numbers still shown.
3. Chord names in prose use the lead-sheet form (Cmaj7, Cm7♭5); code uses the STYLE spelling (`C^7`, `Cm7b5`). When both appear, show both once: "`C^7` (Cmaj7)".
4. Feedback says **where and how many** first ("2 notes differ, bars 2 and 4"). The catalog template usually suffices; override `feedbackText` only to add the lesson's own image or to avoid a term not yet taught.
5. Pass copy (`<OnPass>` in the MDX step) names what the learner just did and, if a term is introduced, bolds it once.
6. Never claim the app or the tutor heard the learner's audio beyond what the mic grader measured. Mic copy: "Your voice is analysed live on this computer. Nothing is recorded or sent." The sing widget and the mic set-up already show it; do not repeat it in a step's prose.
7. No guilt, no streak talk, no countdowns, no "you should". No emoji, no exclamation marks, no "simply", "easy", "obviously", "let's", "awesome". Sentence case in titles and buttons. (Check 14 fails all of these.)
8. Titles are 2–4 words and concrete ("Count to Four", "Lift the line").
9. Do not mention unlock mechanics in lesson prose; the Coda announces unlocks.
10. Prompts are at most 60 words and ask for one thing.

## 6. Views, notation and scaffold fading

1. Every pitched concept is shown in the linked views (code ↔ roll ↔ staff), but **not all at once early on**: in U00–U04 a step enables at most two of roll, staff and keyboard. Pick the two that serve the step.
2. Notation is rendered from code by the notation engine. Never hand-write ABC. Every example on a step with a staff panel must render with `approximate = false` and no warnings (check 9 engraves it; `staff: "perc"` engraves the drums on the percussion staff). Declare `swing` and `pickup` metadata where they apply (contracts §6.10, §6.11).
3. On the percussion staff, hits that start together are engraved as one chord with the shorter length: a `cr@2` under eighth hats reads as an eighth. When the staff must show a long value, play that example without the hats.
4. Staff reading is practised daily by the generated "Read it" family and once per unit by your "Play from the page" step.

## 7. Terms

1. Introduce a term only **after** the learner has heard or used the thing. It appears in bold exactly once, in the `<OnPass>` text of the step where it is named, and is declared in `lesson.ts` as `terms: [{ id: 'term.cadence', step: 'S4' }]`.
2. At most three per lesson. If you need a fourth, the lesson has two ideas. An antonym pair introduced together counts once (`pairedWith` in `content/terms.ts`); declare **both** halves in `terms` (`term.sharp` and `term.flat`, each with its step), because the tutor's introduced-terms list is built from what lessons declare.
3. Every term has a glossary entry in `content/terms.ts`: a one-sentence definition in sound-first words, one audio example (code, legal at the introducing lesson), the lesson that introduces it. The entries for U00–U02 exist; ask for others (§12).
4. **Glossary, not card, by default.** A term becomes a spaced-repetition fact card only if a later lesson tests it: `card: { testedIn: 'U06.L07' }`. CI checks that lesson exists and uses the term in a graded step.
5. Never put a term in a title, prompt, hint, choice label, prose or feedback override before its step's pass text. Hints may use any term introduced in earlier lessons or earlier in this lesson. Check 14 fails the first and advises (`advice:`) on terms of later lessons, which are often plain English ("rest of the bar"): rephrase or be sure.
6. The tutor receives the introduced-terms list and treats everything else as a preview; your lesson's `terms` are what make that list correct.

A Band written in major degrees (`band.two-chord-vamp`, `band.phrase-8bar`, `band.axis-4bar`, `band.doo-wop`, `band.three-chord`, `band.sections`) plays its bass and chords only under a major key: under a minor, pentatonic or blues key it would play the major key's chords, so a step in such a key takes `tracks: ["drums"]` or one of the tonic-written Bands above (`content/bands/index.test.ts` enforces it).

`followsLearnerKey: true` makes the Band take the key of the learner's `.scale()` (a pattern included, from U03.L05), so a learner who moves the tune keeps the backing. The degree rubric rules (`ends-on-degree`, `degree-set`) are judged in the exercise's declared key: until the grader reads the learner's `.scale()` there, a Make whose required rule counts degrees does not invite another key (U03.L02 S9 keeps C).

2. A Band carries no tempo line: it plays at the lesson's tempo, so its bar must have the lesson's number of beats. The Make check fails a Band written in another meter (`BAND_METERS` in `content/bands/index.ts`).
3. Band code is read-only for the learner and may use locked tools (it is a preview context). It must follow STYLE, pass the strict lint, and exist for every flavour.
4. The Band supplies the roles the learner is **not** writing. If the Make is a melody, the Band is drums + bass + chords; if the Make is a groove, the Band is bass + chords (`tracks: ["bass", "chords"]`).
5. A check that needs harmony under a learner melody reads a chord track **in the learner's code** (`harmony: "chords"`), with the Band on drums and bass only. The grader is not given the Band's code (neither content:check nor the app passes `ctx.band` yet), so a rule against `band.chords` reads no chords and can only ever say "not yet" (U06.L05, U07.L03–L07 and U07.CP were written this way). When the Band reaches grading, `harmony: "band.chords"` becomes usable.
6. A flavour changes sounds and the Band, **never the rubric and never the notes the learner must write**. Declare starter sound swaps in `flavours`; ship a passing solution test for each shipped flavour. A swap applies to learner-editable code only where that sound is already unlocked; otherwise the default (`piano`, `ebass`, the default kit) stays, and only the Band carries the flavour's sounds (contracts §5.10 rule 8). In U01–U02 that means: no swaps at all.
7. The jazz-soul Band uses seventh chords and swing from U03 on; your prose must not name those ideas before their lessons. "Hear how the band leans back" is fine; "swing" as a term waits for U11 (the U01.L09 preview calls it a feel and does not add a term).
8. Use `band: false` only when silence around the part is the point, or in U00 (the onboarding loop is already a band); give the reason in a comment.

## 9. Exercise authoring checklist

For **every** exercise:

- [ ] `id` is a stable slug; `version` starts at 1 and is bumped only when grading semantics change.
- [ ] `skills` use node IDs or node categories from `content/skills.ts`; weights say what the step really tests.
- [ ] `prompt` asks for one thing. A learner who has done only the earlier lessons can do it.
- [ ] `hints` is the fixed tuple **[nudge, pointer, partial, reveal]** (contracts §5.3): a conceptual question → the place and the concept → one issue solved or the same idea elsewhere → `'auto'`. No hint uses a term not yet introduced. No hint below rung 4 contains the answer. (`hints: null` only for predict, listen and checkpoint items.)
- [ ] Every code string follows STYLE and uses only tools unlocked at or before this lesson; tools this lesson itself unlocks are usable from its first step (or the example is `preview: true` and read-only). Listen items cannot be `preview`: a locked tool in a hook goes in a `preview: true` example.
- [ ] You evaluated every code string and looked at the events. Four mistakes make no error and no silence, so only the events show them (STYLE §5.2, §5.3, §11): a chord in a dictionary that voices it wrongly (`Caug` in `triads`); `.n()` written after `.voicing()`; a fraction inside a string time argument (`.late("1/16")`); a control pattern whose values do not line up with the steps (`"1 .35 .6 .35"` over sixteen hats).
- [ ] `tests` are present (below). Rational numbers are reduced: `[1, 2]`, never `[2, 4]` (the schema refuses it).

For **code kinds** (`code-modify`, `code-fill`, `error-hunt`, `parsons` with code, `dictation` answered in code):

- [ ] At least one reference solution in `reference.solutions`; `[0]` is the canonical one and the hint-4 reveal. Every solution must grade 1.0.
- [ ] **At least one passing test that is not the reference** (an equivalent answer written differently: `bd*4` for `bd bd bd bd`, `setcpm(30)` for `setcpm(120/4)`, the same notes with a different `.gain()`).
- [ ] **At least two known-wrong variants, each expecting a different feedback code**: the realistic mistakes for this step (the unchanged starter, `/4` forgotten, the right rhythm displaced, the rest deleted while editing, the right notes in the wrong octave). `expect: 'fail'`, `feedback: ['rhythm.displaced']`.
- [ ] The starter itself fails: a test whose code is the starter with its markers removed, expecting `code.unchanged` (check 14).
- [ ] The matcher is the loosest one that still tests the idea: `rhythm` when sounds are free; `events` with `sound: "drum-role"` when the drums matter; `events` with `pitch: "absolute"` for note names; `transposition-invariant` when any key is fine; `pcset` for anything voiced. Never compare text.
- [ ] Constraints (`must-use`, `max-changed-tokens`, `tempo`, `labels`) only where the idea needs them, each with a feedback code.
- [ ] If values are randomized, they are `params` with a seeded generator, referenced as `{{param:x}}` / `{ param: 'x' }`; tests pin `params`.
- [ ] Exact-match steps avoid randomness in the code (`?`, `degradeBy`, …).

For **Make** steps:

- [ ] Exactly one `required: true` rule: the lesson's concept, and one that cannot be met by accident or failed by good music. Everything else is a suggestion.
- [ ] Tests: the starter (it never passes as it came: a starter that already meets the required rule fails with `make.unchanged`, one that does not fails with `rubric.unmet`; onboarding's preview Makes are exempt), one minimal passing sketch that changes something, one rich sketch that goes beyond the brief and still passes, one sketch that breaks the required rule and fails with `rubric.unmet`.
- [ ] No rule depends on singing or tapping.
- [ ] The Make's `title` is the saved sketch's default title (the lesson title otherwise): set it when the step heading names the sketch ("Sketch 002").

For **ear, sing, tap** steps:

- [ ] Stimuli come from a family generator (`stimulus: { src: 'generator', family, level }`) unless the lesson needs a fixed example. A generator stimulus has no tests in the lesson (the generator is tested in `@oak/drills`).
- [ ] `sing` declares `fallback`; tolerance is `cents: 'learner'`; tap tolerance is `windowMs: 'auto'` (check 14). Sung targets are degrees or stimulus-relative, so the engine can fold them into the learner's range. A degree pattern says what the learner sings on (`labels: "numbers"` before U03.L08, `"syllables"` from it) and, when it crosses home, the octaves (`octaves`). Only U00.L02's first note is `lenient` (§16.3).
- [ ] Tap tests are tap times in **seconds from the start of the stimulus** (the count-in is not counted). U01.L01's `beatTimes(bpm, n, every, offset)` builds them; test the on-beat taps (pass), half-speed taps and taps on the off-beats (fail), no taps (fail).
- [ ] Sing tests need recorded pitch fixtures, which do not exist yet: content CI lists them as **pending**, never as passes.

What CI adds on its own: **auto-mutants** (five single-token mutations of the reference must fail) run **only for exact-match kinds**; they are skipped for rubric, Make, compose and any matcher with a pass threshold below 1. **Listen QA** covers shown examples and reference solutions only, not starters and not wrong variants.

## 13. Mistakes the checks caught in the first lessons

- **A line that fights the Band.** The two-chord vamp plays C, then F, one chord a bar. A hook that ended bar 2 on `f#4` put F♯ against the chord's F, and a riff transposed by 2 and 4 put F♯ over F and G♯ over C. Evaluate the line with the Band (contracts §5.10) and keep held notes off the half step above a chord note; `course.test.ts` checks it for every shown example and reference. Transpose by 5 or 7 instead: the copies then fit the Band's chords.
- **A rule stated for one bar as if it were general.** "`@2` makes a word two beats long" is true of `bd@2 sd sd` and false of `cr@2 bd sd sd` (two fifths of the bar). Words share the bar by weight; say so, and give the beat count only for the bar on screen.
- **Telling the learner something is new when they have seen it.** `~` sits in the U00 loop; U01.L02 calls back to it instead of introducing it as new. The percussion staff of U01.L05 has five lines too; U02.L04 says so when it names the **staff**.

- **A term inside an ordinary word or title.** "Space bar" used "bar" two steps before U01.L01 introduced it; the step title "One loop, one bar" did too. Check 14 matches whole words, plurals included, in titles, prompts, hints, labels and prose.
- **`<Degrees scale="C:major">` and `<Keyboard degrees scale>`**, never `key=`: React keeps `key` for itself, so the component never saw it.
- **`<CodeRef fn>` takes catalog names**, the way the toolbox card is titled: `~`, `@`, `[a b]`, `setcpm`, `room`. Tool IDs (`tb.mini.rest`) are refused.
- **A second line of the same kind.** STYLE §2 allows `mel2`, but the grader does not yet give a digit-suffixed label a role, so a `tracks` rule counts `mel` + `mel2` as one melody. Label a second melody line `vox` until it does (U02.L05, U02.L06).
- **Two choice questions in one investigate step.** A test response carries one `answer.choice`, checked against every choice question, so such a step cannot have a passing test. Combine one choice question with one number or click question.
- **Guessed feedback codes.** `events.onset` and `events.sound` are not codes; the grader says `rhythm.missing` / `rhythm.extra`. The schema refuses unknown codes before any grading runs. Use `--verbose`.
- **Unreduced rationals.** `[2, 4]` in an `onsets` rule; write `[1, 2]`.
- **Catalog text that teaches too early.** `melody.octave` says "an octave higher" on the step that introduces the octave, and `melody.wrong-degree`, `melody.missing-note` and `melody.extra-note` speak in degrees and solfège ("you have 5 (sol)") long before U03.L02 teaches them. Override them in `feedbackText`; before U03.L02 check 14 fails a pitched code step that does not.
- **A locked operator in a hook.** `< >` unlocks in U01.L07; a U01.L04 hook that alternated bars with it failed the lint. Spell the alternatives as separate listen items, or make the example `preview: true`.
- **A patterned `.transpose("<0 2>")`** is an interval string to the lint (U04.L02); use a number, or a preview example.
- **A checkpoint skill nobody measures.** R03 in U01.CP had no family and no authored item until the tempo item took it.
- **Capstone rules the grader does not implement.** C0's §6.6 custom checks returned `rubric.unknown` on every attempt; the check now refuses unimplemented rules.
- **A Band in the wrong meter.** A 4/4 Band under a 3/4 Make plays four beats against three; use `band.waltz-vamp`.
- **Rhythms on the percussion staff.** A long crash under eighth hats engraves as an eighth (§6 rule 3).
- **The octave above home before U03.L03.** `n` 7 and negative `n` are `tb.n.range` (U03.L03): in U03.L02 the lint refuses `n("0 .. 7")` and `n("-3")`. Degree 1' waits a lesson.
- **`.transpose()` after `.scale()`.** The lint reads it as a key change and refuses it: move a degree line with a patterned `.scale()`, and write a harmony line a named distance away in note names (`note("c4 e4").transpose("3M")`).
- **A step param inside code.** `{{param:x}}` in a stimulus or starter does not evaluate in check 6; a fixed stimulus with two audio cards (U03.L02 S6) gives the same contrast.
- **A word that waits.** "Cadence" is U06.L04's term: in U03 the chords that set home are "a few chords that set home". The spec's conventions list every such word for its unit.
- **A degree rule in another key.** A Make that said "any key you like" graded `degree-set` in C while the learner's line was in G; the prompt now keeps the declared key (§8).
- **Numerals before they are taught.** A `progression` or `contains-rn` rule's detail reads "chords go I; the brief asks for I V vi IV". Before U06.L02 override `rubric.unmet` with the lesson's own words; the override serves every rule of the rubric, suggestions included, so keep it neutral ("{label}: not yet.", U05.L01 S8).
- **A cluster that passes as a chord.** `progression` reads each chord's triad core, and today it calls C D E a C chord. Test "steps instead of 3rds" where a `pcset` matcher grades the chord (U05.L01 S4), and use `chord-qualities` (§17.6) when a Make must insist on triads.

## 15. Review

### 15.1 Checklist for the music-theory reviewer

Work adversarially: assume each claim is wrong until you have re-derived it with the theory CLI (`bun run theory <op> …`) or by evaluating the code (`evalHeadless` and `midisOf` from `@oak/grader/testing`).

- [ ] Every note, interval, chord, Roman numeral, cadence name and key named in prose, hints, feedback and answers is correct **for the code shown**, in the declared key. Check spellings (each letter once in a scale; F♯ not G♭ in G major).
- [ ] Every "this sounds like …" claim is true of the evaluated events, not of the author's intention. Evaluate; do not read the mini-notation by eye.
- [ ] `n` values and stated degrees agree (degree = `n` + 1); chromatic step strings follow STYLE §6.
- [ ] Chord symbols resolve in the dictionary used and are not ✗ cells of STYLE §5.2; voiced chords contain the pitch classes the prose says they contain. A chord's name is checked against its symbol, not guessed from a rootless or two-note voicing.
- [ ] In a piece with sections, each section's length is a multiple of its pattern's length, and any key change is written after `arrange()` (STYLE §9, §10).
- [ ] Before U07.L08 no example or tune starts with a pickup; before U13.L03 none needs a tie or a dot (contracts §2.8).
- [ ] Cadence, function and schema labels use Oak's vocabulary (contracts feedback and schema lists) and are right for the bars named.
- [ ] Terms are introduced after their sound, at most three, each once; nothing later-taught is used earlier.
- [ ] The reference solution is the most natural answer, not a trick; the "equivalent" passing test really is musically equivalent.
- [ ] The Make's required rule tests the lesson's idea and cannot be met by accident or failed by good music.
- [ ] Repertoire: title, year, public-domain status and provenance are right; the answer key matches the cited source; no modern arrangement, no lyrics from a non-PD text.
- [ ] Swing, meter, tempo unit and pickup metadata match the code and the notated result.
- [ ] Nothing in the lesson contradicts contracts or STYLE (conventions are not re-litigated in lessons).

Report each finding with the step ID, the claim, the evidence (CLI output or events) and a proposed fix.

### 15.2 Checklist for the playtest agent

You are a learner who has completed exactly the lessons before this one. You may use only tools unlocked so far and only terms introduced so far.

- [ ] Solve every step from the prompt alone, then with hint 1, hint 2, hint 3. Record the first rung at which the step became solvable. A step that needs rung 3 for a first-time learner is too hard or under-explained.
- [ ] Try the two most likely mistakes on each graded step; confirm the feedback names the place and helps (`--verbose` prints the text). Report any pass that should fail and any fail that should pass, with the code.
- [ ] Try a correct answer written differently from the reference (other sound, other octave, `*` versus repetition). It should pass unless the prompt forbade it.
- [ ] In the Make: submit the starter unchanged, a minimal answer, and an ambitious answer that goes beyond the brief. The ambitious one must not be marked wrong.
- [ ] Check every prompt and hint for words not yet taught, and for answers given away early.
- [ ] Time yourself per step and compare with `minutes × 1.4`. Report steps that run over.
- [ ] Run the lesson in Quiet mode: every sing step must have a working fallback and nothing may block.
- [ ] Run it at the 15-minute target: the split at `wrapAt` must leave part A satisfying on its own.
- [ ] Note anything that sounds bad: muddy low chords, a melody fighting the Band, a starter that is silent or harsh.

### 15.3 Listen QA (human)

On `/dev/listen`, for each new or changed shown example and reference solution: it sounds like what the prose says; the balance is pleasant on headphones; the notation matches; the Band supports and does not mask the learner's part. Verdicts are OK / Wrong sound / Wrong notation / Musically weak / Wrong theory, each with a note. A release build requires every such item to be OK.

### 16.1 Notes on a staff with a key signature

A `notate` step with `StaffWriteEx`. The staff input prints the key signature (`key`); a note entered on a line takes its sharp or flat, as on paper, and the answer is the sounding spelling (`F#4:4`). What is compared is the `expect`:

```ts
answer: { ui: "staff-input", clef: "treble", bars: 2, key: { tonic: "G", mode: "major" },
  palette: { durations: ["4", "2"], accidentals: true, ties: false, rests: true } },
// pitch only: spelled notes in order, lengths ignored
expect: { e: "pitches", spelled: ["G4", "B4", "D5", "F#5"], order: "sequence", withOctave: true },
// or pitch and rhythm: against a reference (code, never hand-written)
expect: { e: "events", reference: { solutions: [TUNE_IN_G], bars: 2 },
  matcher: { m: "events", pitch: "absolute", rhythm: "onsets+durations", durations: true } },
```

Tests answer with `{ staff: "G4:4 B4:4 D5:4 F#5:4" }` (the note-list DSL). A bass or grand staff (`clef: "bass"`) waits for U05.L06.

### 16.2 A key signature, in order

The same staff input in `mode: "key-signature"`: the learner places the accidentals at the start of the staff, in the order written. `order: "sequence"` also checks the order (F C G D A E B, or B E A D G C F); the default `set` checks only which ones.

```ts
answer: { ui: "staff-input", mode: "key-signature", clef: "treble", bars: 1,
  palette: { durations: [], accidentals: true, ties: false, rests: false } },
expect: { e: "key-signature", key: { tonic: "D", mode: "major" }, order: "sequence" },
tests: [{ name: "F# then C#", response: { answer: { text: ["F#", "C#"] } }, expect: "pass" },
        { name: "C# first", response: { answer: { text: ["C#", "F#"] } }, expect: "fail", feedback: ["keysig.order"] },
        { name: "F# only", response: { answer: { text: ["F#"] } }, expect: "fail", feedback: ["keysig.count"] }],
```

A wrong order earns `keysig.order` only with `order: "sequence"`; with `set` it passes. A missing or extra accidental is `keysig.count`, sharps for a flat key `keysig.type`, a wrong accidental `keysig.wrong`; a wrong answer earns partial credit below 1, still a fail. `mode: "key-signature"` and `e: "key-signature"` go together (`staff.key-signature-mode`). The "name the key" direction is a `fact` with `text` answers (`normalize: "key"`) or a card of `deck.key.signature`.

### 16.3 Singing a degree pattern, sight-singing, the first note

A `sing` step whose targets are degrees, with what the learner sings on and, when the pattern crosses home, the octave of each degree (0 = from home up to 7, 1 = the octave above, −1 below). U03.L02 S8:

```ts
mode: "degree",                       // the cue sets the key, then the learner sings the targets
stimulus: { src: "code", code: deg("<[0 2 4 2] 0>"), bars: 2, showCode: false },  // Oak's model ("Hear it")
cue: { context: "cadence", countInBars: 1, click: false, replays: -1 },
target: { from: "degrees", degrees: ["1", "3", "5", "3", "1"] },   // 1-3-5-1' adds octaves: [0, 0, 0, 1]
labels: "numbers",                    // "syllables" from U03.L08 (check 14 refuses them before)
tol: { cents: "learner", octave: "global", driftCompCents: 30, minCoverage: 0.4, passHitRate: 0.8 },
feedbackText: { "sing.drift": "…numbers only, before U03.L08…" },
fallback: "skip",
```

`mode: "sight"` is sight-singing: the target is shown (a code stimulus with `showStaff: true`, or the degree numbers) and only the cue plays; Oak plays the target after the attempt. `mode: "echo"` plays the target first. `octaves` has one entry per degree (`sing.octaves-length`).

`tol.lenient: true` is the one exception to the tolerance ladder: U00.L02's first sung note, where any sustained pitch within ±100¢ of the target after octave folding passes (contracts §8.1 item 8). It needs `mode: "pitch"` (`sing.lenient-mode`), and check 14 refuses it anywhere else.

Sing tests need recorded pitch fixtures (`{ pitchFixture }`); until they exist, content CI lists sing tests as pending.

### 16.4 By-ear transcription

A `dictate` step with `DictationEx` and `byEar`: the source plays with its code, roll and staff hidden; the learner writes code and switches between the source and their own code at the same bar (A/B), at the rates offered. The transposition rule is stated to the learner and must match the matcher's pitch mode (`by-ear.accepts-matcher`): `exact` = `absolute`, `any-octave` = `octave-free`, `any-key` = `transposition-invariant`.

```ts
kind: "dictation", domain: "melody",
stimulus: { src: "code", code: SWING_LOW, bars: 4, showCode: false },
cue: { context: "tonic-chord", countInBars: 1, click: false, replays: -1 },
answer: { ui: "code", starter: `${TEMPO}mel: n("⟪0 ~ ~ ~⟫").scale("C4:major").s("piano")` },
reference: { solutions: [SWING_LOW], bars: 4 },
matcher: { m: "melody", pitch: "transposition-invariant", wPitch: 0.8, wRhythm: 0.2, rhythmTol: [1, 16] },
byEar: { ab: true, rates: [1, 0.75, 0.5], accepts: "any-key" },
```

The answer is code (`by-ear.code-answer`); `rates` start at 1 (`by-ear.rates`). Tests are code like any code step: the reference, the tune in another key (passes under `any-key`), a wrong first note, a missing note, the unchanged starter.

### 16.5 Passive listening

A `listen` step with a `passive` block: a stream of generated examples from a family, each named as it plays (`reveal: "with"`) or just after (`"after"`), with nothing to answer. It feeds no evidence and no SRS and completes when the stream ends (or on Continue). `items` may then be empty, or hold the hook that opens the step.

```ts
kind: "listen",
items: [{ code: HOOK, caption: "Home, then a 5th, a 3rd, a 6th and the octave.", bars: 4 }],
passive: { family: "fam.int.in-key", level: 3, items: 12, reveal: "with", gapBeats: 2 },
```

### 17.1 Chords in code: stack them, grade them as chords

From U05.L01 a chord is a stack of degrees, `n("[0,2,4]")` over `.scale("C3:major")`: "skip a degree, twice" from the root. Grade any step whose answer is a chord with the `pcset` matcher (pitch classes per onset; `bass: true` when the lowest note matters), never with `events` and never by text: `[9,7,5]` and `[-2,0,2]` are the same chord as `[5,7,9]`, and one of them is your "equivalent answer" test. `voicing()` output (U05.L08 on) is graded only this way (contracts §6.7). The worked example is U05.L01 S4:

```ts
code: { starter: code(chords("<[0,2,4] [4,6,8] ⟪[5]⟫ [3,5,7]>")) },
reference: { solutions: [code(chords("<[0,2,4] [4,6,8] [5,7,9] [3,5,7]>"))], bars: 4 },
matcher: { m: "pcset", perOnset: true, bass: true, tracks: ["chords"] },
constraints: [{ c: "edit-only-regions", feedback: "code.edit-outside" }],
feedbackText: { "harmony.pcset": "Bar {bar}: your chord has {got}; the target has {exp}. Stack from the bass note: …" },
```

A chord outside the key's triads (A major in C, C augmented) is a note list, `note("[a3,c#4,e4]")`; chromatic `n` steps wait for U15.L01. `chord()` and `.voicing()` are locked until U05.L08 (the voicing gate, contracts §3.1 rule 6) and appear before it only in `preview: true` examples.

### 17.4 By ear in parts, bass first

A by-ear transcription (§16.4) of a loop with chords is taken one track at a time (feature `by-ear.parts`): the bass, then the chords. Each part names its track, a label the learner reads, its matcher and pass mark; the step's own `matcher` grades the whole at the end and must still carry the pitch mode `accepts` names (an `all` of the parts' matchers does).

```ts
byEar: { ab: true, rates: [1, 0.75, 0.5], accepts: "exact", parts: [
  { track: "bass", label: "The bass: one note a bar", matcher: { m: "melody", pitch: "absolute", wPitch: 0.8, wRhythm: 0.2, rhythmTol: [1, 8], track: "bass" } },

] },
```

A part graded on harmony needs a bass part before it (`by-ear.bass-first`); each track appears once (`by-ear.parts-tracks`).

### 17.5 Voice leading and the voicing gate

`voice-leading` reads a hand-voiced chord track as voices **in the order the notes are written**, so `[c4,a4,f4]` is a leap plus a crossing even though it sounds like `[c4,f4,a4]`: teach "keep the three in order, lowest first". `maxMovePerVoice: 2` ("every voice moves by a step or stays") suits loops whose neighbouring chords share a note (C G Am F; U05.L07's Make). Where IV meets V nothing is shared and one voice must fall a 3rd, so I–IV–V–I needs `maxMovePerVoice: 3` (the gate check, U05.CP). The gate check adds (feature `voice-leading.gate`): `voices: 3` (every chord has three notes), `commonTones: true` (a shared pitch class stays put in its voice), `noCrossing: true` (voices keep their order), `bassFree: true` (a root bass inside the track is left out of the motion limits). The gate check is the exercise `U05.L07/hand-voiced-cadence` (the id is fixed: toolbox `VOICING_GATE`), graded with `all` of `pcset` per onset and that rubric rule; it must pass at 0.9 with nothing revealed. U05.L08's first step is the same check in another key with `gateCheck: true` and the id `hand-voiced-cadence-again` (a `variants` entry of the gate), shown only while the gate is unmet (feature `step.gate-check`). It may not use the tools it guards.

### 17.6 Rubric rules for harmony and melody over chords

Graded: `progression` (`rn` or a `schema`, `allowRotation`, `track`, `bars: [from, to]` to compare only those bars, C2's "bars 1–6 loop"), `cadence` (`types`, `at`), `chord-tones-strong` (`melody`, `harmony`: a chord track of the learner's code, see §8 rule 5; `strength`, `minRatio`), `contains-rn` (`rn`, `min`, `mustResolve`), `harmonic-rhythm` (`changesPerBar`), `voice-leading` (§17.5), `motif-reuse` (`shape: "steps"` counts a diatonic sequence), `range`, `ends-on-degree`, `degree-set`, `chord-qualities` (`contains`, `anyOf` with `min`, `subsetOf`, or `min` triads; a cluster is not a triad), `chord-inversions` (`min`, `of: [1, 2]`), `non-chord-tones` (`roles`: passing, neighbour, appoggiatura, suspension; `min`; U07.L04–L05), `pickup` (`track`, `units`: the line starts with a pickup in bar 0, U07.L08). A lesson Make still has exactly one required rule.

What the grader reads, so a Make can be passed by the music it asks for:

- **Two chords in a bar: roots first.** `.add()` keeps the structure of the pattern on its left, so `n("[0,2,4]").add(n("<[5 3]>"))` plays only vi; write `n("<[5 3]>").add(n("[0,2,4]"))` (U06.L08 teaches this).
- **An arpeggiated `n` stack is not read as a chord** (each note sounds alone), while `chord(…).voicing().arp(…)` is (U06.L07's Make starts in chord names for this reason).
- **`motif-reuse` reads one period of the line**: four identical bars are a 1-bar loop and score 0; a sequence (the idea moved) counts. At `minRatio: 0.5` it needs about half the bars to repeat an earlier shape.
- **Progression and cadence rules judge in the declared key**: a Make that requires them does not invite another key.
- **`rn-palette` with `allow: ["inversions"]` grades the bass** (`V6` against `V`).
- **Chord symbols in `chord("…")`**: `o` for °, `aug` for +, never a slash (contracts §0.3). Unknown symbols (`Cmaj`) fail lint.

"Play from the page" with a lead sheet is a `modify` step whose target is shown only as the lead sheet; "sing from the page" is a `sing` step in `mode: "sight"` with a code stimulus and `showLeadSheet: true`.

## 18. The Wave 5 shapes (bass, form, minor, blues, sound)

### 18.1 A bass line against the chords (the worked example: U08.L01)

The chords a bass is judged against are a `chords:` line in the learner's code (the grader is not given the Band, §8 rule 5), so the Band plays `tracks: ["drums"]`. A bass in degrees is an `events` match on its own track; "the root under each chord" is a `matches` rule with a `harmony` matcher at level `bass`, which compares the lowest note sounding at each chord change with the chord's root. U08.L01 S7, graded today:

```ts
code: { starter: code(chords("<0 4 5 3>"), bass("0")) },          // C under every chord: fails (1 bar of 4)
rubric: [{ id: "roots-on-the-one", label: "Each chord's root in the bass on its beat 1", required: true,
  skills: ["B01"], rule: "matches", min: 0.75,
  reference: { solutions: [code(chords("<0 4 5 3>"), bass("<0 4 5 3>"))], bars: 4 },
  matcher: { m: "harmony", level: "bass", qualityDepth: "triad", inversion: false, harmonicRhythm: "given" } }],
feedbackText: { "rubric.unmet": "{label}: not yet." },             // serves every rule, so neutral
```

A bass that repeats, holds or decorates its roots passes (eighths on the root, `.rootNotes(2).struct(…)`, root–5th); the grader names each miss ("Chord 2 (bar 2): bass is C; you should hear G", `harmony.bass`). The S4 modify step grades a bass written in degrees with `{ m: "events", pitch: "octave-free", rhythm: "onsets", durations: false, tracks: ["bass"] }` and pins the off-by-one (`<0 6 4 5>` for I vi IV V, `melody.wrong-degree`).

The new rule `bass-line` (planned) says more: `roots: { on: "change" | "downbeat", minRatio }`, `members: { allowed: ["root", "fifth", "octave", "third", "approach"], minRatio, each? }` (U08.L02: only roots, 5ths and octaves), `approach: { min, kinds?: ["diatonic", "chromatic"] }` (U08.L03: approach notes into the changes). At least one of the three (`rubric.bass-line`). The kick lock is `groove-lock` (`kick`, `bass`, `min`; planned for content, graded by the grader).

### 18.2 Sections: `section` on any rule, form, contrast, the form answer

Every rubric rule may read one part of the piece with `section` (feature `rubric.section`): `{ section: "verse" }` (the `nth` one when it recurs), `{ bars: [12, 12] }` (1-based, inclusive: "bar 12 contains V"), or `"last"`. ### 18.3 Minor keys

In a key whose 3rd is ♭3 (`MINOR_THIRD_MODES`: the minors, minor pentatonic, blues) a degree pad offers `b3`, never `3`, and every degree of the right answer (`minor.degree-pad`); a sung degree target writes `b3` (`minor.sung-degree`). ### 18.4 Pentatonic, blues and the twelve bars

Build a scale with the `scale-builder` answer (planned): `{ ui: "scale-builder", tonic: "A", input: "degrees", palette: [...] }` (or `input: "keyboard"` with a `range`, answered as `midi`), `correct: { degrees: ["1", "b3", "4", "5", "b7"] }`, graded as a set (`scale-builder.correct`: home and one more, each once; `scale-builder.palette`: every answer on the pad). Colours by ear: `SCALE_COLOUR_OPTIONS`.

Chart a twelve-bar blues with the `bar-grid` answer (planned): `{ ui: "bar-grid", bars: 12, perRow: 4, entry: "rn", qualities: ["7"], given: [1] }`, `correct: { rn: ["I7", "I7", "I7", "I7", "IV7", "IV7", "I7", "I7", "V7", "IV7", "I7", "V7"] }` (or `entry: "symbol"` with `correct.text` in code spelling: `C7`). One entry per bar (`bar-grid.correct`); graded bar by bar by degree and quality, never as strings.

## 19. The Wave 6 shapes (sevenths, rhythm II, modes, chromatic harmony, key changes, the finale)

Wave 6 added these to `@oak/content-kit` (`CONTENT_SCHEMA_VERSION` 5). There is no new exercise kind, answer widget or matcher: the wave is new fields on kinds you know, one new rule (`reharmonize`), and rules that have had an analyzer since Wave 1 and now meet authored content. As in §17 and §18, a shape marked (planned) sits in `PLANNED`: a unit spec may use it, and a lesson that does fails check 2 until the Build phase moves it into `IMPLEMENTED`. `bun run content:check <id>` names them. `docs/design/wave6.md` §11 says who renders and who grades each one.

### 19.1 Seventh chords by name (the worked example: U12.L01)

A seventh chord in code is its symbol inside `chord()` (STYLE §5.1): `C^7`, `C7`, `Cm7`, `Cm7b5`, `Co7`. Prose uses the lead-sheet form (Cmaj7, Cm7♭5, C°7); show both once. Each family unlocks with its lesson (`chord.seventh` U12.L01, `chord.seventh-dim` U12.L03), so `Cm7b5` in a U12.L01 answer is `code.locked-chord` and `Cmaj7` is `code.unknown-chord`: pin both where a learner would write them. The default dictionary does not always put the root lowest (`Dm7` voices F3 C4 D4 F4 C5), so every loop carries a `bass:` line on `.rootNotes(2)` and no prose points at a voice of the voicing.

Graded today, as U12.L01 does it:

```ts
// S5 modify: a named chord is its pitch classes and its lowest note at each onset
code: { starter: loop("<⟪C Am Dm⟫ G7>") },
reference: { solutions: [loop("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>")], bars: 4 },
matcher: { m: "pcset", perOnset: true, bass: true, tracks: ["chords"] },   // C7 for C^7: harmony.pcset
// S4 keyboard: build Fmaj7
expect: { pcs: [5, 9, 0, 4], bassPc: 5 }, pitchMode: "octave-free",
// S6 ear: which of the lesson's qualities (a fixed item until `fam.chd.quality-7th` opens in U12.L02)
answer: { ui: "quality-pad", set: ["maj7", "m7"] }, correct: { qualities: ["maj7"] },   // id.quality
// S7 Make: one required rule, read at seventh depth
{ rule: "chord-qualities", track: "chords", contains: ["maj7", "m7"], depth: "seventh", required: true, … }
```

`SEVENTH_QUALITIES` lists the five qualities in teaching order (offer a quality only from its lesson), `SEVENTH_DEGREES` each one as degrees above its root, `DIATONIC_SEVENTHS` the quality on each degree of a major and of a natural-minor key. Numerals with sevenths are an `rn-palette` with `allow: ["sevenths"]` (`Imaj7 vi7 ii7 V7`, `viiø7`, `vii°7`), graded by degree and quality at seventh depth: `V` for `V7` and `I7` for `Imaj7` are `harmony.rn-quality`. A twelve-bar or four-bar chart of sevenths is a `bar-grid` with `qualities` from `SEVENTH_QUALITIES`. Left-hand voicings are `.dict("lefthand")` before `.voicing()` (U12.L06; sevenths and sixths only, STYLE §5.2), graded by `pcset` against a reference in the same dictionary, with `must-use tb.dict.lefthand`.

### 19.2 Guide tones

"The 3rd and 7th as a thread" (U12.L05; the jazz track builds on it). A guide-tone line the learner writes note by note is an `events` match on its track. A tune that lands on guide tones is the `guide-tones` rule (planned): `{ rule: "guide-tones", track: "mel", harmony: "chords", minRatio: 0.75, at: "change", maxMoveSemis: 2 }`: the share of chord changes at which the line sounds the new chord's 3rd or 7th; `at` says which notes are judged (`change`, every `strong` beat, or `all`), `maxMoveSemis` how far the line may move between them. Singing the thread is a `sing` step with `mode: "guide-tones"` (planned, feature `sing.guide-tones`): the target is a reference track or degrees (`sing.guide-tones-target`), and like every sung step it declares `fallback` (Quiet mode picks the notes) and never gates.

### 19.3 Rhythm II: pushes, ghost notes, ties and dots, compound meter, triplets

- **Pushes** are `.early(1/8)` and `.late(1/16)` with a number, never a fraction inside a string (§9; contracts §0.16). They move onsets, so a `rhythm` or `events` matcher sees them; `nudge` never unlocks. "On the beat or pushed" by ear is an `identify` with `PUSH_OPTIONS`; a Make that asks for off-beat accents uses `syncopation` (`track`, `min`).
- **Ghost notes** are `.velocity("…")` with one value per step (a control pattern takes its rhythm from the left: evaluate and read the events). A graded step and a Make both use `velocity-ghosts` (planned; in a modify step inside `{ m: "rubric", rules: […] }`), since the `controls` matcher does not read `velocity` yet (ask the grader engineer if a step needs "match these dynamics" value by value): `{ rule: "velocity-ghosts", track: "snare", min: 4, on: "16th-offbeat", accents: 2 }`: at least `min` hits at an effective gain (`gain × velocity`) of `maxGain` (0.4) or less, and with `accents` that many hits at full level, so a line that is quiet throughout does not pass.
- **Ties, dots and triplets on the staff**: the staff input's `palette.durations` are note-list tokens (`staff.duration-token`): `4` `8` `16`, `4.` a dotted quarter, `8t` a triplet eighth; `ties: true` lets a note be tied to the next one of the same pitch, across the bar line too. Tests answer in the same DSL: `{ staff: "C4:4. D4:8 E4:4~ E4:8 F4:8" }`, `"(3:2 C4:8 D4:8 E4:8)"` or `C4:8t D4:8t E4:8t`. Each is a planned feature (`staff-input.dots`, `.triplets`, `.ties`). Before U13.L03 nothing needs a tie or a dot (contracts §2.8).
- **Compound meter**: the lesson (or one exercise) declares `meter: "6/8"` (or `9/8`, `12/8`); BPM counts the dotted quarter and the tempo line is `setcpm(bpm/2)` in 6/8 (contracts §6.9; `tb.meter.compound`, U13.L04). A lesson or exercise in a compound meter is the planned feature `meter.compound` until every surface draws it. A step grid is `stepsPerBar: 6` for the six eighths of a 6/8 bar and `12` for 12/8 or for eighth-note triplets in 4/4; `24` is sixteenth-note triplets (6 and 24 are the planned feature `grid.steps`). Swing is not used in compound meters (STYLE §7). "Simple or compound" is `METER_FEEL_OPTIONS`; "two or three to the beat" inside 4/4 is `BEAT_DIVISION_OPTIONS`.
- **Triplets in code** are a bracket of three (`[hh hh hh]`) or `.ply(3)` on a beat (U13.L05); a rule that asks for them is `onsets` with `contains: [[1, 12], [1, 6]]`.

### 19.4 Modes

A mode is a `KeySpec` like any key: `{ tonic: "G", mode: "mixolydian" }`, and `.scale("G4:mixolydian")` in code; each scale name unlocks with its lesson (U14.L01, L03, L04, L05). `MODE_DEGREES` gives a mode's degrees against major (Dorian 1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 ♭7), `MODE_CHARACTERISTIC` its parent (major or minor), its characteristic tone and the parent's degree it replaces. "Which mode" is an `identify` with `MODE_OPTIONS` (offer each from its lesson). "Raise or lower one note to get the mode" is the scale builder opened on a scale (planned, feature `scale-builder.from`):

```ts
answer: { ui: "scale-builder", tonic: "G", input: "degrees", from: "major",
  palette: ["1", "2", "3", "4", "#4", "5", "6", "b7", "7"] },
correct: { degrees: ["1", "2", "3", "4", "5", "6", "b7"] },      // untick 7, tick ♭7
```

The palette holds every degree of the starting scale and of the answer, and the answer differs from the start (`scale-builder.from`). A modal piece is checked with the `mode` rule (planned): the characteristic tone on a strong beat at least twice (`requireCharacteristic`), none of the degree it replaces, the tonic chord in at least 40% of the bars. `anyOf` lets the learner choose and `tonic: "declared"` reads the tonic from the piece's own `.scale()` (feature `mode.any-of`): C6 is `{ rule: "mode", tonic: "declared", mode: "mixolydian", anyOf: ["mixolydian", "dorian"], requireCharacteristic: true }`.

### 19.5 Chromatic degrees

From U15.L01 a degree may come from outside the key: ♯4 ♭7 ♯5 ♯1 ♭2 ♭3 ♭6 (`CHROMATIC_DEGREES`), sung fi te si di ra me le (`DEGREE_SYLLABLES`), written in code as step strings, `n("3#")` for ♯4 and `n("6b")` for ♭7 (`CHROMATIC_STEPS`; the number is the step, not the degree: STYLE §6.3). A degree pad lists them in its `set` with `syllables: true`; a sung target writes them in `degrees` and declares `fallback: "identify"`, which is the Quiet path. **Before U15.L01 a degree pad and a sung target stay inside the key's own degrees** (check 14, `chromatic.before-u15`): a mode's own degrees are not chromatic in that mode (♭7 in G Mixolydian, ♭3 in C blues), and the three minors count as one key.

### 19.6 Chords from outside the key: applied and borrowed

- **Name it**: an `rn-palette` with `allow: ["triads", "sevenths", "secondary"]` (V/V, V7/ii, vii°/V) or `"borrowed"` (iv, ♭III, ♭VI, ♭VII, the Picardy I). `labels` names the slots, so one step can ask for the chord and where it goes: `{ slots: 2, labels: ["The chord", "It leads to"] }`, `correct: { rn: ["V7/V", "V"] }`. Numerals are read by degree, chroma and quality, never as strings (`♭VI` and `bVI` are one answer; `II7` for `V7/V` is `harmony.rn-degree`).
- **Build it**: the keyboard (`expect: { pcs, bassPc }`) or code graded with `pcset`.
- **Find it**: "which bar has the chord from outside the key" is `{ ui: "bar-buttons", bars: 4 }` with `correct: { number: 2 }`; "where is this chord from" is a `choice` with `OUTSIDE_CHORD_OPTIONS` (offer each kind from its lesson).
- **In a Make or a brief** (planned features of `contains-rn`): `kind: ["secondary-dominant"]` counts any chord of that kind (`OUTSIDE_CHORD_KINDS`: `secondary-dominant`, `applied-leading-tone`, `borrowed`), with `rn: []`; `mustResolve` asks that it go to its target. `inOrder: true` asks for a run: `{ rule: "contains-rn", rn: ["bVI", "bVII", "I"], min: 1, inOrder: true }`.
- **A reharmonization** (U16.L05) is one rule, so a lesson Make can require it: `{ rule: "reharmonize", melody: "mel", reference: { solutions: [ORIGINAL], bars: 8 }, min: 2, kinds: ["borrowed", "secondary-dominant"] }` (planned): the tune's notes are the reference's, note for note, and at least `min` chords are of the asked kinds (without `kinds`: at least `min` bars carry another chord than the original's).

### 19.7 Key changes

A key change is written with a patterned `.scale()` (contracts §6.8, STYLE §9), counted in bars of the whole piece and placed after `arrange()` when the piece has sections; `.transpose()` after `.scale()` is refused. An exercise whose example or answer changes key declares its plan (planned, feature `key-plan`):

```ts
key: { tonic: "C", mode: "major" },
keyPlan: [{ bar: 1, key: { tonic: "C", mode: "major" } }, { bar: 9, key: { tonic: "D", mode: "major" } }],
```

Degrees, syllables and numerals of a bar are then read in the key in force at that bar. The plan starts at bar 1 (0 with a pickup) in the exercise's `key`, in bar order (`key-plan.first`, `key-plan.order`). "Where does it change" is `bar-buttons`; "to what" a `text` answer with `normalize: "key"`; "how" a `choice` with `KEY_CHANGE_OPTIONS`. A pivot chord is one chord named in two keys (planned, feature `rn-palette.slot-keys`): `{ ui: "rn-palette", slots: 2, allow: ["triads"], labels: ["In C major", "In G major"], slotKeys: [C, G] }`, `correct: { rn: ["vi", "ii"] }`. In a Make: a lift is `key-change` (`at`, `semitones`, `minBarsAfter`, `sameMelody`; C7), a modulation through a pivot is `modulation` with `via: "pivot"` (both planned).

### 20.2 The corrections-pass standard (every graded step, every Make)

The corrections pass found about 180 places where a rule accepted the right syntax without the musical result (docs/decisions.md decision 71). Do not write that class of defect again.

1. **Every required rule checks what is heard.** Reach for an effect rule before a bare `uses`: `silent-beat`, `split-beat`, `held`, `bars-differ`, `speed-change`, `transposed-copy`, `distinct-keys`, `orbits-differ`, `call-response`, `stack-intervals`, `chords-per-bar`, `last-chord`, `song-kept`, `tuplets-every-bar`, `swung-offbeats`, `voiced-with`, `control-set`, `parts-enter`, `broken-chord`, `black-keys`, `chance-varies`, `echo` (implemented; `packages/grader/src/rubric/corrections-rules.ts`), the track rules of §20.3, and fields such as `onsets.excludes`, `pcset.durations`, `melody.durations`, `cadence.before`. Each custom rule takes `params.alsoUses`: once the effect is heard, the code must also use one of these tools (credit 0.5 otherwise), so "written with `[ ]`" stays inside the Make's ONE required rule.
2. **Tests for every graded code step**: the reference; at least **three other right answers** (another spelling, the short form, a lighter amount inside the window, another sound); the unchanged starter (markers stripped) failing with `code.unchanged`; at least **one wrong answer that uses the right function** and fails on the effect (`.swingBy(1/3, 8)`, which leaves eighths even; `.swingBy(0, 4)`; the right places at `.gain(0)`; `.dict("lefthand")` after `.voicing()`; a `^7` where the chart says `7`); and the usual two realistic mistakes with different feedback codes (§9).
3. **What is heard**: an event at effective gain ≤ 0.01 is dropped by the grader; treat a part more than 24 dB under the loudest part as not heard, and never let a required rule pass on it.
4. **No feedback or hint below rung 4 names the answer.** A `feedbackText` override describes the sound and the place ("the two hits inside beats 2 and 4 sit an equal distance apart"), never the code that fixes it.
5. **Every sentence about music is checked by running it**: evaluate the code (`evalHeadless`, `midisOf` from `@oak/grader/testing`) or `bun run theory …`, and read the events. "Every note on a beat is in its chord" is a claim; the J1.L01 author found it false of the first draft's vibes line (E over Dm7) and changed the line.

Content CI checks 1, 2 and 4 on every graded step and Make of the tracks (`content:check` section 15, `content/validate/standard.ts`): a code step's matcher compares something heard and a Make's required rules include one that is not about the code's shape (`uses`, `tracks`, `bars`, a tempo or a length); beside the reference, three other right answers pass (a Make: four passing pieces); a failing test that is not the starter calls every function the answer adds to the starter (the right call, the wrong result); and neither rungs 1–3, nor a `feedbackText` override, nor any line the learner reads on a failing test quotes the answer (the shortest edit from the starter to a right answer, read as the whole call it sits in: `.swingBy(1/3, 4)`, not `4`). A non-code graded step pins one right and one wrong answer, and no low rung quotes the right option's label. A capstone brief is graded beside the song's melody and, for AC1, a pinned second version (`briefContext` in `content/validate/owners.ts`), as the capstone host grades it.

The worked example is `content/advanced/J1/L01`. Its S4 asks for the ride pattern; the matcher is the rhythm on the straightened grid plus the measured swing of the skip notes:

```ts
matcher: { m: "all", of: [
  { m: "rhythm", tol: [1, 16], durations: false, perRole: false, displacementCredit: 0.5, tracks: ["drums"] },
  { m: "rubric", rules: [{ id: "skips-swing", label: "The short hits swing", required: true, skills: ["R13"],
      rule: "custom", fn: "swung-offbeats", params: { track: "drums", min: 2 } }] } ] },
// passes: the reference, `.swing(4)`, the pattern as eighths with rests, the swing written on the triplet
// grid with no call, `.swingBy(.3, 4)`; fails: the unchanged starter (code.unchanged), `.swingBy(1/3, 8)` and
// `.swingBy(0, 4)` (rubric.unmet: the right function, no swing heard), the skip notes after 1 and 3
// (rhythm.displaced), `.swingBy("1/3", 4)` (code.swing-fraction-string)
```

Its S6 asks for the hi-hat on 2 and 4 with `onsets` (`contains` 2 and 4, `excludes` 1 and 3, `perBar`), so hats on every beat, on 1 and 3, or silent at `.gain(0)` all fail; its Make requires `swing` on the ride (`drums: ["ride"]`).

### 20.4 Canonical timing fixtures

Compute these one way (`@oak/content-kit`), in prose, generators and rules:

- **Swing**: `swingRatio(amount)` = (1 + a) / (1 − a): `.1` → 1.22, `.2` → 3:2, `1/3` → 2:1, `.5` → 3:1 (`SWING_FEELS`). A check's window is [1.6, 2.4]; a Make that invites a lighter swing, [1.45, 2.4].
- **Microtiming**: `.late(x)` moves by `x` bars, so it is tempo-dependent: `lateMs(1/32, 76)` is 99 ms, a displacement you hear as rhythm; a laid-back feel is 8–45 ms (`FEEL_MS`): `lateCycles(20, 90)` = 0.0075. Prose gives milliseconds and the code the decimal for the lesson's tempo.
- **Clave**: `CLAVE_GRIDS` on the two-bar 8th grid, 1 cycle = 1 bar: son 3-2 bar A {0, 3/8, 6/8}, bar B {2/8, 4/8}; rumba moves the three-side's last hit to 7/8; 2-3 swaps the bars. The direction is read from bar 1 (`claveDirectionOf`); `claveMini` writes it.
- **Polymeter**: `polymeterPeriodBars(steps, perBar)`: `{bd ~ ~}%4` meets the bar again after 3 bars (evaluated: kicks at 0, 3/4, 3/2, 9/4).
- **Odd meters**: `METER_GROUPINGS` (5/4, 5/8, 7/8, 9/8i) and `groupStarts`; `9/8i` is the irregular 9/8 (2+2+2+3), never compound 9/8.
- **Euclid**: `(k,n,r)` and `.euclidRot(k, n, r)` move every pulse r steps LATER (evaluated: `cb(3,8,2)` plays 0, 1/4, 5/8; `.euclidRot(5, 8, 1)` plays 1/8, 3/8, 1/2, 3/4, 7/8).

### 20.5 Strudel facts the tracks rest on (evaluated)

- `lefthand` is mostly rootless, not uniformly (STYLE §5.2): `C^7` → B D E G (the 7th lowest), `G7` → B E F A, `Dm7` → C E F A; `Cm7b5` keeps its root; `7#9` has three notes; `G7alt` is silent in it. `.offset(1)` moves to the other rootless position (`C^7` → E G B D). Rootless A and B, shells and drop-2 as shapes are hand-voiced and graded by `voicing-shape`.
- `.mode("duck")` leaves out the note equal to the anchor so a chord does not double the melody (anchored on G4, `C^7` in `lefthand` plays B3 D4 E4); it does not remove the root.
- `Cø` is silent: write `Cm7b5`; ø is display only. `C7alt` sounds in `ireal` and `ireal-ext` only.
- `polymeter()` and `pm()` are not substitutes for `{ }%n` (they time differently) and are uncatalogued; `euclidLegato`, `fmenv` and the FM envelope methods are uncatalogued; `.nrpn()` does not exist.
- `chop(4)` and `striate(4)` on a single event give the same slices: explain them on a multi-event pattern. `slice` plays slices; `splice` also sets the playback speed.
- The Charleston (`.struct("x ~ ~ x ~ ~ ~ ~")`) strikes twice in the first half of the bar, so the second chord of a two-chord bar (`[Gm7 C7]`) is never heard from the comping (J4, J5, JC2, JC3): comp such bars on 1, the and of 2 and the and of 3 (`"x ~ ~ x ~ x ~ ~"`) or on 1, the and of 2 and 3.
- `lefthand` under a tune that sits on chord 3rds puts the voicing's 9th a half step under the tune (Gm7's A under B♭, Dm7's E under F; evaluated): comp such a tune in `guidetones` or anchor the hand below the tune's register.
- `.swingBy(1/3, 8)` on eighths changes nothing (each slice is one eighth): eighths swing with `.swingBy(1/3, 4)`; on sixteenths `.swingBy(1/3, 4)` moves the and and the a of each beat together, and `.swingBy(x, 8)` swings the sixteenths.
- `walking-bass` reads a bar's beat 4 against the NEXT bar's downbeat and does not wrap: grade a looped walk over 8 rendered bars (`reference.bars: 8`) so its last approach is heard.
- An investigate step holds one choice question (a test answers one `choice`): ask a second fact as a number. `SrsLink` has no `level`: a family's level gates its own items.
- In a lesson in 4/4, a stimulus in another meter declares the item's `meter` and writes that meter's tempo line (`setcpm(240/7)` for 7/8, eighths a minute).

## 21. Listening, repetition and progression (the copy pass of 2026-11-19)

The owner, learning from the course, found the copy repetitive and some listening steps without a point ("it's just sounds playing: why, and what should be noticed?"). Every lesson was rewritten against these rules; keep them.

### 21.1 Every listening moment has a purpose and a payoff

Applies to `listen` and `compare` steps, the prose of `ear` steps, and any prose that asks the learner to play an `<Example>`.

1. **Frame** (prose, one or two sentences): why this sound, now; tied to what the learner can already do or hear.
2. **Focus** (the `prompt`): one concrete thing to listen for, as a question or "Listen for …". "Press Play." alone is not a focus.
3. **Captions** point the ear at the feature or the difference without giving the reveal away.
4. **Reveal**: every `listen` step has an `<OnPass>` that names what was heard and why it matters musically. The player shows it once every loop of the step has been heard through (`ListenItems`), never after the first of two loops.
5. Two neighbouring steps never ask about the same contrast at the same level. Climb the ladder **feel → count → code → name**: what it does to the music, then where it is and how many, then how it is written, then what it is called.

The warm-up's "Just listen" block and Practice's listening block say what to listen for per family (`app/src/drills/listen-for.ts`). A new perceptual family gets its line there, in the words the learner has at the family's unlock lesson.

### 21.2 No boilerplate

1. The app says the stop point (`wrapAt`: a line under the passed step, beside Wrap up) and the mic privacy line (the sing widget). Never write either in prose.
2. Each step adds one new thing: the prose, prompt, pass text and hints of neighbouring steps do not restate one fact. A key phrase appears where it is taught and where it is used.
3. Avoid course-wide formulas ("Your turn." opening every Make, "The drums are in …", "Play the target first", "Same notes, same drums … both times", "Press Play." opening every listen prompt, "Before you run it:" in every predict). Say it the way this lesson needs.

### 21.3 Progression the learner can feel

1. **Bridge in**: the first step ties today to something the learner can already do or hear, concretely, in a different form each time.
2. **Spiral in the Make**: the prose and prompt invite today's tool together with one or two earlier ones, named. The rubric does not change for it.
3. **Name the growth** once per lesson, concretely, where it is true: what the learner can now write or hear that they could not before. No generic praise.
4. The lesson's `<Recap />` card shows, from data, the tools this lesson puts in the learner's code, the earlier skills it brought back (`practises`), its terms and the size of their toolbox. Keep `practises` honest: it is shown to the learner.

### 21.4 Depth by progressive disclosure, readable prose

1. Sound, then code, then the name and its place in music. Where a step names a phenomenon, at most one sentence on why it works or where it lives (at most two per lesson), and only what is true; prefer general statements to dates and names.
2. One idea per pass text, two at most. Short sentences, one idea each; at most one colon per sentence, no chains of colons and semicolons; concrete verbs; active voice.


## 22. Clarity: the learner never guesses what is asked (the clarity pass of 2026-11-22)

The owner, learning from the course, made most of their mistakes because the objective was unclear, not because the music was hard: "This is a learning platform, I shouldn't be guessing what's expected from me." Real cases: U01.L04 S6 said only "A kick placed the same way, on an and, pushes the groove forward" above the editor (nine failed attempts, then Skip); U01.L02's Make checklist said "At least one beat where nothing plays" and the hats kept playing; the tap pad was "weird and hard to understand". Every lesson was rewritten against these rules; keep them. They win over §21 where the two pull apart: clear beats elegant.

### 22.1 The voice is instructional

1. Write like a good teacher giving a lab instruction: direct, second person, imperative. "Add a kick on the second half of beat 2." Not "A kick could go on an and." Not "What if the kick moved?"
2. Say exactly what to do, where, and how much: which line (`kick:`), which word of the pattern (the second word), which beat and bar, how many hits, notes or bars. A number beats an adjective.
3. Only words the learner has been taught. When the precise word is not taught yet, describe the place plainly ("the second half of beat 2"), never leave it vague.
4. No riddles. A question is fine as a focus for listening or prediction; it is never the only statement of a task the learner must perform.

### 22.2 Every graded or interactive step states one task, in its `prompt`

The app shows the `prompt` in a box headed **Your task**. That box is the contract with the learner.

1. The task lives in the prompt, complete. The prose above it gives context (why, what it sounds like) in at most three short sentences; prose never carries part of the task, and the prompt never depends on the prose to make sense.
2. Open with the action verb: Tap, Sing, Write, Add, Change, Remove, Pick, Listen for, Predict, Count. One task per step. Two required changes are written as two short sentences, in order.
3. End with what counts as done when it is not obvious from the action itself: "Done when the kick plays on 1 and on the second half of beat 2, and nowhere else." Keep "Done when" short and checkable. Leave it out where the action already says it ("Pick the line that plays on beat 2.").
4. Name the sound to act on and how to recognise it when it is new: "Tap on each crash, the long splashy cymbal that opens every bar."
5. Say what must stay unchanged when it matters: "Keep the snare and hats as they are."
6. Do not write controls (Space, Enter, Start, Check, Play): the app draws a **How** line under the task for every interactive kind (tap, sing, code, choice, Make). Write the music, not the buttons. Exception: a step that teaches a control.
7. Still ≤ 60 words (check 14).

### 22.3 Checklists say exactly what is checked

Make rubric `label`s (and capstone rubric labels) are shown as the learner's checklist. Each is one concrete, checkable statement, naming lines and places in the learner's words: "One whole beat where every line is silent, hats included", not "At least one beat where nothing plays". The label must match what the rule really checks; if a rule checks something the label cannot say plainly, say less, never something different. Labels are text only: never change a rule, its params or its `required` flag.

### 22.4 Hints climb from the goal to the answer

1. Rung 1 (nudge) restates the goal more concretely and points at where to look. It may end with a question, never be only one.
2. Rung 2 (pointer) names the exact place and the idea needed there.
3. Rung 3 (partial) gives the shape of the answer (as U01.L04 S6's "the same shape as `[~ hh]`"), without the literal solution.
4. Rung 4 stays `'auto'` (the reveal).

### 22.5 Feedback says what is wrong and what to do next

A `feedbackText` override names the place, the problem and the fix in the lesson's words: "Bar 1, beat 3: one kick too many on the kick line. The only new kick goes on the second half of beat 2." Override a catalog template whenever it uses an untaught word or leaves the learner without a next action. Keep every `{placeholder}` the template has.

### 22.6 Listening, predicting and investigating

1. A predict prompt says what to predict and what form the answer takes: "Predict how many hats play in one bar, then run it."
2. An investigate prompt says what to find and how to answer: "Find the line that plays on beats 2 and 4. Pick it below."
3. A listen prompt keeps §21.1: one concrete thing to listen for.

### 22.7 What the clarity pass did not change

Grading is untouched: rules, matchers, targets, tolerances, tests, IDs, code, examples and `required` flags stay as they were. Text changes bump `LessonDef.version` (contracts §11.7), never an exercise version.

## 23. Unit songs (the songs pass of 2026-11-24)

The owner, six lessons into the course, found it "very bare" and asked for "building up something bigger as we advance the lectures as opposed to the disparate concepts we are exercising in each lesson". So every unit except U00 is now **one song**. When the unit starts, Oak plays the whole song, finished, in the learner's style. Each lesson's Make takes over **cells** of it (a part in a section): "your groove now plays the second part". Oak keeps playing every cell nobody has taken, and the song is released to the album when the unit ends.

The binding rules are contracts §5.11; the design and its reasons are `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-24-unit-songs-design.md` (read §4, §5.1–§5.3, §7 and §8 before you write a song). Start from the unit's proposal in `docs/design/songs/makes-core-{a,b,c}.md` §8 (form, harmony, takeover order); you may change it. `content/unit-songs/U01.ts` is the worked example. A unit song task writes three things: `content/unit-songs/<UNIT>.ts`, the Make of every take lesson (prose, prompt, version bump), and, rarely, a re-keyed Make (§23.11).

**Grading is untouched.** The grader sees the learner's Make code only, as before. You never change a rubric rule, matcher, test or `required` flag for a song, except when you re-key a Make (§23.11). Everything below is about what is heard and shown, not about what passes.

### 23.1 What the learner's code is, and what Oak does with it

1. The Make's editor holds only the learner's code for today's cells: a loop, the same kind of code the lesson already grades. Oak's song engine places that loop into the sections of the step's `sections`, and plays everything else.
2. A loop restarts at the start of every section it plays in (`arrange()` semantics, contracts §6.3 item 7). A four-bar loop with a fill in its last bar, placed in an 8-bar section, plays the fill in bars 4 and 8; placed in a one-bar stop, only its first bar sounds. **Give each section a length that is a whole multiple of the loops placed in it.**
3. The rubric reads the loop, not the song. "The last bar differs" means the loop's last bar. Write the prompt and the checklist in the loop's terms ("the fourth bar of your loop"), and let the song card show where it plays.
4. From U09.L02 a learner's code may contain `arrange()`. Its sections map onto the song's by type: its verse fills every song section of type `verse`, `chorus2` the second chorus when there is one. The song's form stays the one you wrote (decision 90); the Make's **Today's code** switch plays the learner's form as written.
5. The song plays at the newest take's tempo, so the learner owns the tempo from U01.L01. Keep each starter's first line a plain `setcpm(<n>/<beats>)`: the app sets that number to the song's tempo when the Make opens, when the Make accepts that tempo.

### 23.2 Oak's song: the code shape

`code` holds Oak's whole song once per flavour. The schema refuses any other shape.

- **No tempo line.** The song plays at the learner's tempo, else `bpm`.
- **One labelled track per part**, its label the part's `label` (STYLE §2, lowercase, never `$:`), each label once.
- **Section consts named `<label><Section>`** after the section's *type* (`kickVerse`, `melChorus`; a digit for a variant, `chordsChorus2`), and **one `arrange()` per track** listing every section of the song, in order, with its bars. A part that rests in a section uses `silence`.
- **A section key is a key plan after `arrange()`**, counted in bars of the whole song (STYLE §9, contracts §6.8 item 4); section consts stay bare degree patterns. A `chord("…")` track needs a lifted const with the new symbols instead.
- Profile `band`: it must pass the strict lint and may use locked tools (a preview context).

```js
const kickIntro = s("bd ~ ~ ~")
const kickVerse = s("bd ~ bd ~")
const kickChorus = s("bd ~ [~ bd] ~")
const melVerse = n("<[2 4 2 0] [3 5 7 5]>")
const melChorus = n("<[7 ~ 4 2] [6 ~ 4 ~] [5 ~ 2 0] [5 ~ 3 ~]>")

kick: arrange([4, kickIntro], [8, kickVerse], [8, kickChorus], [8, kickChorus]).gain(.75)
mel: arrange([4, silence], [8, melVerse], [8, melChorus], [8, melChorus]).scale("C4:major").s("piano").gain(.5)
```

Do not write five flavours by hand. Write one builder that takes the kit (sounds, production numbers, comp and bass grooves) and emits the code, as `content/unit-songs/U01.ts` does (and CF-A's `single()` in `content/capstones.ts`): the notes then cannot drift between flavours.

### 23.3 Sections and parts

**Sections**, in form order:

1. `id` is a slug, unique in the song (`intro`, `verse1`, `chorus2`). Takes are stored by it: once the song has shipped, never rename or reuse a section id. A removed id falls back to Oak, and the take history keeps it.
2. `type` is the section kind, one of the STYLE §10 words (`intro verse pre chorus bridge solo break head vamp lift outro ballad drive a b c`) or `prechorus`. It names the section once the learner has its term, and it is what a learner's `arrange()` maps onto.
3. `bars` is a whole multiple of 4, or 1 for a one-bar stop (type `break`) or a pickup bar. The song is at most **64 bars** in all (the Workbench analysis limit).
4. `key` only where the section leaves the song's key: a key change, a modal section, a minor bridge.

**Parts:**

1. `id` is a slug, unique and stable like a section id. `label` is a STYLE §2 label in a role it allows (`kick snare hats perc drums` are drums, `bass` bass, `chords` chords, `pad` pad or chords, `mel vox` melody, `keys gtr horns` chords, melody or pad). `fx` is never a part.
2. Give the song a part for every label the unit's references use, so the grid shows what the learner wrote. A learner track is matched to the part with its exact label first, then to the first part of its role; a track that matches no part plays wherever its take owns any cell of the section.
3. **Drum parts list their `sounds`** (required, drum parts only): the sound names that belong to the part, read before any `:` index (`bd:3` is `bd`). A sound belongs to one part only. List every sound Oak's flavours use and every sound the unit's starters, references and hints use, plus the obvious neighbours: snare `sd rim cp`, hats `hh oh`, cymbals and toms `cr rd lt mt ht`. A learner's `drums:` line is then split hit by hit: its kick goes to the kick part, its snare to the snare part. Sound pack v4's names need no listing (contracts §5.11 rule 9): a name no part lists is read as what it stands for, so a `conga`, `bongo`, `clave` or foley hit (`foley snap impact swoosh scratch`) goes to the part that owns the percussion (`cb tb sh`, else the part labelled `perc`), `sbd` to the part that owns `bd`, and a break (`brkfunk brkboombap brkjungle brkhouse brkjazz brklofi`) to every drum part, because one sample plays the whole kit. List a v4 name in a part only to send it somewhere else (a clave on the snare part).
4. `name` is the musical name ("Melody"), `plainName` the name before its term ("Tune"), `termId` the term that switches between them. Without a `termId` the `name` shows from the first lesson, so it must be plain English (Kick, Snare, Bass).

### 23.4 The five flavours: what may differ

Every song exists in all five flavours (pop, indie, jazz-soul, funk, electronic). The learner picks one when the song opens and may change it later; their takes stay.

| The same in every flavour | May differ by flavour |
|---|---|
| sections, their order and bars | sounds and banks (the kit's, contracts §5.10) |
| parts, and the sections each part plays in | the drum groove and the comp rhythm inside a section |
| chord roots and functions | swing, as the kit's staging allows |
| the bass roots on the downbeats | chord colour: sixths, sevenths, ninths as the kit's staging allows (jazz-soul from U03, funk from U06, electronic from U06.L10) |
| every pitch of the lead tune | production: room, filter, pan, gain inside §23.5; synth controls (electronic's acid filter, pad swell, pump) |

A song stays **straight** wherever the unit's takes are straight-subdivision parts the learner writes: all of U01–U02 and U13. A kit without a sound the others use (jazz-soul has no crash) uses its own, and that sound must be in the drum part's `sounds` (U01 puts `rd` in `perc`).

### 23.5 Loudness and clashes

1. **The learner's part sits on top, and the song loudness harness sets the levels.** Oak's song levels are not copied from the Bands: the v4 kits are peak-normalised, so a drum at a Band's gain sits far over a tune, and a song carries a tune the Bands leave out. Measured, U01–U17 land at −18 to −22.5 LUFS, under the pop, funk and electronic Bands' medians (−15.5 to −16.5). The levels are set and guarded by the harness `packages/strudel/e2e/songs.pw.ts`, against the budget it records in `packages/strudel/sound-songs.json`. It renders Oak's whole song in every flavour offline (before the limiter), and every take step with its reference as the live Make, and holds each song and flavour to: integrated loudness between the flavour's floor and ceiling (`targets`: 2 LU under the quietest Band, up to the Band median + 2 LU and never over the loudest Band), peak under −1 dBFS, the five flavours within 3 LU of each other, loud sections (chorus, lift, drive) at least 1 LU over quiet ones, a low-end share near the flavour's Bands, and **every take's learner part no more than 3 LU under Oak's** in the cells it takes, in every flavour. A later run more than 1 LU (loudness, take differences) or 1.5 dB (peaks) from the recorded numbers fails. Oak's lead sits under a learner's line at default gain. A part the learner takes over must never be the quietest thing in its section: a part more than 24 dB under the loudest is treated as not heard (§20.2 rule 3). Listen to every flavour before you hand over (§15.3).

   Run one song (a shift in 5600–5699 keeps its ports clear of the app's; 500 is port 5692):

   ```
   OAK_E2E_SHIFT=500 bunx playwright test -c packages/strudel/e2e/playwright.config.ts packages/strudel/e2e/songs.pw.ts --project autoplay -g "usong.U03"
   ```

   A song with no entry in the budget is recorded on its first run and held to the rule from then on. After you change levels on purpose, re-record with `SONGS_RECORD=1`; `SONGS_CALIBRATE=1` re-measures the Bands, which moves the targets. `SONGS_PARTS=1 SONGS_ONLY=U10 SONGS_PARTS_OUT=<file>` writes each Oak part alone per section, which is what a gain moves. Two mix conventions the harness found, to copy into every song: an `oak808` (or `RolandTR808`) kick track ends in `.decay(.35).sustain(0)`, because the 808 `bd` rings for seconds and overlapping hits pile up (+5 dBFS peaks at 92 BPM without it); and the electronic sine sub gets `.postgain(.5)` (its swell pad `.postgain(.6)`), or the quiet sections read louder than the loud ones.
2. **No held clash.** Oak's lead tune and bass never hold a note, a beat or more, a half step from a chord tone sounding with it (the §13 clash rule). The scan runs per flavour, so a flavour's added seventh or ninth must clear it too: no major 7th under a tune that lands on 1 or 4, the rule `band.axis-4bar` already follows (§8).
3. Every sentence about the song's music is checked by running it (§20.2 rule 5): evaluate the code, or `bun run theory …`, and read the events.

### 23.6 The plan: one step per lesson

`plan` has one entry per lesson of the unit, in syllabus order (the unit's array order: U05.L09 comes after U05.L04), each lesson exactly once. Checkpoint Makes are never in the plan; they stay standalone. Each entry is a **take** or a **side** step.

A take step says where its code plays (`sections`, or `'all'`), which parts it takes (`parts`), how the Make opens (`startFrom`, §23.9) and one line for Today (`today`, §23.10).

**U01–U04: plan by part.** The learner writes one part at a time (drums in U01, the lead in U02–U03, a second voice in U04), so a step takes named parts in named sections: U01.L01 takes `kick` and `snare` in the verse; U01.L03, one line per drum, takes `kick`, `snare` and `hats` there.

**From U05: plan by section.** From U05 most starters already hold chords, a bass and a tune, because the required rule reads them. Those given lines play in the song **as the learner's**, in plain default sounds (decision 88): the claim rules (§23.7) take every cell they play. So hand the learner whole sections ("the chorus is yours"): list in `parts` every part the starter and the reference play, and expect Oak's drums to be the only Oak part left in those sections. Do not plan a U05-on step as "the bass only" when its starter also plays chords: the chords take the section anyway.

`releaseAfter` is the unit's checkpoint when it has one, else its last lesson. Capstones stay separate singles and are never a song's `releaseAfter`. It must come after the last take step.

### 23.7 What a take claims (and what that means for you)

In each section it plays in, a take claims:

| A take claims | So, as the author |
|---|---|
| the parts in its `parts` | list what the step hands over |
| every part its code actually plays, read from the saved code: drum hits by `sounds`, pitched tracks by label, then role | `parts` must include every part the passing reference plays (CI); a learner who goes past the brief (a snare in a kick lesson) takes that part too, in those sections only |
| when its code plays chords, every Oak pitched part (bass, chords, melody, pad) in those sections, except those in `oakKeeps` | a chord take silences Oak's bass and tune there; Oak's drums keep playing |

Later takes override earlier ones cell by cell. An earlier `drums:` line keeps only the drums it still owns: when a later step takes the kick, the earlier line's kick hits stop and its snare plays on. A take claims only the sections it filled; a section it leaves empty keeps its owner. A skipped lesson (an optional or split lesson the learner never played) leaves its cells to Oak, so the song is whole whatever the route.

### 23.8 `oakKeeps`

A take that plays chords silences Oak's pitched parts in its sections (decision 89). When one of them fits the learner's chords, because the take keeps Oak's roots (U05.L01's verse chords on the Band's roots), name it in `oakKeeps` and Oak keeps playing it there. Only pitched parts, never a part the step takes. CI clash-scans the reference against every part you keep: keep only what you have checked by running it.

### 23.9 `startFrom`, and the "already passes" rule

1. `startFrom: 'oak'` opens the Make with the lesson's authored starter, as before.
2. `startFrom: '<lesson>'` opens it with the learner's saved Make code of that earlier take step **of this song**, with "Start from Oak's copy" beside it. With no take of that lesson, the Make opens with the starter. Name only a lesson completed before this one in every legal order: never a side step, an optional lesson or a lesson that may be skipped.
3. **The "already passes" rule.** The earlier step's passing reference, opened here, must lint and evaluate in this lesson's unlock context **and must not already meet today's required rule**. Otherwise a learner who changes nothing would pass with yesterday's work. `make.unchanged` compares with the code the Make opened with, so an unchanged earlier loop fails, but a learner who only renames a line would still pass; CI therefore refuses the step.
4. The starter stays a complete starter and every test still grades against it: `startFrom` changes the opening code, not the exercise.
5. A take step's opening code comes from `startFrom`, not from `fromSketch`. U06.L10, U10.L06, U11.L07 and U15.L06 carry a `fromSketch`; when the lesson it names is a take step of the same song, use it as `startFrom`; when it names another unit's lesson (U06.L10 from U05.L01, U15.L06 from U12.L04), start from `'oak'` and rewrite the prose that promises "your loop from …", or make the step a side step. Say which in your report.

### 23.10 `today` lines

The `today` line is shown on Today above the setlist and on the lesson card above step 1: **before the lesson starts**. So:

1. At most 20 words, one sentence, instructional and concrete: what the learner's code will do in the song, today. "Your drum line plays the kick and snare in the second part of the song."
2. **Only words the learner already has**: terms of earlier lessons, never this lesson's new terms (they come later, in its pass texts) and never a later lesson's (check 14's rules, §7). Section names are terms (verse and chorus at U09.L02, bridge at U09.L03): until then name a section by its place ("the opening", "the second part", "the last loud part", "the ending"), never "verse" or "chorus". Name a part by its `plainName` until its term is introduced.
3. The §5 voice: no exclamation marks, no "let's", "simply", "easy", "awesome", sentence case. Not the prompt again: the prompt is the task, the today line is where it lands in the song.

### 23.11 Side steps, keys and meters, re-keying

**Side steps.** A Make that cannot live in the song is `{ kind: 'side', reason }`. It stays today's standalone sketch over its own Band, and the lesson card shows `reason`: one plain line in the learner's words ("This lesson counts three to a bar and the song counts four, so this beat stays a sketch of its own."). Known now: U01.L08 and U07.L09 (3/4), U13.L04 (6/8), most of R2 (odd meters), and U10.L09 and U11.L01 (their unit's proposal says why). A song has one meter and one tempo line, so another meter is always a side step.

**Keys.** A take step's Make must be in the key of every section it plays in. Fit the song to the lessons first: a section that carries the lesson's key (`sections[].key`: U03.L05–L07, U10, U14, U17). Oak never follows the learner's key in a song (decision 91); a learner who changes key past the brief changes only their own cells.

**Re-keying a Make** (spec §7.3) is the last resort, for a Make whose point survives in the song's key when a side step would lose a song step. It is a grading change:

1. Bump the exercise's `version` (contracts §11.7) and the lesson's `version`. Old passes are honoured.
2. Move everything: the exercise `key`, the starter, the reference, **every test**, hints, `feedbackText`, the flavour swaps, and every sentence of prose that names a note or a key. Check 13 (theory) must pass.
3. Keep the corrections-pass standard (§20.2): the reference, three other right answers, the unchanged starter failing, a wrong answer that uses the right function, two realistic mistakes.
4. Check every claim about the new key by running it.

### 23.12 Rewriting a take lesson's Make

1. The task is about the song, and §22 still decides how it is said: one task in **Your task**, exact, the place and the amount, only taught words. "Put a kick on the second half of beat 2 in your song's second part." The prose may say where the loop will play; it never explains the song engine.
2. Name sections as §23.10 does, by place until their term. The song card and the grid are drawn by the app from data: never write them in the MDX.
3. Checklist labels stay true to what the rule checks (§22.3), which is the loop, never the song ("in the chorus" is false of a rule that reads the loop).
4. Bump `LessonDef.version` for every prose or prompt change. The exercise `version` moves only when grading does (§23.11).

### 23.13 The checks, and how to run them without the suites

During the songs work the full suites are **not** run (the owner, 2026-11-24: they take too long). Never run `bun run test`, `bun run check`, `bun run test:content`, `bun run e2e` or `bun test` without file paths; the full suites run once, at the end of the work. This replaces §12's "run `test:content` before you hand over" for song tasks. Run only these:

| Command | When |
|---|---|
| `bun run content:check <lessonId>` | every lesson of the unit, after every change to it or to the song. For a lesson in a song it also runs the unit song checks: the song's, and the plan step's |
| `bun test ./content/validate/unit-songs.test.ts ./content/unit-songs/index.test.ts` | after writing or changing a song file: every song, every plan and every take step |
| `bun run content:build` | after adding a song file (its `usong.<UNIT>` id joins `content/ids.lock.json`) |
| `bun run theory …` | to check a claim about harmony or a clash before you write it |
| `OAK_E2E_SHIFT=500 bunx playwright test -c packages/strudel/e2e/playwright.config.ts packages/strudel/e2e/songs.pw.ts --project autoplay -g "usong.<UNIT>"` | after writing a song or changing its levels: the loudness harness for that song alone (§23.5 rule 1); `SONGS_RECORD=1` to re-record it |

What the checks hold a song and a plan to (contracts §5.11, spec §8), with the issue each failure names:

| Check | Issue | Usual fix |
|---|---|---|
| every flavour evaluates, is not silent, lints in profile `band`, has events for each part in every section it plays | `usong.silent` | a const left as `silence`, a sound missing from the kit |
| sections, parts, chord roots, bass downbeat roots and lead pitches agree across flavours | `usong.flavour-notes` | build the flavours from one builder (§23.2) |
| lead and bass hold no note a half step from a sounding chord tone, per flavour; with `oakKeeps`, the reference against the kept parts | `usong.clash` | move the note to a chord tone, or drop the part from `oakKeeps` |
| take Makes in the song's meter and in the key of every section they play in | `usong.key` | a section key, a side step, or a re-key (§23.11) |
| `parts` include every part the passing reference plays | `usong.parts` | add the part, or check the drum `sounds` |
| `startFrom` names a lesson completed before this one in every legal order: never an optional, side or passable lesson (the app opens the next track lesson without it) | `usong.start-from` | start from `'oak'` (§23.9 rule 2) |
| the `startFrom` reference lints and evaluates here and does not already meet today's required rule | `usong.start-passes` | start from `'oak'`, or from an earlier step |
| a reference with its own `arrange()` names the kind of at least one of the step's sections (filling some is fine; filling none enters no cell) | `usong.form` | put the step on sections of the kinds the reference arranges, or change the reference |
| `today` passes the lesson's voice and terms checks; a term counts as met only when its lesson comes before this one in every legal order and is not optional, side or passable | `usong.terms` | §23.10 |

The schema (`defineUnitSong`) refuses the rest at import, each with its path: a missing flavour, duplicate ids or labels, an unknown section or part in a step or in `oakKeeps`, sounds on a pitched part or none on a drum part, a section that is not a multiple of 4 bars, over 64 bars, a tempo line in Oak's code, a track that is not arranged in the song's sections, a lesson in the plan twice or from another unit, a `startFrom` that is not an earlier take step, a `today` line over 20 words, `releaseAfter` before the last take step.

A song is done when `content:check` passes every lesson of the unit, the two test files above are green for it, and a music review agent has re-derived its harmony and clash claims with `bun run theory …` and found nothing.
