# U14 Modes: unit spec

<!-- GENERATED from content/specs/U14.ts by `bun scripts/content/spec.ts`. Edit the .ts, never this file; content/validate/specs.test.ts fails when they differ. -->

Six lessons, a checkpoint and a capstone on four colours the learner has heard all their life and never had a name for. A mode is taught here one way only: take a scale the learner already knows, change ONE note, and listen. G major with its 7th lowered is G Mixolydian, the sound of a rock riff (U14.L01, the exemplar); that lowered 7th brings a chord of its own, ♭VII, and the loop I–♭VII–IV, with Old Joe Clark as the old tune that sings it (U14.L02). D minor with its 6th raised is D Dorian, the two-chord groove i–IV of funk and soul and the tune of Drunken Sailor (U14.L03). F major with its 4th raised is F Lydian, the floating I–II of film music and dream pop (U14.L04). E minor with its 2nd lowered is E Phrygian, the i–♭II of flamenco and heavy riffs (U14.L05). The lab puts all six colours on one home note, brightest to darkest, each one note from the next, trains the ear to name them, and ends in a vamp of the learner's own (U14.L06). The learner never "starts the major scale on another note": that the four modes of this unit can all be played on the white keys is noticed once, in the lab, after the colours are in the ear. In the default order U14.L01 is session 145, before U13.CP (147); U14.L02–L04 are 148–150, GYM.15, then U14.L05–L06 (152–153), U15.L01 (154) and U14.CP (155), which opens C6 (sessions 157–158, after CF-T.S1) and the chord-scale lesson J2.L04 of the jazz track.

## Conventions for this unit

- The one rule of the unit: a mode is a scale the learner knows with one note changed, and the sound comes first. Every lesson opens with the same music twice, on the known scale and then on the mode, so the change is heard before anything is said. No lesson, hint or feedback text derives a mode by "starting the major scale on another note", and none says "the second mode of C major". The fact that G Mixolydian, D Dorian, F Lydian and E Phrygian use the notes of C major is an observation of U14.L06 S4 ("the same seven notes, another home"), made after four lessons of colour and never used as a recipe.
- The four colours (content-kit `MODE_CHARACTERISTIC`, `MODE_DEGREES`; `bun run theory scale G_mixolydian`): Mixolydian is major with ♭7 (te; `n` 6), its chord ♭VII, its loop I–♭VII–IV, taught in G (F♯ becomes F). Dorian is natural minor with its 6th raised, written 6 or ♮6 (la; `n` 5), its chord a major IV, its loop i–IV, taught in D (B♭ becomes B). Lydian is major with ♯4 (`n` 3), its chord a major II, its loop I–II over a held home bass, taught in F (B♭ becomes B). Phrygian is natural minor with ♭2 (`n` 1), its chord ♭II, its loop i–♭II, taught in E (F♯ becomes F). Each parent key has exactly one accidental and the mode cancels it, so the learner never fights a key signature while listening for one note. Ear steps, the checkpoint and C6 use other home notes, so a colour is never learned as one key.
- In code the mode is one word: `.scale("G4:mixolydian")`, `"D4:dorian"`, `"F4:lydian"`, `"E4:phrygian"` (STYLE §3). The numbers do not change: `n` 6 is degree 7 on both scales, and the scale word decides which 7 that is. Each name unlocks with its lesson (`scale.mixolydian` U14.L01, `scale.dorian` U14.L03, `scale.lydian` U14.L04, `scale.phrygian` U14.L05); an earlier use is `code.locked-scale`, a misspelling (`myxolydian`) is `code.uncatalogued` and plays nothing: U14.L01 S5 pins both. Chromatic step strings (`n("6b")`) are `code.locked-tool` until U15.L01: in this unit a learner who wants ♭7 changes the scale word. Locrian is not taught in the core (`scale.locrian` unlocks at J2.L04).
- Keys and content check 13: the lesson's `key` is the scale the lesson STARTS from, so the starter of a "change the scale word" step may sit on it (U14.L01 G major, U14.L03 D minor, U14.L04 F major, U14.L05 E minor); every step that is on the mode declares it on its exercise (`key: { tonic: "G", mode: "mixolydian" }`), which is also the key its degrees, numerals and rules are read in. U14.L02 (G Mixolydian) and U14.L06 (D Dorian) start in the mode. A before-and-after example is ONE code string with a patterned scale, `.scale("<G3:major!2 G3:mixolydian!2>")`: listen items carry no key of their own, and check 13 reads only a literal `.scale("…")`.
- Numerals in a mode (`bun run theory diatonic G_mixolydian`; graded by degree, chroma and quality, never by string). The major-side modes are written against major, accidentals shown: G Mixolydian I ii iii° IV v vi ♭VII; F Lydian I II iii ♯iv° V vi vii. The minor-side modes are written as U10 writes minor (against the minor scale): D Dorian i ii III IV v vi° VII, where the new thing is the upper-case IV; E Phrygian i ♭II III iv v° VI vii. Probed with the real grader: `VII` for `♭VII` in Mixolydian and `II` for `♭II` in Phrygian are `harmony.rn-degree`; `iv` for `IV` in Dorian and `ii` for `II` in Lydian are `harmony.rn-case`; `♭VII` and `bVII` are one answer. The chord band prints the same numerals (a major chord on degree 2 of a Lydian key reads II, never V/V).
- Grading, all of it run in modal keys for this spec. Implemented today and used by every lesson Make but the lab's: `degree-set` with `contains` (the colour note is in the line: "b7", "6", "#4", "b2", read in the exercise's key) and `contains-rn` (the colour chord is in the loop: "bVII", "IV", "II", "bII"; its detail is helpful: "iv (Gm7) is minor: IV is G major, with B"). A scale word is graded by its notes (`events`, absolute pitch) and a named chord by `pcset` per onset with the bass. PLANNED: the `mode` rule and its `anyOf` / `tonic: "declared"` fields (feature `mode.any-of`), which U14.L06's Make and C6 need; U14.L06's author says so. Three things the grader does today that authors must write around: `degree-set` `minCount` counts over the whole graded window, so a loop that plays the note once a pass already meets "twice" (no Make of this unit says "twice"); `progression` with `schema: "schema.mixolydian"` and `allowRotation` refuses F C G G, because the two I bars merge (no Make requires it); the `mode` rule counts chord notes as colour notes and wants a chord track (a riff over a bass pedal has "no G chord").
- The sound of each colour is the sound of its records, and the hook of every lesson is a loop in that sound: Mixolydian on the `crunch` guitar with `organ` chords and a pumping `ebass` (rock); Dorian on the `rhodes` with a `fingerbass` (funk, soul) and as a sea shanty; Lydian on a `pad` over one held bass note with a delayed piano (film, dream pop); Phrygian on the `acgtr` in a three-three-two strum (flamenco), with `crunch` and `.distort()` offered for the heavy version. Makes take the drums of `band.axis-4bar` (`tracks: ["drums"]`) and keep every pitched part in the learner's code, where the rules read it (AUTHORING §8 rule 5).
- Singing (contracts §8.3): te and la are syllables the learner has sung since U10 (do-based minor), so Mixolydian and Dorian are sung: U14.L03 sings sol la te do, the climb of Drunken Sailor's sixth bar, and U14.L06 sings do, the home note of two loops. The syllables of ♯4 and ♭2 (fi, ra) are chromatic syllables and arrive with U15.L01, so U14.L04 and U14.L05 have no sung step; their degree pads and labels show ♯4 and ♭2 as degrees of the mode, which check 14 allows (`chromatic.before-u15`: a mode's own degrees are not chromatic in that mode). Every sung step declares `fallback: "identify"` and never gates.
- Words that wait: "secondary dominant" and "five of five" (U15.L02: Lydian's II is "the major chord on degree 2" and is never explained as a dominant), "borrowed chord" and "modal mixture" (U16: the ♭VII here belongs to the mode, it is not borrowed), "chromatic" (U15.L01), "Ionian", "Aeolian", "Locrian" and "chord-scale" (J2.L04), "parent scale" and "relative mode" (never; U14.L06 says "the same seven notes as D major, with another home"), "avoid note" (never; say "the note that would turn it back into major"). "Vamp", "riff" and "pedal" are words the learner has (U08, U11, U06.L11). New terms: mode, Mixolydian and characteristic tone (U14.L01), Dorian (U14.L03), Lydian (U14.L04), Phrygian (U14.L05); U14.L02 and U14.L06 introduce none.
- Repertoire (contracts §2.8). Old Joe Clark (trad; A Mixolydian in content/repertoire) and Drunken Sailor (trad; D Dorian) are pinned in this spec as they are commonly sung, in plain values, so the lesson authors and author:repertoire work from the same notes; both entries are `verified: false` with the source still to be chosen, and the answer key of content/repertoire wins if the verified print differs. ## U14.L01 Modes as Colours

Author: **spec-U14**. Key G major, 4/4, 104 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** The characteristic tone; Mixolydian ♭7.

**The one idea.** Change one note of a scale I know and its colour changes. G major with its 7th lowered a half step, F♯ to F, is G Mixolydian: looser, the sound of a rock riff. Scales like these are modes, and the one note that sets a mode apart is its characteristic tone. In code it is one word in `.scale()`; the numbers stay.

**Sound first.** One guitar riff over a walking bass and the Band's drums, twice: two bars on G major, bright and reaching up for home, then the same numbers with one note a half step lower. The second time it is a rock riff. Both colours play within the first ten seconds, before either has a name.

```js
setcpm(104/4)

gtr: n("<[0 0 -1 0 ~ 2 4 ~] [6 ~ 4 2 0 ~ -1 0]>").scale("<G3:major!2 G3:mixolydian!2>").s("crunch")
bass: n("0!6 4 6").scale("<G1:major!2 G1:mixolydian!2>").s("ebass").lpf(600)
```

**Skills.** Introduces: S12 Mixolydian & Dorian (needs S05, S06). Practises: none.

**Terms (≤ 3, a pair counts once).** `term.mode` "mode" in S3: A set of notes around one home with its own colour. ## U14.L02 Mixolydian Rock

Author: **U14a**. Key G mixolydian, 4/4, 112 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** I–♭VII–IV; Old Joe Clark.

**The one idea.** The ♭7 gives Mixolydian a chord that major does not have: a plain major chord a whole step below home, written ♭VII. With home and IV it makes I–♭VII–IV, the three-chord loop of rock, and the same note is the top of an old fiddle tune, Old Joe Clark.

**Sound first.** Four bars of rock: an organ on G, F, C and G, an electric bass pumping eighths on the roots and a crunch guitar riff that outlines each chord. In the listen step it follows the same loop with D in place of F, the three-chord song the learner knows: one chord changed, and country became rock.

```js
setcpm(112/4)

const changes = "<G F C G>"

chords: chord(changes).voicing().s("organ")
bass: chord(changes).rootNotes(2).struct("x*8").s("ebass").lpf(600)
gtr: n("<[0 ~ 2 4 ~ 4 2 ~] [6 ~ 3 1 ~ 3 -1 ~] [3 ~ 5 7 ~ 5 3 ~] [4 2 0 ~ ~ ~ ~ ~]>").scale("G3:mixolydian").s("crunch")
```

**Skills.** Introduces: H11 Modal harmony (♭VII, I–♭VII–IV, i–IV) (needs S12, H07). Practises: none.

**Terms (≤ 3, a pair counts once).** none

**Can-do.** `cd.compose.mixolydian` "I can write a Mixolydian riff over I, ♭VII and IV."

**Unlocked here (content/toolbox.ts).** none

### C6.S1: Mixolydian or Dorian vamp and characteristic tone.

- **Opens with.** The brief and its starter (content/capstones.ts): tempo 100, a G Mixolydian vamp, `chord("<G F>")`, a line on `G4:mixolydian` with one bar marked, and a `drums:` line. The brief says in one line how to make the same vamp Dorian instead (the scale word `dorian`, the chords `<Gm7 C7>`) and that the home note is the learner's to move.
- **Hears.** The vamp looping under the marked line; A/B against the last saved draft.
- **Sees.** The capstone workspace: the Workbench (code, roll with degree labels, the chord band with numerals in the piece's own mode: I ♭VII, or i IV), the mode read from the code beside the checklist (`modeView`, wave6.md §3.2: which modes the notes fit, how often the colour note falls on a strong beat, the share of bars on the home chord, any note outside the mode), and the C6 checklist: the `mode` line live, shown as its three parts, and "At least 16 bars" greyed with "next session". Session bar: "Session 1 of 2: The vamp".
- **Work.**
  1. Choose the colour and the home note: Mixolydian (rock, folk) or Dorian (funk, soul), the scale word and the tonic in every `.scale()`; a tempo between 80 and 130.
  2. The vamp: two to four chords with home in at least half the bars. Mixolydian: I and ♭VII, or I–♭VII–IV. Dorian: i and IV, as Dm7 and G7 or their triads.
  3. A bass that states home on beat 1 and touches the colour note on its way round.
  4. A first hook of two to four bars that lands on the colour note on beat 1 or 3, at least twice in the piece.
  5. Listen for the one note to leave out: the leading tone in Mixolydian, ♭6 in Dorian. The scale word keeps a line on `n` clear of it; a chord written by name can still bring it in (D in G Mixolydian, Gm in D Dorian).
- **Checks.**
  - `capstones.check` runs the whole rubric on each save; this session's line is `mode` (`tonic: "declared"`, `mode: "mixolydian"`, `anyOf: ["mixolydian", "dorian"]`, `requireCharacteristic: true`; PLANNED with feature `mode.any-of`). `bars` ≥ 16 is shown and not expected yet.
  - Nothing blocks saving; the draft is the session's Make (contracts §9.1). The session is done when a draft is saved (`capstones.saveDraft`, then `capstones.completeSession`).
- **Ends.** Draft saved and bound to C6.S1; the Coda says "Next time: a melody, the groove, and sixteen bars" and shows which parts of the mode line are met. No review, no release.

### C6.S2: Melody, groove, release. (review, release)

- **Opens with.** The C6.S1 draft. If it is still one loop, the workspace offers it in sections (U09.L02): for each labelled track a `…Vamp` const holding what the learner wrote and an empty `…Tune` const, with one `arrange([4, …Vamp], [8, …Tune], [4, …Vamp])` per track (contracts §6.3, STYLE §10), so sixteen bars are a matter of filling the middle.
- **Hears.** The piece in order, sixteen bars or more; A/B between the draft and the last version; the release recording plays the final version once in full.
- **Sees.** The same workspace with the form map showing the sections (click one to loop it), the range lens, both checklist lines live, and the "Review" and "Release" actions at the end. Session bar: "Session 2 of 2: Melody, groove, release".
- **Work.**
  1. A melody over the vamp: four to eight bars in call and answer (U11.L08), resting on 1, 3 or 5 of the home chord and landing on the colour note where the ear will catch it.
  2. The groove, with U13's tools: a chord or a bass note pushed an eighth early (`.early(1/8)`), ghost notes on the hats or the snare (`.velocity()`), a bass that locks to the kick.
  3. Arrange to sixteen bars or more with one `arrange()` per labelled track: the vamp alone, the tune, a break or a variation, the vamp again. Home stays in at least 40% of the bars across the whole piece.
  4. Optional: width with `.pan()` and `.jux(rev)` (U14.L06); a second sound for the answer phrases.
  5. Check, read the review, release.
- **Checks.**
  - The whole rubric of contracts §6.6 C6 (content/capstones.ts): `mode` as above, in one rule (the characteristic tone on a strong beat at least twice; no leading tone in Mixolydian, no ♭6 in Dorian; the home chord in at least 40% of the bars), and `bars` ≥ 16, counted on the piece as written (`arrange()` or one period of the loop, contracts §6.4). Tempo 80–130 from the brief's `bpm`.
  - Release is allowed once every required line is met and the latest passing check graded the code being released (an edited draft says "Check again before you release"); the tutor review (contracts §10.6, two revisions from C5 on) is offered before release and never affects pass or fail.
- **Ends.** Review offered (`review: true`), then release (`release: true`): album track C6 v1 with a recording, a tree-ring cover, deterministic liner notes and the learner's own line. Milestone ms.17 "You can now write in a mode." shows, and the Studio template "Dorian funk vamp" unlocks (`tb.template.dorian-funk-vamp`). Revising later makes v2.
