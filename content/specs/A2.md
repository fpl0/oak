The second unit of the Arrangement & Sound track (contracts §2.6): six lessons that open after U10.CP and follow A1.L07 in the track's order, then the track checkpoint A.CP over A1–A2 and the two capstones. A1 shaped one sound at a time; A2 shapes how the parts sound together. Each part has a job and a height (A2.L01, the exemplar: registers, why low close chords turn to mud, Oak's spectrum panel); a section can be thin or thick (A2.L02); a second line can answer the tune (A2.L03); a recorded drum break can be cut up and played in a new order (A2.L04); every part gets its level and its room (A2.L05); and one tune can wear two styles (A2.L06). ## A2.L01 Roles & Registers

Author: **spec-A**. Key A minor, 4/4, 90 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Frequency space and why low thirds turn to mud; Oak's spectrum panel.

**The one idea.** Every part has a job and a height: the bass low, the chords in the middle, the tune above them. Two notes a third apart are clear in the middle of the keyboard and blur into one rumble low down, so chords voiced in the bass's octave turn the low end to mud. Oak's spectrum panel (`.fscope()`) draws where each part sits, low on the left, high on the right.

**Sound first.** The A2 loop twice within 20 seconds: first the chords and the tune an octave too low, the chords sitting on the bass (a blur); then the same notes with each part at its own height (clear), the spectrum panel drawing both.

## A2.L02 Texture & Density

Author: **A2a**. Key A minor, 4/4, 90 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Thin vs thick.

**The one idea.** How many parts play at once, and how they relate, is the texture; how many notes sound in a bar is the density. A thin section leaves room and makes the next thick one land: arrangers thin a verse and fill a chorus without changing a note of the tune.

**Sound first.** Eight bars of the A2 loop in two sections within the first 20 seconds: a thin verse (bass on the one, two-note chords, the tune) and a thick chorus (the bass in eighths, full chords four times a bar, a strings pad above), the same tune over both.

```js
setcpm(90/4)

const chordsThin = n("<[0,2] [-2,0] [-5,-3] [-1,1]>")
const chordsThick = n("<[0,2,4]*4 [-2,0,2]*4 [-5,-3,-1]*4 [-1,1,3]*4>")
const bassThin = note("<a1 f1 c2 g1>")
const bassThick = note("<a1*8 f1*8 c2*8 g1*8>")
const padThick = n("<[0,4] [-2,2] [-5,-1] [-1,3]>")
bass: arrange([4, bassThin], [4, bassThick]).s("sawtooth").lpf(400)
chords: arrange([4, chordsThin], [4, chordsThick]).scale("A3:minor").s("piano").gain(.6)
pad: arrange([4, silence], [4, padThick]).scale("A4:minor").s("strings").gain(.3)
mel: n("<[4 ~ 2 0] [2 ~ 0 ~] [4 2 4 ~] [1 ~ 6 ~]>").scale("A4:minor").s("square").lpf(2000).gain(.5)
```

**Skills.** Introduces: D09 Texture, density, counter-melody, genre re-arrangement (needs D08, M07, F06). Practises: F05 Energy curve & contrast (needs F02).

**Terms (≤ 3, a pair counts once).** `term.texture` "texture" + `term.density` "density" in S3: How many parts play at once and how they relate: one line alone, a tune over chords, many lines woven together. `term.density` "density" + `term.texture` "texture" in S4: How full the music is: how many notes and parts sound in a bar. Thin leaves room; thick fills it.

**Can-do.** `cd.compose.texture` "I can thin a texture out and fill it back up."

**Unlocked here (content/toolbox.ts).** none

**Tools the learner's code uses.** `tb.setcpm` `tb.label` `tb.const` `tb.mini.seq` `tb.mini.rest` `tb.mini.sub` `tb.mini.alt` `tb.mini.repeat` `tb.mini.stack` `tb.gain` `tb.s` `tb.s.pitched` `tb.n` `tb.scale` `scale.minor` `tb.note` `tb.note.names` `snd.drum.bd` `snd.drum.sd` `snd.drum.hh` `snd.wave.sawtooth` `snd.wave.square` `snd.wave.triangle` `snd.piano` `snd.strings` `tb.lpf` `tb.room` `tb.fscope` `tb.arrange`

## A2.L03 Counter-Melody & Re-Voicing per Section

Author: **A2a**. Key A minor, 4/4, 90 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** A second line and section-specific voicings.

**The one idea.** A counter-melody is a second tune that moves against the main one: it moves when the tune holds, and when both move it often goes the other way, so the ear can follow two lines at once. It sits in its own register, under or over the tune, never doubling it for long.

**Sound first.** The A2 loop with a strings line under the tune that fills the tune's gaps and moves against it, within the first 20 seconds; then without it, for comparison.

```js
setcpm(90/4)

bass: note("<a1 f1 c2 g1>").s("sawtooth").lpf(400)
chords: n("<[0,2,4] [-2,0,2] [-5,-3,-1] [-1,1,3]>").scale("A3:minor").s("piano").gain(.5)
mel: n("<[4 ~ 2 0] [2 ~ 0 ~] [4 2 4 ~] [1 ~ 6 ~]>").scale("A4:minor").s("square").lpf(2000).gain(.5)
vox: n("<[~ ~ 0 2] [~ 0 2 ~] [2 ~ ~ 4] [~ 1 ~ -1]>").scale("A3:minor").s("strings").gain(.5)
```

**Skills.** Introduces: none. Practises: D09 Texture, density, counter-melody, genre re-arrangement (needs D08, M07, F06); M07 Call & response (needs M06).

**Terms (≤ 3, a pair counts once).** `term.counter-melody` "counter-melody" in S3: A second tune that moves against the main one: it moves when the melody holds, often the other way.
