# U07 Melody: unit spec

<!-- GENERATED from content/specs/U07.ts by `bun scripts/content/spec.ts`. Edit the .ts, never this file; content/validate/specs.test.ts fails when they differ. -->

Ten lessons on writing tunes that go somewhere, over the chords U05–U06 built. A line has a shape (contour, one high point, steps and leaps); it is built from a motif that comes back, moved (sequence) or turned round (retrograde); it lands on chord tones on the strong beats and decorates them with passing and neighbour tones on the weak ones; it leans on a strong beat and resolves (appoggiatura, suspension); its phrases ask and answer (antecedent, consequent), trade with another part (call and response), start before the bar (pickups), and are read and sung from a lead sheet (Amazing Grace, 3/4 with a pickup). The Hook Lab puts it together with controlled chance. Melodic dictation is pushed to `fam.mel.dictation` level 5–6 by a by-ear step in most lessons, which is what X1 needs (level ≥ 4). The Transcription Gym starts here (GYM.01 after U07.L03). U07.CP follows U08.L01 and GYM.03 in the default order and opens C2, then X1.

## Conventions for this unit

- Melodies are written in degrees, `n("…").scale("C4:major")`, over chords an octave lower (U05 convention). Prose names a note degree first, letter in brackets: "degree 5 (G)". Chord names in prose are lead-sheet symbols and Roman numerals (U06.L02 on): "over V (G)".
- The harmony under a tune is the Band's whenever the Band's chords fit the lesson (`band.axis-4bar` I–V–vi–IV, `band.phrase-8bar` I IV I V | I IV V I, `band.two-chord-vamp` I–IV), so a Make sounds like a song from the first run. Where the lesson needs chords the Band does not play (I–IV–V–I with a suspension, a pickup bar where the chords wait), the chords are a `chords:` line of stacks in the learner's code and the Band plays `tracks: ["drums"]`. A Band never starts late: its bass and chords loop from bar 0, so every lesson with a pickup uses drums only.
- Chord tones are judged on the strong beats, 1 and 3 in 4/4 (the `chord-tones-strong` rule's `strength: "strong"`; contracts §6.6). Every hook was checked by hand against its chords: on beats 1 and 3 every note is a chord tone of the chord sounding (a sixth or seventh in the jazz-soul Band's chords counts too), except where the lesson is about a note that is not (U07.L04 on the weak beats, U07.L05's leaning notes).
- Non-chord tones are named as the grader's `toneRole` names them (wave4.md §3.1): passing, neighbour, appoggiatura, suspension; anticipation and escape tone are left to later units. The chord-tone and range lenses (`tb.lens.chord-tones`, `tb.lens.range`) unlock in U07.L03 and U07.L01; a step opens them with `panels` (planned feature `step.initial-panels`).
- Melodic dictation (stage C, contracts §2.9) is pushed towards X1: a graded by-ear step (`dictate`, `byEar`, AUTHORING §16.4) in U07.L03, L07, L08 and L10, at `fam.mel.dictation` level 4–5 material (leaps, two bars, dictated rhythm), with evidence to the family. X1 opens at `fam.mel.dictation` level ≥ 4; U07.L04 opens level 6 (non-chord tones).
- Pickups: before U07.L08 nothing starts with one (contracts §2.8, §6.11). From U07.L08 on, a tune with a pickup is written with leading rests in bar 0 and the step or lesson declares `pickup: { units }` in beats (`[1, 1]` is one quarter note; the grader divides by the beats in a bar). The repertoire's `pickup` values for Auld Lang Syne, Michael Row and Amazing Grace are written `[1, 4]` and `[1, 3]`; they mean one beat, and must be `[1, 1]` (reported to author:songs).
- Randomness (`?`, `.degradeBy()`, `.sometimesBy()`, U07.L10) is never under an exact-match grader (STYLE §11 rule 6): only in rubric-graded Makes and ungraded steps.
- Words that wait: "cadence" kinds are U06's and may be used; "form", "verse", "chorus" (U09), "riff" in the blues sense (U11.L08), "syncopation" (U13), "anticipation" and "escape tone" (later). "Hook" is plain English (no glossary term); "phrase" is plain English from U03.
- Wave 4 shapes (AUTHORING §17) U07 needs before its lessons can pass: `step.initial-panels` (L01, L03, L09), `lead-sheet` (L09), `slot-choice` (L03 S4), the `pickup` rule (L09's Make suggestion; L08 uses an implemented `onsets` rule instead). The rubric rule `non-chord-tones` (count passing / neighbour / appoggiatura / suspension notes from `harmonyView`) is implemented, so L04 and L05 require their own idea with it. The grader is not given the Band's code, so a rule that reads harmony reads a `chords:` line in the learner's code (`harmony: "chords"`), never `band.chords`; those steps play the Band on drums and bass.
- U07's "Play from the page" step is U07.L09 S3: phrase 1 of Amazing Grace shown only as a lead sheet, written in code.
- Feedback the grader emits on U07 code steps (seen in U07.L08's tests): `melody.wrong-degree`, `melody.missing-note`, `melody.rhythm-only`, `melody.rhythm-note`, `melody.index-shift`, `rhythm.pickup-missing` (only from the `rhythm` matcher: put it in an `all` with `melody` where a pickup matters), `code.unchanged`, `code.edit-outside`, `rubric.unmet`. Override `rubric.unmet` with "{label}: not yet." where a rule's own detail says nothing a learner can use (`onsets`: "the onsets differ from the brief").

## U07.L01 Shape

Author: **U07a**. Key C major, 4/4, 92 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Contour, range, steps vs leaps.

**The one idea.** A melody has a shape: it moves mostly by step with a few leaps, and it reaches its high point once, late, and comes home. The range lens shows the shape; you place the high point on purpose.

**Sound first.** An eight-bar tune over the Band's phrase (I IV I V | I IV V I): it climbs by steps and small leaps, reaches its highest note, degree 4 an octave up (F), in bar 6, and steps home.

```js
setcpm(92/4)

mel: n("<[0 1 2 ~] [3 4 5 ~] [4 ~ 2 ~] [1@3 ~] [2 ~ 4 5] [10 9 7 ~] [6 5 4 ~] [2 1 0@2]>").scale("C4:major").s("piano")
```

## U07.L02 Motif & Variation

Author: **U07a**. Key C major, 4/4, 96 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Repetition, sequence, retrograde.

**The one idea.** A tune is built from a motif, a short idea that comes back: the same, moved to start on another degree (a sequence), or played backwards (retrograde). `.scaleTranspose()` moves it by degrees; `.rev()` turns it round.

**Sound first.** One four-note figure, 1 3 5 3, played on the root of each chord of the four-chord loop over the Band: the same shape four times, each starting somewhere else.

```js
setcpm(96/4)

mel: n("0 2 4 2").scale("C4:major").scaleTranspose("<0 4 5 3>").s("piano")
```

## U07.L04 Passing & Neighbour Tones

Author: **U07b**. Key C major, 4/4, 88 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Stepwise non-chord tones between and around chord tones.

**The one idea.** Between chord tones a tune may step through a note that is not in the chord (a passing tone) or step off one and come straight back (a neighbour tone). They sit on the weak beats and move by step, so the ear hears them as motion, not as wrong notes.

**Sound first.** Over the I–IV vamp, a plain tune of chord tones, then the same tune with a note slipped between each pair: C D E, A B A, G F E, F E F.

```js
setcpm(88/4)

mel: n("<[0 1 2 ~] [5 6 5 ~] [4 3 2 ~] [3 2 3 ~]>").scale("C4:major").s("piano")
```

**Skills.** Introduces: M04 Passing & neighbour tones (needs M03). Practises: none.

- PAC needs the melody on 1 over a root-position I (`cadenceOf`), so the brief's template gives a bass of roots: the learner always has a way to put the root in the bass (§6.7 item 5).
- After editing content/capstones.ts, run `bun scripts/content/spec.ts`: this document renders C2's rubric from it.

## X1 Folk Transcription

Author: **songs**. The first transcription on the album (sessions 81–83 in the default order): a whole public-domain folk tune by ear, Aura Lee (1861) or Red River Valley (trad), phrase by phrase, then its chords bass first, then arranged and released. The learner is told the meter, the key and its tonic, where the pickup falls and the rhythm of phrase 1; everything else is heard. Pass is 0.85 per melody phrase.

**Opens.** When U07.CP is passed (a soft pass counts), C2 is released, and `fam.mel.dictation` is at level ≥ 4 (contracts §2.4, §6.6). While it is closed, `capstones.gate` lists each condition with `met`; when only the dictation level is short, Today says "Two more gym sets open X1" (`gymSetsToOpen`) and the planner schedules the gym's dictation sets. X1 never gates a lesson; a learner may leave it closed and carry on into U08.

**Band.** None while transcribing: the source is the song itself, played as a pattern source (hidden reference code in a hidden adapter instance, contracts §6.12) with Oak's plain accompaniment (block chords and a root bass) at realism R1, the same for every learner. In X1.S3's arrangement the learner adds what they like under their melody, bass and chords: a `drums:` line, a second sound; the whole arrangement is their code.

### X1.S1: Phrases 1–2 of Aura Lee or Red River Valley, by ear, checked per phrase.

- **Opens with.** The song choice (two cards, each with a 4-bar preview, title, year or "trad" and the source), then the givens (`TranscribeGiven`): meter, key and tonic ("C major, home is C"), the pickup ("the tune starts on beat 4 of bar 0" or "on beat 1"), and the rhythm skeleton of phrase 1 as code (`mel: s("…")` hits on the rhythm, no pitches). Starter: the skeleton turned into `n("⟪…⟫").scale("C4:major")` with every pitch `0`, bar 0 present if the song has a pickup.
- **Hears.** The song, a phrase at a time: rate 25–100 %, ±12 semitones ("Fit my voice"), an A–B loop on the phrase, the bar timeline in place of a waveform (no fine-tune for a pattern source), and V to switch original ⇄ mine at the same bar.
- **Sees.** The capstone workspace in transcription mode: the phrase strip (Phrase 1 · 2 · 3 · 4, each with its state), the Workbench (code and roll; the source's code never shown), the givens card, and a Check button per phrase. Session bar: "Session 1 of 3: Phrases 1–2".
- **Work.**
  1. Phrase 1: find home, the first note, then each move; the rhythm is given. Check.
  2. Phrase 2: the same without the rhythm. Check.
  3. Hints follow the normal ladder; a reveal shows the phrase, gives no evidence and is named in the liner notes.
- **Checks.**
  - `PhraseCheck` `phrase-1`, `phrase-2`: `melody`, `pitch: "key-relative"`, `wPitch: 0.8`, `wRhythm: 0.2`, `rhythmTol: [1, 16]`, pass ≥ 0.85, with the song's `pickup` in the context (graded in the browser in capstone mode, recorded with `capstones.recordCheck`).
  - A phrase written from the bar line where the song has a pickup: add the `rhythm` matcher in an `all` so the learner reads `rhythm.pickup-missing`, not a list of wrong rhythms (as U07.L08 S5).
- **Ends.** Draft saved (the transcription code so far) and bound to X1.S1; the session completes when both checks are recorded (passed or revealed). Coda: "Phrases 1–2 done; next time 3–4".

### X1.S2: Phrases 3–4.

- **Opens with.** The X1.S1 draft; the givens card stays; no rhythm skeleton for phrases 3–4.
- **Hears.** As X1.S1, with the A–B loop on phrases 3–4; "play from bar N" for the joins.
- **Sees.** The same; phrases 1–2 marked done (or revealed).
- **Work.**
  1. Phrase 3: often a return of phrase 1 or a new line for the chorus; check.
  2. Phrase 4: the ending, home; check.
  3. Join the four phrases into one line and play it against the original from bar 0.
- **Checks.**
  - `phrase-3`, `phrase-4`: the same matcher and pass mark as X1.S1.
- **Ends.** Draft saved; complete when both checks are recorded. Coda: "The whole tune is down; next time its chords".

### X1.S3: Chords bass-first, arrange, review, release. (review, release)

- **Opens with.** The X1.S2 draft with a `bass:` line added (roots, one a bar, all `0`) and a `chords:` line of rests.
- **Hears.** The original's bass and chords (the melody layer can be muted in the source mixer), A/B with the learner's lines.
- **Sees.** The stage strip Bass → Quality → Numerals (a stage opens when the one before passes or is revealed), the chord band showing the learner's chords as symbols and numerals, then the arrange view (Workbench `full`) and the Review and Release actions. Session bar: "Session 3 of 3: Chords, arrange, release".
- **Work.**
  1. Bass: the root of each bar's chord, by ear (U06.L05's skill).
  2. Quality: major or minor over each root (the song's chords are diatonic triads, some with a 7th in the source).
  3. Numerals: the whole progression in Roman numerals; the learner's chords line now plays the stacks.
  4. Arrange: melody, bass, chords and, if wanted, a `drums:` line; a short intro allowed. The arrangement is the learner's (not graded).
- **Checks.**
  - `PhraseCheck` `chords`, `phrase: [1, 4]`, `layer: "chords"`, `stages: ["bass", "quality", "rn"]`, `matcher: { m: "harmony", level: "rn", qualityDepth: "triad", inversion: false, harmonicRhythm: "given" }`, pass ≥ 0.85 per stage (contracts §6.6 as of 1.6; it was 0.80).
  - The arrangement is not graded; release needs the four phrase checks and the three stages recorded (passed or revealed).
- **Ends.** Tutor transcription review offered (`review: true`, contracts §10.6: sections, plausibility flags, next focus; never pass or fail), then release (`release: true`): album track X1 v1, recording, cover, liner notes.

**Liner notes.**

- The song: title, year or "trad", status (US-PD / trad), the source edition (`provenance`), "Oak's rendition; transcribed by ear".
- Key, meter, the pickup, form (4 phrases), the numerals of the chords.
- Per phrase: transcribed by ear (score) or "revealed" (contracts §6.6: a revealed phrase is named); the three chord stages likewise.
- Instruments of the learner's arrangement; the learner's line; the tutor's listening note only with consent.

**Tests the brief or the checks must carry.**

- `bun run content:check X1` resolves every check against both songs (`capstoneSessionSections`): until each song's answer key has `answerKey.phrases` (4) and `layers.melody` / `layers.chords`, the checks are pending.
- Per song: the answer key's phrase passes each melody check; the same phrase in another key passes (key-relative); one wrong note in a phrase of ≥ 8 notes still passes (≥ 0.85) and names `melody.wrong-degree`; a phrase a degree off throughout fails with `melody.index-shift`; a phrase written from the bar line fails with `rhythm.pickup-missing` (when the song has a pickup and the check has the `rhythm` matcher).
- Chords: the answer key's bass passes stage 1; the right bass with a minor chord where the key has major fails stage 2; the numerals with IV for ii fail stage 3.

- Pool: Aura Lee (1861) and Red River Valley (trad), never Amazing Grace (§2.8). Both are 4/4 in Oak's teaching key C (content/repertoire/index.ts); author:songs writes `content/repertoire/aura-lee/song.ts` and `red-river-valley/song.ts` with verified answer keys, 4 phrases of 4 bars each, and the `pickup` the cited edition has (Red River Valley is usually printed with one; Aura Lee starts on the beat): `{ units: [n, 1] }` in beats.
- content/capstones.ts (author:songs) needs, per session, the `checks` above (X1.S1: phrase-1, phrase-2; X1.S2: phrase-3, phrase-4; X1.S3: chords), phrase 1's `given` with the rhythm skeleton, and `starter` for S1. A stage exercise written out uses `stimulus: { src: "capstone-song", bars, layers }` (planned `stimulus.capstone-song`, `transcribe.stages`).
- X1 sessions take the gym slot when they are next (§2.5): a learner in X1 has no separate gym session until X1.S3 is done.
- The source plays at R1 for everyone so that X1's difficulty is the song's, not the learner's gym realism; grading tolerances never change with realism (§6.12).

## Transcription Gym

The Transcription Gym is how a learner keeps transcribing between capstones: every fifth session from U07.L01 is a transcription session (GYM.nn), alternating a graded ladder session and a session on the learner's own audio. Nobody authors a gym session: ladder levels 1–4 are generated by @oak/drills, levels 5–6 draw on the repertoire, own-audio sessions use the templates in content/gym/templates.ts. The surface is the Transcription Studio (`/transcribe`, owner: transcribe); the plan is `gym.plan` and the ladder `ladder.*` (wave4.md §5.4–5.5).

**The slot rule (content/gym/slots.ts).** From U07.L01, after 4 completed core sessions since the last transcription session, the next session's New block is a transcription slot.

- Counting starts at U07.L01. Completed core sessions since the last transcription session (a gym session, an `X#.S#` or a `CF-T.S#`) are counted; at 4 the next session's New block is a transcription slot. Two exceptions: a capstone in progress is never interrupted (the slot waits until its last session), and an `X#` session that is next anyway takes the slot.
- What fills a slot: the next CF-T session if CF-T is open (U15.L01 reached) and unfinished; otherwise a gym session with the next free `GYM.nn` (the learner's own ordinals; an optional extra gym session from Today takes the next number too).
- Odd `GYM.nn`: a graded ladder session at the learner's current ladder level and realism level. Even `GYM.nn`: an own-audio session (self-assessed, "hum it, Oak drafts it" available). With no own-audio project, an even slot is a ladder session one realism level up (`ownAudioFallback: true`).
- A gym session is a full session in the plan, the pace estimate and the streak (contracts §9.6); its Make is the saved transcription version. Today shows it as the New block ("Transcription Gym · Ladder L2, R1" or "· Your audio"); "Do something else instead" can swap it, and the slot stays due.
- In the advanced tracks the slot rule does not run: Today offers the gym once a week as an alternative chip.

**Slots in the default order.** GYM.01 (session 67, after U07.L03; ladder); GYM.02 (session 72, after U07.L07; own-audio); GYM.03 (session 77, after U08.L01; ladder); GYM.04 (session 88, after U08.L05; own-audio); GYM.05 (session 93, after U09.L04; ladder); GYM.06 (session 101, after C3.S4; own-audio); GYM.07 (session 106, after U10.L05; ladder); GYM.08 (session 111, after U10.L09; own-audio); GYM.09 (session 116, after C4.S2; ladder); GYM.10 (session 121, after U11.L05; own-audio); GYM.11 (session 126, after U12.L01; ladder); GYM.12 (session 136, after U12.L05; own-audio); GYM.13 (session 141, after U13.L03; ladder); GYM.14 (session 146, after U14.L01; own-audio); GYM.15 (session 151, after U14.L04; ladder); GYM.16 (session 176, after U17.L04; own-audio); GYM.17 (session 181, after C7.S3; ladder).

**Realism.**

- R0 clean and quantized; R1 timing humanized ±15 ms and velocity ±0.1; R2 swing or shuffle feel where idiomatic, plus room; R3 dense mix with the target part 6 dB down; R4 vocal-like lead (`tenor`, `choir`) and detune up to ±30¢ (contracts §6.12; `realismSpec` in @oak/strudel).
- Within a level, realism also widens the material a little (more keys from R2, drums under L2–L4 from R2, 4-bar items from R3), as `generateLadderPiece` does.
- Up one step after two consecutive ladder sessions whose best item scored ≥ 0.9; down one after a session below 0.6 (`nextRealism`). The change is announced once, in the Coda.
- Grading tolerances never change with realism; answers stay on the grid.

**Moving up the ladder.**

- Every learner starts at L1, R0 at GYM.01 (dev seed `after U07.L03 (gym due)`).
- A level-up to the next level is offered (`ladder.get` `levelUp`) after two graded passes at the current level at realism R2 or higher, on two different learner days (wave4.md §5.4). The learner takes it on the gym screen (`ladder.setLevel`) or stays; they may move down a level at any time. A new level starts at R0.
- A graded pass is a submission at ≥ 0.85 (each stage ≥ 0.80 at L3) with nothing revealed (`ladderGradedPass`). A hum-to-draft that was not corrected and checked never counts.
- L4 → L5 is offered only once X1 is released (contracts §2.9: L5's first availability is X1), and L5 → L6 only once X2 is released. L7 is never a ladder level a learner moves to: it is the even slots.
- Ladder milestones (`ms.*` with `ladder: { level }`) count graded passes only.

**Own audio (even slots).**

- The learner drops an audio file ("it stays on this device"); the project opens in the Transcription Studio with the waveform (wavesurfer), rate 25–100 %, ±12 semitones, ±50¢ fine-tune, an A–B loop, and the BeatGrid (tap tempo, drag the bar-1 marker).
- The learner marks a region (a hook, a bass riff, a chorus), picks a layer mode its unlocks allow (content/gym/templates.ts: Melody from U07.L01; Bass, Chords and Full arrangement as their `tb.feature.transcribe.*` rows unlock) and writes it in code, switching original ⇄ mine (V) or overlaying them (O).
- "Hum it, Oak drafts it": the learner sings the line; Oak writes a draft `n("…").scale("…")` flagged machine-made (`machineDraft: true`); it gives no evidence until the learner has edited it and filled the checklist.
- The checklist is self-assessed, each row 0–2 (`OWN_AUDIO_CHECKLIST`: contour, rhythm, landing, bass, chords; rows outside the chosen layers are hidden). The session saves a transcription version; `status: "ungraded"`, evidence 0.3 flagged `selfReported`.
- The tutor may review the transcription (`transcription:{projectId}` thread): it sees the code, the checklist and the title the learner typed, never the audio ("Audio never sent" chip).
- With no project, Today offers "Add a song you love" on the gym card; if the learner skips, the slot is a ladder session one realism level up.

**Notes.**

- In the default order the first three slots fall inside U07 (GYM.01 ladder L1, GYM.02 own audio or its fallback, GYM.03 ladder), and X1.S1–S3 take the slot after C2 (sessions 81–83).
- Ladder stimuli have no audio file: the hidden reference plays through the pattern source (§6.12); the browser receives events, never the hidden code (Wave 1 decision, wave4.md §5.3).
- content/gym/ladder.ts gives L3 `pass: 0.8` (per stage), which matches X1's and X2's stage rule; contracts §6.12 says ladder answers pass at ≥ 0.85 "unless §6.6 says otherwise". The level-up rule above uses each level's own pass mark.
- Two checklists exist: content/gym/templates.ts gives each layer mode its own rows; the API's `OWN_AUDIO_CHECKLIST` has five fixed rows. The gym's even slots should use one: this spec uses the API's five rows, filtered by layer, and the templates' rows as the prompts under each (transcribe and server to settle).
- Nothing in the gym is authored by content authors; drills-engine owns `generateLadderPiece` and content/gym, server owns `gym.plan` and `ladder.*`, transcribe owns the Studio.
