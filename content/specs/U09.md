# U09 Form: unit spec

Five lessons on how a song is put together from sections, taught on the kind of music the learner has already made: the four-bar hooks of U07, C2's eight bars that loop and land, the bass lines of U08. Bars group into four-bar phrases and phrases into sections, the first bar of each four feeling strong (hypermeter, U09.L01); two sections that contrast, verse and chorus, written as section `const`s with one `arrange()` per labelled track (U09.L02, the exemplar); two more song forms, AABA with its bridge and strophic, heard in songs the learner already knows (U09.L03); the energy curve, with a build-up drawn by a signal (`saw.range(.2, .9).slow(4)`) and stepped with `.segment()` (U09.L04); and arranging, parts entering and leaving by bar with `.mask()` (U09.L05). U09.CP covers U08 and U09 and opens C3 Verse–Chorus Demo (four sessions since contracts 1.6) and the side lessons A1.L05–L07. In the default order U09.L01–L04 follow GYM.04; GYM.05 sits before U09.L05, and U10.L01 comes before U09.CP (session 96), so the checkpoint falls on a later day than the unit's last lesson.

## Conventions for this unit

- Sections in code (contracts §6.3, STYLE §10). Each section of each part is a `const` named `<role><Section>` in camelCase: `chordsVerse`, `bassChorus`, `melA`, `hatsBuild`, `fxBuild`; the role prefix is a label of STYLE §2, the section word one of `intro verse pre chorus bridge solo break head vamp lift outro` or a letter `a b c`, with a digit for a variant (`melChorus2` is a chorus). Every labelled track that changes between sections gets **one** `arrange()` at the root of its chain (`chords: arrange([4, chordsVerse], [4, chordsChorus]).s("piano")`), listing the same sections with the same bar counts as every other arranged track; a part that rests in a section uses `silence`; a part that never changes stays a plain line (a through track). `arrange()` is never wrapped (`stack(arrange(…))`, `arrange(…).mask(…)` across sections is fine only as a method chain on the same track).
- Sections restart (contracts §6.3 item 7, evaluated): inside `arrange()` every section plays from its own bar 1, so a section's length is a whole multiple of its pattern's length (`<C Am F G>` is four bars: sections of 4 or 8, never 6; `form.section-period` otherwise), a build drawn with `.slow(4)` inside a four-bar section starts again at each section, and an intro's `.mask("<0 1 1 1>")` lets the bass in at bar 2 of the intro every time the intro plays. Anything that must run across sections (a fade over the whole song) goes after `arrange()`.
- Lesson sections are four bars, so a whole song of four sections is 16 bars at 100–112 BPM (34–38 seconds) and the chorus arrives within 10 seconds of Play; the lesson-mode grader reads up to 16 bars of a learner's loop (contracts §6.4), so a graded reference is at most 16 bars. C3's sections are eight bars (§6.6 asks for 90 seconds).
- Form is taught on what the learner made and what they know. The hooks are shaped like their own work: U09.L01's eight bars are the question-and-answer pair of U07.L06 over the Band's eight-bar phrase (C2's "loop, then land" is one section of the same kind); U09.L02's verse is the doo-wop loop of U06.L07 with a U07-style tune and a U08 root bass. Every Coda suggests the same thing for Studio, which is never gated: open your C2 track or a U07 hook and give it what today added (a crash on each phrase, a chorus, a bridge, a build-up, an intro). The forms they know from records are the songs already met in Oak: Ode to Joy is A A' B A' (U02.L08, U03.L09), When the Saints and Michael, Row the Boat Ashore are strophic (U02.L08, U03.L10), Swing Low and Auld Lang Syne have a verse and a chorus (U03.L10); standards of the 1920s–30s such as I Got Rhythm are AABA (named only: no recording is played).
- Harmony stays in the learner's code wherever a rule or a graded step reads it (AUTHORING §8 rule 5), so a lesson whose song has chords plays the Band on drums only (`band.axis-4bar`, `tracks: ["drums"]`). U09.L01's Make is a tune over the Band's eight-bar phrase (`band.phrase-8bar`, every track). The verse loop is I vi IV V (C Am F G) and the chorus IV V I I (F G C C) in U09.L02 and L04; the AABA bridge in U09.L03 is IV iii ii V (F Em Dm G). Every hook was checked against its chords: on beats 1 and 3 every tune note is a chord tone.
- Grading (AUTHORING §18.2). Implemented today: a track's arrangement is an `events` match on that track over the whole song (bass in degrees octave-free, drums by role, `pcset` per onset for voiced chords), and the Make of U09.L02 reads the chords' order with `matches` + a `harmony` matcher at level `rn` against a V C V C reference that includes the root bass (so `.voicing()`'s E under C is not reported as a wrong bass). PLANNED until the grader lists them: the rules `form`, `contrast`, `duration-sec`, `groove-lock`; the `form` answer (U09.L03); the `controls` matcher (U09.L04 S4, on `gain`); the form-map panel (`initialPanels: ["form"]`, feature `panel.wave5`). A lesson that uses one fails check 2 until it lands: its author writes it, says so, and keeps a fallback in the notes (the exemplar's `matches` shape for `form`).
- The form lens (`tb.lens.form`, U09.L02) draws a ribbon of sections over the roll and the staff from the learner's own `arrange()` (wave5.md §4.3–4.4); a click or Enter on a section loops it. From U09.L03 the investigate steps open the form map with `initialPanels: ["form"]` (planned). A piece whose tracks disagree shows the rule's message, "Write one arrange() per labelled track", never a broken ribbon. The lead sheet draws a label and a double bar at each section (notation `sectionMarks`).
- Words that wait and words that are plain. Terms: "hypermeter" (U09.L01), "verse" and "chorus" (a pair, U09.L02 S3), "song form" (U09.L02 S4), "bridge" and "strophic" (U09.L03), "build-up" and "energy curve" (U09.L04); U09.L05 introduces none. Before U09.L02 S3 nothing says "verse" or "chorus" (U09.L01 says "section", "the part that comes back"); before U09.L03 nothing says "bridge" (U04.L01's plain use is allowed). Plain English, not terms: "section", "phrase", "intro", "outro", "drop", "riser", "arrangement", "lift". Still waiting: "syncopation" (U13), "swing" (U11), "filter" and "cutoff" (A1.L02, a side lesson the learner may not have taken).
- Ear training: `fam.frm.sections` (stage D's form family, contracts §7.3) is seeded in U09.L03 at level 1 (AABA against verse–chorus); its level 2 (four- or eight-bar phrases) opens at the same lesson. `fam.har.cadence` is reviewed in U09.L01 (phrases end open or closed). No sing step in U09 (a form is heard, not sung; contracts §8.3 asks for singing in the warm-ups' sets); U09.L01 has a tap step, never graded.

## U09.L01 Phrases Make Sections

Author: **U09a**. Key C major, 4/4, 100 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** 4- and 8-bar units, hypermeter.

**The one idea.** Bars group the way beats do: four bars make a phrase, and the first bar of each four feels strong, like beat 1 of a bar. Two phrases that belong together, a question and its answer, make an eight-bar section. A crash on bar 1 of each phrase, `s("<cr ~ ~ ~>")`, marks the grouping: `< >` with four steps takes four bars.

**Sound first.** Eight bars over I IV I V | I IV V I: a four-bar question that ends open on 2, then an answer that starts the same way and comes home, with a crash on bar 1 and bar 5. The whole section plays within 20 seconds; the second time round the learner counts the phrases.

```js
setcpm(100/4)

chords: n("<[0,2,4] [3,5,7] [0,2,4] [4,6,8] [0,2,4] [3,5,7] [4,6,8] [0,2,4]>").scale("C3:major").s("piano")
mel: n("<[0 2 4 2] [3 5 3 ~] [2 4 2 0] [1 ~ ~ ~] [0 2 4 2] [3 5 3 ~] [1 3 1 -1] [0 ~ ~ ~]>").scale("C4:major").s("piano")
perc: s("<cr ~ ~ ~>")
```

**Skills.** Introduces: none. Practises: F01 Phrase, section, hypermeter (needs R03, M06).

**Terms (≤ 3, a pair counts once).** `term.hypermeter` "hypermeter" in S3: Bars grouped the way beats are: the first bar of a four-bar phrase feels strong, like beat 1 of a bar.

**Can-do.** `cd.hear.sections` "I can hear where a section starts and count a song in 4- and 8-bar phrases."

**Unlocked here (content/toolbox.ts).** none

## U09.L02 Verse & Chorus

Author: **spec-U09**. Key C major, 4/4, 100 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Contrast between sections; one `arrange()` per labelled track.

**The one idea.** A song is built from sections that contrast: the verse, whose music comes back with new words, and the chorus, which comes back with the hook and lifts the song (new chords, a higher tune, something new coming in). In code each section of each part is a `const`, and every part that changes gets one `arrange()` with the same sections in the same order: `chords: arrange([4, chordsVerse], [4, chordsChorus], [4, chordsVerse], [4, chordsChorus])`.

**Sound first.** Over the Band's drums, sixteen bars: a four-bar verse on the doo-wop loop with a tune in the middle, then a chorus on F G C C whose tune climbs a 4th to a 6th higher and brings a tambourine on 2 and 4, then both again. The chorus lands at bar 5, under 10 seconds in.

```js
setcpm(100/4)

const chordsVerse = chord("<C Am F G>").voicing()
const chordsChorus = chord("<F G C C>").voicing()
const bassVerse = n("<0 5 3 4>").scale("C2:major")
const bassChorus = n("<3 4 0 0>").scale("C2:major")
const melVerse = n("<[2 ~ 4 2] [0 ~ 2 ~] [3 ~ 5 3] [1 ~ ~ ~]>").scale("C4:major")
const melChorus = n("<[7 ~ 5 ~] [6 ~ 4 ~] [7 ~ 9 7] [7 ~ ~ ~]>").scale("C4:major")
const percChorus = s("~ tb ~ tb")

chords: arrange([4, chordsVerse], [4, chordsChorus], [4, chordsVerse], [4, chordsChorus]).s("piano")
bass: arrange([4, bassVerse], [4, bassChorus], [4, bassVerse], [4, bassChorus]).s("ebass").lpf(600)
mel: arrange([4, melVerse], [4, melChorus], [4, melVerse], [4, melChorus]).s("piano")
perc: arrange([4, silence], [4, percChorus], [4, silence], [4, percChorus])
```

## U09.L03 AABA & Strophic

Author: **U09a**. Key C major, 4/4, 112 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Two more song forms.

## U09.L04 The Energy Curve

Author: **U09b**. Key C major, 4/4, 112 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Builds and drops with signals.

**The one idea.** A song's energy rises and falls from section to section: louder, busier, higher, more parts. A build-up raises it into the next section. ## C3 Verse–Chorus Demo

Author: **capstones**. The first song with sections on the album (sessions 97–100 in the default order): a verse–chorus demo of at least 90 seconds, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, whose chorus differs from the verse on at least two counts, with a bass locked to the kick and a lead sheet to show for it. Session 1 lays down both sections' groove and changes; session 2 writes both melodies and makes the chorus lift; session 3 arranges the whole song to length and checks the lead sheet; session 4 (contracts 1.6, the learner's decision of 2026-11-13: the real-time take, the lead sheet and the review were too much for one 20-minute session) records the take, takes a review and releases. Everything it asks for was taught in U08–U09; nothing new is introduced. The learner may start from their own C2 (its eight bars become the verse).

Opens after: {"checkpoints":["U09.CP"]}. Meter 4/4, 70–140 BPM. Brief: "Write a song in the form verse, chorus, verse, chorus, 90 seconds or more, with no intro. Make the chorus differ from the verse in two ways or more: a higher tune, busier drums, new chords, more parts. Land at least half the bass notes with a kick. Start from the template, or paste your C2 in as the verse."

Starter:

```js
setcpm(100/4)

// verse and chorus, 8 bars each
const kickVerse = s("bd ~ bd ~")
const kickChorus = s("⟪bd ~ bd ~⟫")
const hatsVerse = s("hh*8").gain(.4)
const hatsChorus = s("⟪hh*8⟫").gain(.4)
const chordsVerse = chord("<C Am F G>").voicing()
const chordsChorus = chord("<F G C C>").voicing()
const bassVerse = n("<0 5 3 4>").struct("⟪x*8⟫").scale("C2:major")
const bassChorus = n("<3 4 0 0>").struct("⟪x*8⟫").scale("C2:major")

kick: arrange([8, kickVerse], [8, kickChorus])
snare: s("~ sd ~ sd")
hats: arrange([8, hatsVerse], [8, hatsChorus])
chords: arrange([8, chordsVerse], [8, chordsChorus]).s("piano")
bass: arrange([8, bassVerse], [8, bassChorus]).s("ebass").lpf(600)
```

### C3.S1: Groove and changes for verse and chorus.

- **Opens with.** The brief, then a choice of two starts. "Start from the template": section consts for both sections, 8 bars each (`kickVerse`, `kickChorus`, `snareVerse`, `hatsVerse`, `hatsChorus`, `chordsVerse = chord("<C Am F G>").voicing()`, `chordsChorus = chord("<F G C C>").voicing()`, `bassVerse`, `bassChorus` on the roots, all `0` where the learner decides), each track arranged verse–chorus once (16 bars), tempo 100, C major. "Start from my C2": the latest version of the learner's C2 track becomes the verse (its chords, bass and drums lines renamed to `chordsVerse`, `bassVerse`, …; its tune kept as `melVerse` for session 2) and the chorus consts start empty. Offered only when C2 is released.
- **Hears.** The draft looping its verse and chorus (16 bars); a click on a section of the form lens loops that section alone; A/B against the last saved draft.
- **Sees.** The capstone workspace: the Workbench (code, roll, lead sheet), the form lens ribbon over the roll (`tb.lens.form`) and the form map panel with each section's energy, the bass-role lens (roots, 5ths, approach notes, notes on the kick), and the C3 checklist read from `formView` and `bassView`: "Bass lands with the kick" (live, with the share), "Verse and chorus both have chords and a bass" (the sections found), then the S2 and S3 lines greyed with "next session". Session bar: "Session 1 of 4: Groove and changes".
- **Work.**
  1. Verse groove: kick, snare and hats; a kick rhythm in a `const` shared with the bass (U08.L04).
  2. Verse changes: a loop of four chords, one a bar, played twice in the eight bars (or the C2 verse as it is).
  3. Chorus changes: chords that start somewhere else (IV or vi is the usual lift) and end home or open; a chorus groove that is busier (open hats, a tambourine, eighths on the bass).
  4. A bass under both: roots on beat 1, 5ths and octaves, approach notes into the changes, locked to the kick.
  5. One `arrange()` per track that changes; the drums that never change may stay through.
- **Checks.**
  - `capstones.check` runs the whole rubric on each save; the session's lines are `groove-lock` (`kick: "@drums"`, `bass: "@bass"`, `min: 0.5`; labelled "Bass lands with the kick") and the sections the form view finds (both a verse and a chorus with chords and bass). The `form`, `contrast`, `duration-sec` and lead-sheet lines are shown as "next sessions" and never fail the draft.
  - Nothing blocks saving; the draft is the session's Make (contracts §9.1). The session is done when a draft is saved (`capstones.saveDraft`, then `capstones.completeSession`).
- **Ends.** Draft saved and bound to C3.S1; the Coda says "Next time: the tunes, and a chorus that lifts" and shows the lines that are met. No review, no release.

### C3.S2: Melodies and section contrast.

- **Opens with.** The C3.S1 draft. `melVerse` and `melChorus` consts are added if the draft has none (`n("⟪~⟫").scale(…)` in the draft's key), with a `mel:` line arranged like the others.
- **Hears.** The draft under the tunes as they grow; the section loop on the form lens to work on one section at a time; A/B between verse and chorus at the same bar of each.
- **Sees.** The same workspace with the chord-tone and range lenses; the form map now draws each section's features and the contrast hints from `contrastAxes` ("The chorus is higher (+5 half steps), denser and louder than the verse"). The checklist line "The chorus differs from the verse on two counts or more" is live, with the counts it hears. Session bar: "Session 2 of 4: Tunes and contrast".
- **Work.**
  1. A verse tune in the middle of the voice: a motif that comes back, chord tones on beats 1 and 3 (U07).
  2. A chorus tune with the hook: higher than the verse (the register line moves), longer notes or a repeated hook phrase.
  3. Make the chorus lift on at least two counts the checklist names: tune higher (pitch, register), busier (density), more parts or louder (energy), new chords (harmony).
  4. Optional: a build-up into the chorus drawn with a signal (U09.L04) or a drop with `.mask()` (U09.L05).
- **Checks.**
  - `contrast` (`a: { section: "chorus" }`, `b: { section: "verse" }`, `axes: ["pitch", "density", "harmony", "register", "energy"]`, `minAxes: 2`), labelled "The chorus differs from the verse on two counts or more"; the line names the counts that pass and the nearest one that does not.
  - `groove-lock` stays live from S1. Saving is never blocked.
- **Ends.** Draft saved and bound to C3.S2; Coda: "Next time: the whole song to 90 seconds, and its lead sheet".

### C3.S3: Arrange V C V C; lead sheet.

- **Opens with.** The C3.S2 draft. The form map shows its length in bars and seconds against 1:30.
- **Hears.** The whole song from bar 1, or any section looped from the form lens.
- **Sees.** The same workspace with the lead-sheet view (section labels and double bars at each section, chord symbols, "Swing 8ths" when the flavour swings); the full checklist live. No "Release" yet: the session bar reads "Session 3 of 4: Arrange and the lead sheet", and the take is next time.
- **Work.**
  1. Arrange verse, chorus, verse, chorus with one `arrange()` per track that changes; a second chorus may be a variant (`melChorus2`) or twice as long.
  2. Reach 90 seconds: at 100 BPM a bar lasts 2.4 seconds, so 8-bar sections give 77 seconds; double the last chorus (40 bars, 96 seconds) or slow to 84 BPM or under (32 bars, 91 seconds). An intro or outro is not a section the `form` rule accepts (see notes).
  3. Open the lead sheet and fix what it cannot write exactly (a tuplet, a note shorter than a sixteenth, a melody track with chords in it).
- **Checks.**
  - The whole rubric of contracts §6.6 C3 (content/capstones.ts): `form` "verse chorus verse chorus" (tags allowed); `contrast` on ≥ 2 axes; `duration-sec` ≥ 90 from the AST (`arrange()` sum ÷ cps, never played); `groove-lock` bass on kick ≥ 0.5; `notates` `view: "lead-sheet"` (renders with `approximate = false`; a declared swing passes). Labels: "Verse, chorus, verse, chorus", "The chorus differs from the verse on two counts or more", "At least 90 seconds", "Bass lands with the kick", "The lead sheet writes your song exactly".
  - Saving is never blocked: a draft with open lines carries them to the last session.
- **Ends.** Draft saved and bound to C3.S3; Coda: "Next time: press record, hear the whole song once through, and release it". The lines that are met are listed; nothing is recorded yet.

### C3.S4: Record the full take, review, release. (review, release)

- **Opens with.** The C3.S3 draft, with the checklist as it stood. A learner who was part-way through the old three-session C3.S3 opens here with everything kept: the draft, every passed check, a take already recorded (then only the review and the release are left).
- **Hears.** The take: the final version played once in full while it records, 90 seconds or more of the learner's own song with nothing to do but listen. Then the recording, from the album player.
- **Sees.** The workspace with the full checklist, then "Record the take" (a count-in, the song's length as a progress line over the form map, each section lighting as it plays), "Review" and "Release", with "Check again before you release" whenever the draft changed after its last passing check (c1). Session bar: "Session 4 of 4: The take and the release".
- **Work.**
  1. A last pass over the open checklist lines, if any.
  2. Record the take: the whole song in real time (about 96 seconds at the template's tempo). It can be taken again; every take is kept as a version (d20).
  3. Ask for a review (optional, never affects pass or fail), then release: title, the learner's one line for the liner notes, the cover.
- **Checks.**
  - The whole rubric once more, on the code being released (contracts §6.6 Release): release is allowed once every required line is met on that code; the tutor review (contracts §10.6, `reviewRevisions: 1`) is offered before release.
- **Ends.** Review offered (`review: true`), then release (`release: true`): album track C3 v1 with a recording (`OakStrudel.recorder`, `album.putAudio`), a tree-ring cover drawn from the code (its rings follow the sections), deterministic liner notes and the learner's own line; milestone ms.11 "You can now write a song with a verse and a chorus." Revising later makes v2.
