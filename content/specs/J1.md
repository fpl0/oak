The first five lessons of the jazz track (contracts §2.6), the sound the learner came for: a swinging rhythm section under a ii–V–I. Music first: the ride pattern and the hi-hat on 2 and 4 make a groove swing (J1.L01, the exemplar); jazz charts write the chords the learner already plays with other glyphs, plus two new colours, the sixth chord and the sus chord (J1.L02); Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 is one gesture, the ii–V–I (J1.L03); its 3rds and 7ths make a thread that moves by a step or stays, the guide tones, sung and written with `.dict("guidetones")` (J1.L04); and the learner comps their own swinging ii–V–I with three-note shells over a supplied walking backing (J1.L05). ## Conventions for this unit

- Written for the learner at the gate (contracts §7.6 rule 5, AUTHORING §20.1): a J1 lesson may be taken with U14–U17 unfinished, so it uses only what U13.CP guarantees plus J1's own unlocks. No mode names or `.scale("D4:dorian")` (U14; chord-scales are J2.L04's, gated on U14.CP), no chromatic `n` steps (U15.L01: an altered note is a note name), no secondary dominants by name (U15), no `horns` / `trumpet` / `tenor`, no `.pan()`. A learner at session 185 reads the same lesson: it never calls the core's ideas new.
- The sound of the unit: the jazz kit (`.bank("jazzkit")`, J1.L01) with the ride pattern on a `drums:` line (`s("rd [rd rd] rd [rd rd]").swingBy(1/3, 4)`) and the hi-hat on 2 and 4 on a `hats:` line; the upright bass; the Rhodes comping with `.dict("lefthand")`; the vibraphone (`vibes`, J1.L01) for melodies. The ride is labelled `drums:` (STYLE §2 has no `ride` label; `drums` is the kit's timekeeping line), so the `swing` rule's track list `["drums", "@drums"]` with `drums: ["ride"]` reads it.
- Swing: `.swingBy(1/3, 4)` on every line that swings (contracts §6.10), swing declared on the lesson (`swing: { subdivision: 8, amount: 1/3 }`), ratio windows [1.6, 2.4] for a check and [1.45, 2.4] for a Make (U11.L07's). The amount → ratio fixture is `swingRatio` (`@oak/content-kit`): `.2` is 3:2, `1/3` is 2:1, `.5` is 3:1. Quarter notes do not swing (§6.10 item 4): a swing check always reads a line with off-beats (the ride's skip notes, comping, the vibes), never the bass.
- Chord symbols (STYLE §5.1, AUTHORING §5 rule 3): code uses `C^7 Cm7 Cm7b5 Co7 C7 C6 Cm6 Csus C7sus`; prose uses the lead-sheet form (Cmaj7, Cm7, Cm7♭5); the jazz glyphs (C∆7, C–7, Cø7, C°7) arrive in J1.L02 with `tb.feature.jazz-glyphs` and are display only: `chord("C–7")` is not code. A chord's name comes from its symbol, never from the notes a dictionary produced (contracts §6.7). `lefthand` is MOSTLY rootless (STYLE §5.2): `C^7` → B D E G (the 7th lowest), `G7` → B E F A (the 3rd lowest), `Cm7b5` keeps its root: never say "the left hand leaves out the root" of all of them; every loop carries a `bass:` line.

## J1.L01 Swing Feel & the Ride

Author: **content-lead**. Key C major, 4/4, 132 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** The ride pattern and swung eighths on a jazz kit; feel first.

**The one idea.** A jazz groove swings because the drummer's ride cymbal plays four beats with a short swung note just after beats 2 and 4 (ding, ding-a, ding, ding-a), and the hi-hat closes on 2 and 4. Written as plain eighths and swung with `.swingBy(1/3, 4)`, the same pattern turns the trio's chords and bass into a swinging rhythm section.

**Sound first.** A jazz trio within the first ten seconds: the ride pattern and the hi-hat on 2 and 4 on the jazz kit, an upright bass in two, Rhodes comping on the Charleston and a vibes line over Dm7 G7 Cmaj7, all swinging. Then the same trio with the ride on plain quarters: it stops swinging.

```js
setcpm(132/4)

drums: s("rd [rd rd] rd [rd rd]").swingBy(1/3, 4).bank("jazzkit")
hats: s("~ hh ~ hh").bank("jazzkit")
bass: note("<[d2 a1] [g1 d2] [c2 g1] [c2 e2]>").s("upright")
chords: chord("<Dm7 G7 C^7 C^7>").dict("lefthand").voicing().struct("x ~ ~ x ~ ~ ~ ~").swingBy(1/3, 4).s("rhodes").gain(.6)
mel: n("<[3 ~ 5 0] [1 ~ 3 ~] [2 4 6 4] [2 ~ ~ ~]>").scale("C4:major").swingBy(1/3, 4).s("vibes")
```

**Skills.** Introduces: none. Practises: R13 Swing & shuffle (needs R05, R08).

## J1.L02 Reading 7th-Chord Symbols

Author: **J1a**. Key F major, 4/4, 116 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** iReal ↔ lead-sheet ↔ jazz glyphs for seventh chords, sixths and sus (∆ – ø °).

**The one idea.** A jazz chart writes the chords you already play with a few new glyphs: ∆ is major seventh, – is minor, ø half-diminished, ° diminished; a symbol tells you the notes. Two symbols are new colours: the sixth chord (C6: the triad plus the 6th, warm and settled) and the sus chord (Csus: the 4th in place of the 3rd, floating until it resolves).

**Sound first.** A four-bar chart in F read aloud by the band: F6, Dm7, Gm7, C7sus resolving to C7, on Rhodes over an upright, swung on the jazz kit. The chart shows glyphs above the staff (F6 D–7 G–7 C7sus).

```js
setcpm(116/4)

const changes = "<F6 Dm7 Gm7 [C7sus C7]>"

drums: s("rd [rd rd] rd [rd rd]").swingBy(1/3, 4).bank("jazzkit")
hats: s("~ hh ~ hh").bank("jazzkit")
chords: chord(changes).voicing().s("rhodes")
bass: chord(changes).rootNotes(2).s("upright")
```

**Skills.** Introduces: J01 Jazz symbols iReal ↔ lead sheet (needs C07, N05); C09 Sus & add chords (needs C02). Practises: none.

**Terms (≤ 3, a pair counts once).** `term.sixth-chord` "sixth chord" in S2: A triad with the 6th above the root added: C6 is C E G A, warm and settled where a major 7th would shimmer. `term.sus-chord` "sus chord" in S4: A chord whose 3rd is replaced by the 4th (sus4) or the 2nd (sus2): it floats, neither major nor minor, until the 3rd comes back.

**Can-do.** `cd.read.jazz-symbols` "I can read jazz chord symbols for sevenths, sixths and sus chords."

**Unlocked here (content/toolbox.ts).** `tb.feature.jazz-glyphs` `chord.sixth-sus`

## J1.L03 The Major ii–V–I

Author: **J1a**. Key C major, 4/4, 132 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** ii7–V7–Imaj7 as one gesture.

**The one idea.** Dm7, G7, Cmaj7: the minor seventh on 2, the dominant seventh on 5, home on 1. Each root falls a 5th, each chord pulls into the next, and the three together are one gesture, the ii–V–I, the sentence most jazz tunes are made of.

**Sound first.** Four bars of ii–V–I in C by the trio (ride, hi-hat, two-feel bass, Rhodes comping, vibes), then the same four bars in F: the same pull, a new home.

```js
setcpm(132/4)

drums: s("rd [rd rd] rd [rd rd]").swingBy(1/3, 4).bank("jazzkit")
```

## J1.L04 Guide-Tone Lines

Author: **J1b**. Key Bb major, 4/4, 120 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** Sing and write the 3rds and 7ths; `dict("guidetones")`.

**The one idea.** Inside a ii–V–I, the 3rd and the 7th of each chord make a thread that moves by a half step or stays: the 7th of ii falls to the 3rd of V, the 7th of V falls to the 3rd of I. **Sound first.** A ii–V–I in B♭ (Cm7 F7 B♭maj7) played only as its guide tones with `.dict("guidetones")`, then with a vibes line that follows the thread: two notes per chord and the changes are unmistakable.
