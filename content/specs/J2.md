# J2 Keys, Minor ii–V & Colour: unit spec

The second unit of the jazz track (contracts §2.6): the ii–V–I the learner comped in J1, taken everywhere a jazz tune takes it. Music first: the same trio moved a 4th higher every four bars walks the cycle of fourths through all twelve keys and home (J2.L01, the exemplar); in a minor key the gesture darkens, a half-diminished ii, a V with one bent note (the ♭9), a minor home (J2.L02); notes stacked above the seventh colour a chord without changing its job, and a long note a half step above a chord tone clashes (J2.L03); and each chord of the ii–V–I has a scale that fits it as a starting choice, so one line of degrees outlines every chord (J2.L04, gated on U14.CP, the one lesson of the track that can be passed over, contracts §7.6). Then J.CP1 checks J1 and J2.L01–L03, and JC1, the first jazz track on the album, is a sixteen-bar ballad over chained ii–Vs with a guide-tone melody. Twelve-key practice is spread over the unit, never a drill wall: each lesson plays its tune fragment in a few keys, each Make is in a new key, and `deck.jzz.ii-v` keeps the twelve alive one card a day.

## Conventions for this unit

- Written for the learner at the gate (contracts §7.6 rule 5, AUTHORING §20.1): J2.L01–L03 may be taken with U14–U17 unfinished, so they use only what U13.CP guarantees plus J1's unlocks and their own. No mode names or `.scale("D4:dorian")` before J2.L04, no chromatic `n` steps (U15.L01: an altered note is a note name, `bb4`, or a chord symbol, `A7b9`), no secondary dominants by name (U15), no `horns` / `trumpet` / `tenor`, no `.pan()`. J2.L04 opens after U14.CP and may use the modes; it never assumes U15–U17. A learner at session 185 reads the same lessons: they never call the core's ideas new.
- The sound of the unit is J1's trio: the jazz kit with the ride pattern on a `drums:` line and the hi-hat on 2 and 4 on `hats:`, Rhodes comping on the Charleston (`.struct("x ~ ~ x ~ ~ ~ ~")`, swung), the upright bass, the vibraphone for melodies. Every loop carries a `bass:` line (`chord(changes).rootNotes(2)` or written by hand); a chord's name comes from its symbol (contracts §6.7), and from its notes only when it is written by hand.
- Swing: `.swingBy(1/3, 4)` on every line that swings, `swing: { subdivision: 8, amount: 1/3 }` declared on the lesson; check windows [1.6, 2.4], Make windows [1.45, 2.4] (J1 conventions). The ballad of J2.L03 and JC1 still swings its ride; a swing rule never reads a quarter-note bass.
- Keys, so that twelve-key practice is spread (pre-flight B1; J1 conventions): J2.L01 tours all twelve once by ear, moves the trio from F to B♭ in one graded step and names A♭'s ii–V–I in another, and its Make asks for three keys from any start; J2.L02 is in D minor (its graded step moves from D major to D minor, its ear step plays the minor cadence in several keys); J2.L03 in E♭; J2.L04 in G. J.CP1 asks for E major, G minor and A♭ (keys no lesson drilled), JC1 for at least three keys of the learner's choice. No lesson asks for twelve keys in one session; `deck.jzz.ii-v` (J1.L03; the other nine major keys from J2.L01, the minor ii–V–i from J2.L02) keeps them alive one card a day once the drills engine writes it (content/decks/planned.ts).
- Moving a whole trio is `.transpose()` (U02.L05) on every line, with a number pattern (`const tour = "<0!4 5!4 -2!4>"`): five half steps up is the next key round the cycle; 12 off keeps the trio in its register. It moves hand-written lines (note names, J1.L05's shells) as one shape. A `chord(...).voicing()` line is NOT moved this way in this unit: `.transpose()` before `.voicing()` does nothing to a symbol (evaluated: Strudel logs "transpose: not a note" and plays the untransposed chord) and after it the symbol still names the old key (§6.7 reads the symbol). Charts by name are written out key by key.
- Chord symbols (STYLE §5.1): code `Em7b5 A7b9 A7#9 A7alt Dm^7 Dm6 Fm9 Bb13 Eb^9 Fm11`; prose Em7♭5 (Eø7 in the jazz glyphs), A7♭9, Dm(maj7), Fm9, B♭13, E♭maj9. Evaluated in the default dictionary (`ireal`): `A7b9` → G B♭ C♯ G A (the ♭9 is B♭, the note D minor already has), `A7#9` → A C♯ G C, `A7alt` → G C♯ E♭ A C, `Dm^7` → D A C♯ F A. `lefthand` voices `A7b9` loosely (C♯ F G B♭: a ♭13 in place of the 5th), has no `Dm^7` (it spells the minor-major seventh `DmM7`) and is silent on `A7alt`: J2.L02 comps with the default dictionary. `ireal-ext` (J2.L03) voices `Fm9` → A♭ E♭ G A♭ C (rootless), `Bb13` → D A♭ D G C, `Eb^9` → E♭ D F G C (it adds the 13th to a maj9): a step that compares pitch classes compares against a reference in the same dictionary.
- J2.L02 introduces ONE altered dominant, the ♭9 (pre-flight B1, contracts 1.8): the V7♭9 is the minor key's own V (its ♭9 is the key's 6th, B♭ in D minor). ♯9 and alt are heard and named in J2.L03's comparison step, never required before it. `chord.alt` unlocks all five symbols at J2.L02, so the lint accepts `A7#9` there; the lesson does not ask for it.
- Avoid notes and chord-scales are starting choices, not laws (pre-flight B2): the rules count only STRUCTURAL notes (on a beat, or a beat long or more; `AVAILABLE_TENSIONS` in @oak/content-kit), so passing notes and a suspension that resolves are free. The prose says "clashes when it is held" and "a scale that fits, to start with", never "wrong" or "forbidden".
- Grading (AUTHORING §20.2, the corrections-pass standard): every required rule checks what is HEARD. For every graded step: the reference, at least three other right answers, the unchanged starter, and at least one WRONG answer that uses the right function (a tour that moves to G; a symbol that is a seventh where the chart is a ninth; the ♭9 played off the beat for an eighth; `.scale("A4:minor")` where the chord wants Dorian; the right names on a silent `.gain(0)` line).

## J2.L01 All Twelve Keys

Author: **spec-J**. Key C major, 4/4, 152 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** ii–V–I around the cycle of fourths.

**The one idea.** A ii–V–I in a new key is the same shape moved: every note of the trio goes up the same distance. Move it up a 4th, five half steps, and you are in the next key round the cycle of fourths (C, F, B♭, E♭ …); twelve moves visit every key and bring you home. In code the move is one number pattern, `.transpose(tour)`, on every line.

**Sound first.** J1's trio (ride, hi-hat, shells on the Charleston, a two-feel bass) with a vibes lick over Dm7 G7 Cmaj7, then the same four bars a 4th higher, and again, round all twelve keys: within thirty seconds the learner has heard it come home in C, F, B♭, E♭ and A♭.

```js
setcpm(152/4)

const tour = "<0!4 5!4 -2!4 3!4 8!4 1!4 6!4 -1!4 4!4 -3!4 2!4 7!4>"

drums: s("rd [rd rd] rd [rd rd]").swingBy(1/3, 4).bank("jazzkit")
hats: s("~ hh ~ hh").bank("jazzkit")
chords: note("<[d3,f3,c4] [g2,f3,b3] [c3,e3,b3] [c3,e3,b3]>").transpose(tour).struct("x ~ ~ x ~ ~ ~ ~").swingBy(1/3, 4).s("rhodes").gain(.7)
bass: note("<[d2 a1] [g1 d2] [c2 g1] [c2 e2]>").transpose(tour).s("upright")
mel: note("<[[d4 f4] [a4 c5] [a4 f4] [d4 e4]] [[f4 d4] [b3 d4] g4 [b4 f4]] [e4 ~ [g4 b4] ~] [[c5 b4] g4 e4 ~]>").transpose(tour).swingBy(1/3, 4).s("vibes")
```

## J2.L02 Minor ii–V–i

Author: **J2a**. Key D minor, 4/4, 112 BPM.

**Objective (syllabus).** iiø7–V7alt–i; ♭9, ♯9 and alt introduced in context.

**The one idea.** In a minor key the ii–V–I darkens: the ii is half-diminished (Em7♭5, Eø7), the V keeps its leading tone but bends its 9th down to the key's own 6th (A7♭9, with B♭ in D minor), and home is minor. The same pull, in shadow: the minor ii–V–i.

**Sound first.** The trio in D minor: Em7♭5, A7♭9, Dm7, a vibes line that leans on the B♭ over the V and falls to A, then F, then home; then the same four bars in D major (Em7 A7 Dmaj7): the same gesture in light.

```js
const changes = "<Em7b5 A7b9 Dm7 Dm7>"

drums: s("rd [rd rd] rd [rd rd]").swingBy(1/3, 4).bank("jazzkit")
hats: s("~ hh ~ hh").bank("jazzkit")
chords: chord(changes).voicing().struct("x ~ ~ x ~ ~ ~ ~").swingBy(1/3, 4).s("rhodes")
bass: chord(changes).rootNotes(2).s("upright")

```

**Skills.** Introduces: J02 Extensions & alterations (needs J01); J06 Minor iiø–V7alt–i (needs J04, J02, S07). Practises: none.

## JC1 ii–V Ballad

Author: **capstones-jazz-a**. **What counts as a chained ii–V.** The `ii-v-chain` rule (planned, `TRACK_CUSTOM_RULES`) reads the chords from their symbols (or from the notes of hand-written shells) and counts the distinct keys the ii–Vs aim at.

- A ii–V is a minor seventh (or m7♭5) followed by a dominant seventh whose root is a 4th above it (Dm7 → G7, Em7♭5 → A7). Its target is the key a 5th below the V (C for G7), whether or not the I arrives: a chain of ii–Vs that never lands (Em7 A7 Dm7 G7 …) aims at a key with every pair, which is how standards move.
- Keys are counted by tonic: a ii–V into C major and one into C minor (Dm7♭5 G7♭9) aim at one tonic, C.
- At least three distinct targets in the sixteen bars passes (`minKeys: 3`); every pair counts toward `minPairs` (default `minKeys`). The I chords, turnarounds and a free last bar are the learner's.
- Examples that pass: Em7 A7 D^7 D^7 | Dm7 G7 C^7 C^7 | Cm7 F7 Bb^7 Bb^7 | Em7b5 A7b9 Dm7 Dm7 (D, C, B♭ and D minor: whole steps down, then home); Dm7 G7 C^7 C^7 | Gm7 C7 F^7 F^7 | Cm7 F7 Bb^7 Bb^7 | Fm7 Bb7 Eb^7 Eb^7 (round the cycle, J2.L01). Fails: three ii–Vs all into C (one key); two keys over sixteen bars.

**What stays the learner's.**

- The keys (any three or more, major or minor), their order, and whether a ii–V lands on its I or chains on.
- The tempo, 56–92; swung or straight eighths (the brief declares light swing; a straight ballad passes every rule).
- The voicings: shells by hand (J1.L05), `.dict("lefthand")`, `.dict("guidetones")`, extensions (`Dm9`, `G13`, J2.L03), or the default; the comping rhythm.
- The melody's rhythm, register and sound; what it does between the chord changes. Only its note AT each change is asked to be a 3rd or a 7th, moving by a step or less from the last.
- Form inside the sixteen bars (AABA in fours, two eights, one long line) and an ending.
