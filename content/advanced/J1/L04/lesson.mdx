<Step id="S1">
You can hear a ii–V–I go by. A pianist can say one with two notes a chord. Here is a ii–V–I in B♭, Cm7, F7, B♭maj7 (`Bb^7` in code), with a Rhodes that plays almost nothing.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>While the chords change under it, the Rhodes' top note hardly moves: B♭ in bar 1, then A, a half step lower, for three bars. In the second loop the vibes start every bar on that note, and the tune follows the chords along it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Which two notes are they? Here they are held a bar each, so the roll shows them standing still or sliding.

<Example id="held" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The 3rd and the 7th, the guide tones you followed through a chain of sevenths earlier in the course. B♭, the 7th of Cm7, falls one half step to A, the 3rd of F7; A stays to become the 7th of B♭maj7. Under it runs a second thread that moves just as little.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Sing the top thread and you hold the whole ii–V–I together with one note a bar.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-thread" />

<OnPass>Do, ti, ti, ti: the chords moved three times under you, and your note moved once, by a half step.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Under the note you sang runs a lower thread.

<Editor exercise="the-other-thread" />

<OnPass>E♭, E♭, D, D: the 3rd of Cm7 stays as the 7th of F7, then falls a half step to the 3rd of B♭maj7. You now have both threads, and between them they spell every chord.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The first loop of this lesson kept only the threads with one call. Here the Rhodes is back to full chords.

<Editor exercise="only-the-thread" />

<OnPass>Two notes a chord, and the ii–V–I is still unmistakable. The guide-tone dictionary is a quick way to hear the threads of any progression, and a sparse, open comp under a busy tune.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The vibes here start each bar on its root, which says nothing the bass has not already said. The thread you sang is a better place to land. In your song this loop is the first half of both bridges, where the music visits B♭, with Oak's drums under it.

<Make exercise="a-tune-on-the-thread" />

<CanDo id="cd.sing.guide-tones" />

You can now sing and write the guide tones through a ii–V–I, and build a tune that outlines the chords by itself. Next: a root under these two notes makes a shell, the voicing jazz pianists comp with.

<Recap />
</Step>
