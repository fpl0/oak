<Step id="S1">
The trio keeps the swing pocket you built and reads a chart in F. Most of the chart you already play, printed with other marks. Two of its chords are colours you have not written yet.

<Example id="chart-in-f" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the first take bar 1 sounds warm and full, and bar 4 hangs for two beats before it leans home. In the second, bar 1 is a bare triad and bar 4 leans from its first beat. Those two colours are what this lesson adds.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
A jazz chart writes the chords you know with a few glyphs: ∆ for a major seventh, – for minor, ø for half-diminished, ° for diminished. A symbol still tells you every note.

<Example id="the-chart" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>D–7 is Dm7: the dash is the m. F6 plays four letters, the F major triad F A C plus D, the 6th above F. That is a **sixth chord**, warm and settled where a major 7th would shimmer.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Read the glyphs again, this time without the chord band's help.

<Choice exercise="glyphs-into-code" />

<OnPass>The triangle, the dash, the slashed circle and the plain circle: a chart's sevenths read into code.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The floating chord from bar 4, on its own: the same G–7, then a chord on C, in two versions.

<Choice exercise="float-or-lean" />

<OnPass>Card A plays C7sus: the 4th above C, F, sits where the 3rd, E, would be. With no 3rd the chord is neither bright nor dark, and it floats until the 3rd comes back. That is a **sus chord**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Play from the page: the chart is all you get, in glyphs only.

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>G–7, C7, F∆7, F6: four glyphs read into four chords, the last one settling on its 6th.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
At the start of this lesson a jazz chart was a page of marks to decode. Now you read it as chords, and you have two new colours to write with. The chart is yours: in your song it plays the second half of both bridges, where the music visits F before the tune comes back in C, with Oak's drums under it.
