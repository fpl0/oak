<Step id="S1">
The two guide-tone threads need one more note to become a pianist's left hand. First the rhythm section with the piano chair empty, then with the pianist sitting down.

<Example id="shell-trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The left hand plays short chords on beat 1 and the and of 2. The trio sounds complete, yet it stays open, and the bass and the ride keep their room.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Count what the left hand plays, starting from the lowest note.

<Example id="shells-held" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three notes: in bar 2, G at the bottom, then F, the 7th, and B, the 3rd. The 5th is gone, and nothing is lost: the root says which chord, the 3rd and the 7th say what kind. That is a **shell voicing**, the kind jazz pianists comp with. Above the roots run the two guide-tone threads from last lesson.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
A shell works on any seventh chord, including one that is not in the loop.

<OnPass>F, A and E: the root, the 3rd and the 7th of Fmaj7, with C, the 5th, left out. Either order of the top two is a shell.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The dictionary's voicings fill the middle of the keyboard. Your shells will open it up.

<Editor exercise="shells-by-hand" />

<OnPass>Your own shells, comping on beat 1 and the and of 2. Each chord is three notes, and the trio sounds more open for it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
How a backing like this one is made. One chord string feeds two parts. For the bass, `rootNotes` takes each chord's root. For the vibes, `n` before `.set(chord(changes))` plays the voicing's notes one at a time, lowest first, so `n` 0 is the lowest note of each chord. The shells stay notes you wrote yourself. Read it; nothing to change.

</Step>
