<Step id="S1">
You can swing a line of hats and voice a seventh chord. A jazz trio does both at once. Here one plays twice, and only the drummer's cymbal changes.

<Example id="trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the first take the cymbal adds a short, late hit after beats 2 and 4, and the time lifts and rolls forward. In the second the comping and the vibes still swing, but the cymbal only counts, and the trio loses its lift.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Here is the cymbal's line on its own, with the swing call you met in the blues.

<Predict exercise="which-hits-move" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Check your guess on the roll, where every hit sits against the beat lines.

<Example id="the-ride" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Six hits a bar. The extra hit inside beats 2 and 4 waits until two thirds of the way through its beat, so each pair is long, then short: ding, ding-a.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Back to the trio from the first take, its cymbal on plain beats.

<Editor exercise="ride-pattern" />

<OnPass>Ding, ding-a, ding, ding-a: that is the **ride pattern**, the swing drummer's timekeeping on the ride cymbal.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Under the cymbal, the drummer's foot closes the hi-hat on two beats of every bar. Find them by ear.

<Tap exercise="snap-two-and-four" />

<OnPass>Two and four: the beats a swing drummer's foot closes the hi-hat on, and the beats the whole band leans toward. They are the backbeat you know from rock, played here by the hi-hat instead of the snare.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The hi-hat line in the code is still empty.

<Editor exercise="hats-two-and-four" />

<OnPass>Cymbal on top, hi-hat on 2 and 4: the swing pocket the rest of the trio sits in.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
You came in with one swing call. You leave with a jazz drummer's whole pocket: the cymbal's lilt and the foot on 2 and 4. The band plays bass and chords, and the drum chair is yours, starting from four plain beats.
