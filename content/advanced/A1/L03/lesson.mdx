<Step id="S1">
Filters changed what each note of your loop is made of. This lesson changes how each note moves through time: how fast it starts, how it falls, when it stops.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In loop 2 each bass note hits and dies away at once, so the low end goes quiet between notes. Each riff note fades in and rings on past its end. The waves and the filters stayed. What changed is how each note's loudness moves through time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The scope draws loudness as the height of the wave. Watch one note go through its whole life.

<Example id="four-stages" views="code" />

<OnPass>Attack: 0.3 seconds to reach full level. Decay: 0.2 seconds to fall to the sustain level, half. Sustain: that level, held while the note lasts. Release: 0.8 seconds to die away after it ends. Those four stages are the note's **envelope**. With `.sustain(0)` the note would die away during the decay, even while it is still held.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The kick and the bass share the bottom of the sound, and only one of them needs to ring there. A bass that stops on its own leaves that room to the kick.

<Editor exercise="pluck-the-bass" />

<OnPass>A pluck: each bass note hits, falls to nothing in a fifth of a second and gets out of the way. Set only `.decay()` and the sustain falls to nothing on its own, the same pluck.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the opposite shape. On longer notes, a slow start and a long tail turn a square wave into something like a pad.

<Editor exercise="swell-the-riff" />

<OnPass>Each riff note swells in over 0.3 seconds and rings for 0.8 after it ends, so the notes overlap and blur together. Set only `.attack()` and `.release()` and the note holds at full level in between.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Listen for the same two edges as in the opening, now with no code to read.

<Choice exercise="start-or-tail" />

<OnPass>The attack lives at the front edge of each note, the release in the silence after it. A singer or a violinist shapes those same two edges on every note.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Three side lessons in, you choose each note's harmonics, its filter and now its shape in time. A shape can move the pitch too: `.penv()` starts each note higher and `.pdecay()` sets how fast it falls back. A kick drum is a low sine that falls two octaves in a blink, with a fast pluck on its loudness; `sbd` is Oak's ready-made synth kick, a wave whose pitch falls the same way. Bar 1 plays the sine kick, bar 2 `sbd`. <CodeRef fn="penv" />

<Example id="synth-kick" views="code" />

In your song, this loop plays the part right after the first loud one, where Oak's bass and chords play short, plucked notes. A punchy part and a floating part sit well together, so try a different shape on each; a sine kick with its own loudness pluck, as in bar 1, counts as a shape too.
