<Step id="S1">
Until now each note of your loop did the same thing every time it played: open, close, ring, echo. This lesson adds movement that goes on while a note is held. Here is the loop from Space with a held pad of chords and a long-note lead in place of the riff.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In loop 2 the lead's pitch wavers a little above and below each note, the way a singer's does, and the pad slowly brightens and darkens. Nothing new plays. A held sound that never changes soon fades into the background; one that moves keeps the ear's attention.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
A machine holds a note perfectly still; a singer never does. Here is one new call on one held note. <CodeRef fn="vib" />

<Predict exercise="one-long-g" />

<Step id="S3">
A wobble has two dials: how fast it goes, and how far it moves.

<Example id="how-fast" views="code" />

<OnPass>Six wobbles a second in bar 2, and the same small width in both bars. A small, regular wobble in a note's pitch is **vibrato**, as a singer or a violinist adds it. `.vib()` sets how often, `.vibmod()` how far, in semitones each way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The lead in the opening's loop 2 sang like this. Too fast and a vibrato buzzes; too wide and it sounds seasick.

<Editor exercise="match-the-vibrato" />

<OnPass>The lead's long notes sing now. Many singers and players save their vibrato for held notes. Here every note gets it, and the long ones show it most.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The pitch is one thing a slow wobble can move. Here are three others, one bar each. <CodeRef fn="tremolosync" />

<Example id="three-moves" views="code" />

<OnPass>Four dips in bar 2, one with every beat: that is tremolo. In bar 1 a phaser sweeps the colour; in bar 3 the cutoff opens and closes. Each time, a slow wave, too slow to hear as a pitch, pushes one setting up and down. That slow wave is an **LFO**, short for low-frequency oscillator, and vibrato is an LFO on the pitch.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No code this time. A held chord gives a slow movement the most room to be heard.

<Choice exercise="what-moves" />

<OnPass>The brightness: an LFO on the cutoff. The notes and the level stayed put while the top of the sound opened and closed. The pad in the opening's loop 2 moved the same way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
An LFO glides: it gives every chord a new cutoff. A setting can also move in steps, holding one value for a while and then jumping, so the movement keeps time with the drums.

<Editor exercise="steps-not-sweeps" />

<OnPass>The brightness now holds, then jumps, then holds again: the movement has a rhythm of its own, a second groove on top of the notes. A glide and a step can move the same setting, and they sound nothing alike.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S9">
A player never strikes two chords exactly alike. A slow random wave can give your parts that small drift. <CodeRef fn="perlin" />

<Example id="drift" views="code" />

<OnPass>No two chords were quite the same length or brightness, yet nothing sounded wrong. A narrow drift sounds like a steady hand; a wide one, a loose one. A little of it keeps a loop that repeats for minutes from sounding stamped out.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
In your song, this loop plays both build-ups before the choruses: there Oak's chords pulse while their cutoff climbs for four bars, a pad swells in, the tune holds long notes with a vibrato, and a noise riser climbs under it all. Oak's chords keep playing under your pad. One more thing can move: the wave itself. `wt_morph` is a wavetable of many shapes in a row; `.wtrate()` sweeps through them that many times a second and `.wtdepth()` sets how far, so the pad changes colour while it holds. <CodeRef fn="wt" />

<Example id="morph" views="code" />

The checklist requires vibrato, a phaser, a tremolo or a filter sweep; a wavetable sweep is on it as an extra, so pair it with one of them. Choose how fast by ear: slow movement breathes, fast movement shimmers. A movement counted in the bar, like `.tremolosync()`, locks to the drums the way your timed echoes did in Space.
