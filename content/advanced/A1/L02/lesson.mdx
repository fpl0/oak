<Step id="S3">
A sawtooth's harmonics climb all the way up: 1, 2, 3, 4 … times its note. The low-pass draws a line among them.

<Example id="what-goes-first" views="code" />

<OnPass>The top goes first. Harmonics above the number are cut back, more the further above it they sit, so the riff darkens and its wave loses its corners. A tool that takes some harmonics out of a sound is a **filter**, and the number where it starts to cut is its **cutoff**. `.lpf()` is a low-pass filter; `.hpf()`, a high-pass, does the opposite and takes away the lows.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The opening's second loop had a bass like this one: low and round. Find its cutoff by ear.

<Editor exercise="match-the-bass" />

<OnPass>A round bass: only its lowest few harmonics get through, so it sits under the riff instead of buzzing beside it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A filter can do more than cut. Listen to what happens right at the cutoff as the peak grows. <CodeRef fn="lpq" />

<Editor exercise="make-it-ring" />

<OnPass>That whistle is **resonance**: a peak the filter adds at its cutoff, so the harmonics near the cutoff ring out. Move the cutoff and the peak moves with it. A little resonance gives a bass or a riff a vocal edge; a lot sounds like a synth solo.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
So far the cutoff stood still. In the opening's second loop it moved on every riff note: each one started bright and closed down. <CodeRef fn="lpenv" />

<Editor exercise="pluck-the-riff" />

<OnPass>Four octaves above 300 is 4800. Each note opens there and falls back to 300 in a fifth of a second. Without `.lpd()` it falls back in about 0.14 seconds, a slightly snappier pluck. That jump and fall on every note is the pluck of a synth riff. The next sound lesson gives the same shape to a note's loudness.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
No code and no picture this time: only your ear, and a cutoff that moves once.

<Choice exercise="brighter-or-darker" />

<OnPass>You heard the cutoff move without seeing a number. That is how a filter gets set in practice, by ear, against the other parts. Oak brings this question back in your warm-ups.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
Last lesson you added harmonics to this loop. Now you can take them away again, make the cutoff ring, and move it on every note. Two sounds of dance music are built from these tools and two more that open here. An acid bass is a sawtooth under a ladder filter, `.ftype("ladder")`, with a high resonance and an `.lpenv()`: each note squelches open and shut. A riser is noise, every frequency at once (`s("pink")`, or `white` and `brown`), under a low-pass whose cutoff climbs: here `.lpa()` makes the cutoff take the whole of bar 2 to open. <CodeRef fn="ftype" />

<Example id="acid-and-riser" views="code" />

In your song, this loop plays the first loud part, where Oak's bass (in jazz-soul, its Rhodes) already squelches open on every note. A sawtooth gives a low-pass the most to cut, so the bass is the place to try the ladder.
