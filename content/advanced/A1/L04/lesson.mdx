<Step id="S2">
You have put a tune in a room with `.room()` before. The other half of space is an echo, and its time is set in seconds. <CodeRef fn="delaytime" />

<Step id="S3">
An echo timed to the music lands on the same grid as the notes: a beat later, or half a beat, or a quarter. An echo at a random time tends to blur the rhythm.

<Example id="half-a-beat" views="code" />

<OnPass>Half of 0.6 is 0.3, so each echo lands half a beat after its note and the riff's rests fill with echoes. Echoes of a sound repeating after a set time are a **delay**. `.delay()` sets how much of the part goes to it, `.delaytime()` when the echoes come. In bar 2 they keep coming for longer.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The riff's echo in the opening came half a beat late and faded after a few repeats. Set one like it by ear: when it comes, and how long it keeps going.

<Editor exercise="match-the-echo" />

<OnPass>Half a beat later, two or three repeats, then gone. How much of each echo goes back into the delay is its **feedback**. At 0.3 each repeat comes back at 0.3 times the level of the one before; at 0.7 they hang on for a long time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
One more rule of space in Strudel: parts on the same orbit share one reverb and one delay. Each part can send more or less into them, but the room size and the echo time belong to the orbit. <CodeRef fn="orbit" />

<Editor exercise="two-rooms" />

<OnPass>Two orbits, two rooms: the snare keeps its big hall and the riff its small one, and neither cuts the other's tail. Give a part its own orbit whenever it needs a space the others do not share.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The opening put both kinds of space in one loop. Here you get one at a time, by ear alone.

<Choice exercise="room-or-echoes" />

<OnPass>A room sends back thousands of reflections, too close together to hear one by one, so they blur into a wash. A delay sends back one copy at a time, far enough apart to hear each.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
In your song, this loop fills the quiet part where Oak's drums stop: there Oak's tune plays slow notes into a big room, with echoes three sixteenths later (0.45 seconds at 100 BPM), on an orbit of its own. Place each part of the dry loop: what is near, what is far, what echoes. Keep the plucked bass dry, since a room on the low end fills the gap you cleared for the kick in Envelopes. Time the echoes to the beat so they move with the drums, and give a part its own orbit if it needs a different space.
