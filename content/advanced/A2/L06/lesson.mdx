<Step id="S1">
Every lesson of this unit kept the A minor tune and changed one thing around it: height, density, a second line, a chopped break, level. This one changes everything around it at once. Two new sounds help: a `kalimba`, a thumb piano, plays the ballad's tune, and a `marimba`, struck wooden bars, plays the drive's chords.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The second half is built for dancing. Its kick lands on every beat and its bass gets busier, so your body finds the pulse at once. The first half leaves long gaps for the tune to breathe in.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The piece is two sections, as in your verse-and-chorus songs, but here the second section is not a chorus: it is the first one in other clothes.

<Example id="two-sections" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Eight bass notes a bar where there was one, and the tune untouched. A style is a set of habits: which drums and where they hit, how busy the bass is, which sounds, how long the notes ring. Those habits are its **idiom**. Change them and keep the tune, and the song changes style. Sounds carry habits too: a kalimba sounds intimate and close, a marimba's short wooden notes are a habit of sunny dance pop, and a `glockenspiel`, small metal bars, sparkles an octave over a tune.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Some habits define a style more than others. Dance music hangs on its kick.

<Editor exercise="drive-the-drums" />

<OnPass>Four on the floor: a kick on every beat, the first habit of a driving dance track. Disco made it a dance-floor habit, and house and techno kept it. The ballad above kept its single kick, and the tune did not move.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
New habits can hide a tune, or hide that it changed. Follow the top line through both halves.

<Choice exercise="same-tune" />

<OnPass>A changed tune: bar 5 starts upward where bar 1 starts downward, and the last bar ends lower. To arrange is to change everything around a tune and leave the tune itself alone.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Some habits live in the harmony. House and deep house have a favourite: a pad held over one low note that never moves.

<Example id="house-pad" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Alone, C E G B sounds bright, a chord on C. With the A under it, the same four notes turn soft and dark: you now hear one minor chord on A, A C E, with G and B stacked above it. The bass names the chord. One held low note turns a plain pad into the warm, hanging sound of a deep house record.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">

Any tool of this track can be one of the habits: a register, a busier bass, a shorter envelope, a sound from Grit or FM & Supersaw, a kalimba, a marimba or a glockenspiel.
