<Step id="S1">
Counter-Melody gave the loop a second tune of its own. Here two sounds play one tune instead: they share its notes between them.

<Example id="hocket" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Loop 2 is still one tune: the notes are the same and in the same places. What changes is the colour, which jumps from the square to the piano and back on every note, so the line sounds lit from two sides.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
When two lines share one tune, each note sits in one line and the other line rests in its place.

<Predict exercise="whose-notes" />

<OnPass>Read the two lines together, slot by slot: where `mel` rests, `vox` plays. Here they pass the tune back and forth in pairs.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
A shared tune can break in a few ways. This version has one.

<Example id="broken" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>In bar 3, `vox` plays the first note too, together with `mel`. For that moment the tune is doubled and the colour blurs, so the hand-over is lost.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The whole tune is on `mel`, and `vox` is empty. Share the tune between them.

<Editor exercise="share-the-line" />

<OnPass>One tune, two sounds, each note played once. Splitting a line between sounds note by note, each playing where the other rests, is a **hocket**. Trading whole bars would be call and response; here the hand-over happens inside every bar, so the ear hears one line that keeps changing colour.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
In a hocket the colour jumps from note to note of a moving line. Here only the colour moves: a slow tune, every repeat in another sound.

<Choice exercise="a-tune-of-colours" />

<OnPass>Version A changes sound on every note, and even a repeated note seems to move: the colours make a tune of their own. A sound-colour melody can go further: one sound fades out as the next fades in.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now the loop is back, with the tune on `mel` and an empty `vox`. Choose your own turns. A `vox` that switches between two sounds of its own, or a split that changes in the last bar, are ideas to try, not rules.

<Make exercise="your-own-split" />
