<Step id="S1">
Texture & Density made a section fuller by adding notes. A second line is another way to fill it. Here is the A minor loop of Roles & Registers with one new part, a strings line under the tune.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Without the strings, the tune's pauses go empty and the loop sounds plainer. In loop 1 the strings speak mostly while the tune rests. The next steps look at exactly when they move, and which way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
A second line can fill the music or blur into the first. Where it starts its notes decides which.

<Predict exercise="which-line-stands-out" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Two lines on the roll, the tune on top and the strings under it.

<Example id="two-lines" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>In bars 2 to 4 the strings start a note while the tune is silent, so you hear them take a turn. In bar 1 they climb while the tune steps down. A second tune that moves against the main one like this, filling its pauses and going the other way when both move, is a **counter-melody**. Lines that move at different times and in different directions are what let the ear follow two at once.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The strings have nothing to say in bar 4. Give them an answer to the tune.

<Editor exercise="answer-in-bar-4" />

<OnPass>The tune says B, the strings answer; the tune says G, the strings answer again. One line holds while the other moves, and the bar sounds like two players listening to each other.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Not every second line is a counter-melody. Listen to this one against the tune.

<Choice exercise="its-own-tune" />

<OnPass>The tune again, an octave down. Doubling a tune makes it thicker and stronger, which is sometimes what a chorus wants, but the ear hears one line, not two.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
For a longer session. A counter-line needs room in the middle of the sound, and close chords fill that room. Arrangers often spread the chords in one section to make space.

<Editor exercise="spread-the-chorus" />

<OnPass>The chorus's chords now reach from low to high with the middle open: in bars 1 and 4 the strings no longer land on the piano's own notes, and they stand out more. Changing how the chords are stacked from one section to the next is a tool you will use again when one tune changes style.</OnPass>
</Step>
