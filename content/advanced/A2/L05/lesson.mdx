<Step id="S1">
Roles & Registers gave each part its own height. Height is one way to give a part its place; how loud it plays and which side it comes from are two more. Here is the A minor loop with a strings pad added.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In loop 2 the tune is on top. The chords and the pad play quieter than it, one a little left and one a little right, so nothing stands in front of it. In loop 1 they were louder than the tune and all in the middle. No note changed; only levels and places did.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Your parts already have their own heights. How loud each one plays decides what the ear follows first.

</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The two controls of where a part sits: how loud, and from which side. <CodeRef fn="pan" />

<Example id="levels-and-sides" views="code" />

<OnPass>Two halvings, so 12 dB down. Bars 1 and 3 come from the left, 2 and 4 from the right. Level, side and the room around each part together make the **mix**, the balance of everything that plays.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The fault from the predict step, now yours to fix.

<Editor exercise="put-the-tune-on-top" />

<OnPass>The tune leads and the chords sit under it, still there. A few dB is enough. Far less and the parts fight; far more and the chords drop out of hearing. `.postgain()` sets a part's level after its effects, the same way. <CodeRef fn="postgain" /></OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A line with loud and soft notes jumps out and falls back. One control evens it out as it plays. <CodeRef fn="compressor" />

<Choice exercise="which-sits-steadier" />

<OnPass>Card B: each loud note is turned down as it sounds, so the line sits steadier and closer. That is **compression**. Nearly every vocal on a pop record is compressed, so its quiet words stay as close as its loud ones. The levels you set with `.gain()` still decide which part leads.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now a balance fault with no code to read. Trust the first thing your ear lands on.

<Choice exercise="what-is-too-loud" />

<OnPass>The bass: low and loud, it fills the bottom and pushes everything else back. A part too loud for its role covers the others, whatever its register.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
Level is one way to put a part in front. Change is another: the ear follows what keeps changing and lets what repeats sink behind it. Here the tune changes every bar over one chord, and the chords and the bass repeat.

<Editor exercise="front-middle-back" />

<OnPass>The chords stood out for one bar and sank back. The part that changes most sits in front, steadier parts in the middle, and parts that barely change at the back: the **foreground, middle ground, background** of a piece. A background part that steps forward once, for a bar, is heard as an event, not as a new lead.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
You can now place a part three ways: by height, by level and by side. Here every part sits at one level, all from the middle: a stack, not yet a balance. Your mix plays the last chorus of your song, where the whole band plays and a tune is easiest to lose. The orbits you met in Space help too: with the pad on `.orbit(2)`, `.duckorbit(2)` on the tune dips the pad each time the tune plays. <CodeRef fn="duckorbit" />
