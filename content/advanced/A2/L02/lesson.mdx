<Step id="S1">
In Verse & Chorus you wrote a song in two sections with `arrange()`. Here the A minor loop of Roles & Registers becomes two of them, a verse and a chorus, with the same tune in both.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>More of everything around the tune, and a new sound high above it. The chorus feels bigger, though the tune and the tempo did not change. The next steps count what changed.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Arrangers rarely change the tune to make a chorus bigger. They change how much plays around it. Here is that change inside a single bar.

<Predict exercise="bigger-or-faster" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The form map draws the two sections; the roll draws every note in them. Look at both while it plays.

<Example id="verse-and-chorus" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The verse has three parts: the bass once a bar, two-note chords, the tune. The chorus keeps them, plays them more often, and adds a fourth, a strings pad high above the tune. How many parts play at once, and how they fit together, is the **texture**. Here it is a tune over a bass and chords, thin in the verse and thick in the chorus.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A full bar and a loud bar can pass for each other. Hear the same chord played two ways.

<Choice exercise="louder-or-fuller" />

<OnPass>Card B: four notes, four times a bar, sixteen sounds where card A has two. How many notes sound in a bar is the **density**. It sits beside texture: one counts the notes, the other the parts. Card A was louder and still thinner, because turning a part down never thins it; playing fewer notes does.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A chorus can only land if the verse leaves it somewhere to go. Here it has nowhere.

<Editor exercise="thin-the-verse" />

<OnPass>Now the verse leaves space and the chorus fills it, though not one note of the tune changed. That is most of what an arranger does between a verse and a chorus.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
There are two ways to thin a section, and they feel different. Here is one of them, by ear alone.

<Choice exercise="thinner-how" />

<OnPass>Every part was still there; the bass and the piano each played fewer notes. Fewer notes keeps the colour of a section and opens space in it. A missing part changes the colour itself.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Last lesson placed each part by height; this one decides how much each part plays, and when. Your chorus plays the first chorus of your song, straight after your verse from Roles & Registers, so fill the chorus rather than thin the verse. A part that waits for the chorus lands harder in a register of its own, as the strings did above the tune.

<Make exercise="let-the-chorus-land" />
