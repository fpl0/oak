<Step id="S1">
The sound lessons shaped one part at a time. Arranging decides how the parts share the space, and the first thing they share is height. Here is a loop in A minor: a bass, piano chords and a tune, with the band on drums.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In loop 1 the chords sit in the bass's octave, and the bottom blurs so that no low note stands out. In loop 2 you can follow the bass, the chords and the tune at once. When a loop sounds muddy, two of its parts are usually playing in the same place.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Every part of a song has a job: the bass holds up the chords, the chords fill the middle, the tune sings over both. Here one chord plays alone, at three heights.

<Predict exercise="which-is-least-clear" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
In Waves & Harmonics you counted how many times a second a note repeats. Low notes repeat slowly, high notes fast. This panel lays the whole sound out by that number, slow on the left and fast on the right, and draws a peak wherever a note and its harmonics sit. <CodeRef fn="fscope" />

<Example id="one-at-a-time" views="code" />

<OnPass>The bass starts at the far left, the chords further right, the tune further still: each part has its own stretch of the panel. A sound laid out from low to high like this is its **spectrum**. Two parts whose peaks land in the same place get in each other's way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Two notes three half steps apart sound sweet in the middle of the keyboard. Hear what happens to the same two notes further down.

<Example id="same-gap" views="code" />

<OnPass>Three half steps in every bar, and only the lowest bar turns to a rumble. Low down, two close notes are near enough in speed that the ear hears them beat against each other instead of as two notes. Arrangers keep close chords out of the bass's octave for that reason, and spread them out or lift them up instead.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The chords are out of the bass's way now, but the tune plays at their height. Two parts in the same place mask each other: the ear follows the fuller one and loses the other.

<Editor exercise="lift-the-tune" />

<OnPass>The tune rides above the chords, and you can follow it again. How high or low a part sits is its **register**: the bass in the low register, the chords in the middle, the tune above.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now find the crowding by ear alone, with no code to read.

<Choice exercise="who-crowds-the-bass" />

<OnPass>The tune: one note at a time, moving against the bass in its own octave. A part in another part's register competes with it, whatever its sound.</OnPass>
</Step>
