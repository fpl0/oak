<Step id="S1">
The blues taught you to swing eighth notes. This neo-soul groove swings something smaller, the sixteenths, and how far it swings them is a choice.

<Example id="light-lean" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the first loop each pair of sixteenths is a little long, then a little short, and the groove sits back. Spaced evenly, the same notes turn stiff. The third loop starts even and leans a little more each bar, until bar 4 has the triplet bounce of the blues.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Before any numbers, put the long and the short in your hands. Swung eighths first: their pairs are wider and easier to feel.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-swing" />

<OnPass>Long, short, long, short: the second hit of each beat comes late, on the last note of a triplet.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The swing call has two numbers: how hard, and which notes. Start with how hard.

<Example id="read-the-swing" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The long hit against the short one is the **swing ratio**. An amount of 0 is straight, 1:1. `.2` is a light swing, 3:2, and `1/3` the triplet swing of the blues, 2:1. `.5` swings hard, 3:1.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Each groove has its own tempo, so speed will not give the answer away. Listen for the long hit against the short one.

<Choice exercise="how-hard-it-swings" />

<OnPass>Light, hard, triplet, and the triplet feel again on the sixteenths, where each half beat is cut long–short instead of each beat.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the second number, which decides the notes that are paired. Here it pairs the wrong ones.

<Editor exercise="swing-the-sixteenths" />

<OnPass>Eight slices a bar, so each half beat swings its own pair of sixteenths: the lean of neo-soul and hip-hop. The other lines here sit on eighth places, which sixteenth swing leaves alone.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Swing is no longer a switch but a number you set by ear, on the eighths or on the sixteenths. In your song this groove plays the bridge, in A minor: Oak's drums and bass lean there at 3:2, while its chords, tune and shaker stay on the eighths, which sixteenth swing never moves. The kick and the bass already pull against the beat on light sixteenths, as your bass did last lesson: swing their lines too, and those pushed notes lean with the hats.
