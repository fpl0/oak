<Step id="S1">
Your interlocking parts kept out of each other's way: bass, clav and guitar each took places of their own. A drummer can do the same with one kit. Two grooves over the same bass and the same kick.

<Example id="one-line" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the first groove the hi-hat ticks on every eighth, on top of the kick and the snare. In the second nothing is stacked: the kick, the snare and the hi-hat take turns, and together they play every sixteenth of the bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
A drummer can build that kind of groove from either end of the kit.

<OnPass>Card B started from the cymbal: its figure has holes of its own, and a few kicks and snares sit in them. Card A started from the kick and the snare, and soft hats filled every place left over. Both keep every drum to its own places.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The second groove has a shape you can count. The roll shows it drum by drum.

<Example id="the-line" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The kick lands on the 1st, 4th and 7th eighths, the start of each group of 3, 3 and 2: 1, the and of 2, and 4. Between the kicks the snare and the hats share the rest, and the bar holds sixteen hits, one at a time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Here the hi-hat ticks every eighth on top of the kick and the snare. Move it into their gaps.

<Editor exercise="hats-in-the-gaps" />

<OnPass>Every hit now belongs to one drum. A beat where no two drums ever sound together is **linear drumming**: one line handed from drum to drum, with no part keeping time on its own.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
This line takes turns, but nothing tells you where its groups begin: on the and of 2 and on 4, a hat just follows another hat.

<Editor exercise="three-three-two" />

<OnPass>Each group of 3, 3 and 2 now starts with a change of drum, so the bar leans in three uneven steps instead of four even beats. The same split works with accents alone, louder hats on the starts.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The beat below is the ordinary kind, every drum on top of another. Rebuild it as one line, from the top or from the bottom, with the band playing bass and chords in E minor. In your song this groove opens the record: your drums play its first four bars, before the verse comes in. Bring back the pull you wrote in Metric Weight if you like: a hit on a light place, the heavier place after it left empty.

<Make exercise="your-one-line-groove" />

<Recap />
</Step>
