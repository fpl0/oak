<Step id="S1">
In the last lesson each part found its own places in the bar. Here one part keeps its rhythm but turns up somewhere new.

<Example id="moved" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In bars 2 and 4 the clav plays the same three chords with the same gaps, only later. Its first chord no longer lands with the kick, so those bars seem to turn round, though nothing in the figure changed.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Follow the figure with your hands, wherever it goes.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-figure" />

<OnPass>The same three chords in every bar, with the same gaps between them. In bars 2 and 4 they start later.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
One call at the end of the clav's line makes the move.

<Example id="read-the-move" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Half a beat later, the figure's first chord sits on the “and” of 1 and only its last lands on a beat. The same rhythm leans on different beats and sounds new. Moving a whole figure to another place in the bar is **displacement**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now a cowbell, and a bigger move.

<Editor exercise="move-the-cowbell" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 on the one, bar 2 a beat later: one figure, two rhythms.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Moving a figure by an eighth or a beat changes the rhythm. Moving a part by a few milliseconds does something else.

<Choice exercise="ahead-or-behind" />

<OnPass>Behind: the snare lands 26 ms after the hi-hat. The rhythm is the same, and the groove only sits back. Playing a few milliseconds behind the beat, roughly 8 to 45 ms, is playing **laid back**. Much further and it turns into a new rhythm: at this tempo a thirty-second of a bar is 82 ms, and you hear it as a displacement.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
This unit began with one bass note that pulled, and ends with whole figures that move across the bar and parts that only lean back. Moved onto light places, your figure pulls the way that first bass note did.
