<Step id="S1">
Funk keeps the sixteenths busy. The kick, the bass and the clav all pull against the beat, and still the groove never falls over. The clav is the new sound on top, a funk keyboard that clicks as much as it rings.

<Example id="funk" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the first loop the kick, the bass and the clav strike beat 1 together, and that one shared hit holds the busy bar in place. A sixteenth early, the bass misses the other two there, and the groove loses its footing.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Everything in the bar moves except one place. Find it with your hand.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-one" />

<OnPass>The first beat of the bar: funk players call it the one. Everything else in the groove is free to move because that beat never does.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Under the one, the drums keep busy too. The snare plays two kinds of hit.

<Example id="the-drums" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Six snare hits a bar: the backbeat cracks on 2 and 4, and four ghost notes whisper on the sixteenths around them. Add a kick that pulls on the sixteenths and parts that all meet on the one, and you have a funk groove.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A kick on every beat holds the floor down. A funk kick keeps only the one and pulls around the rest.

<Editor exercise="kick-that-pulls" />

<OnPass>The one held, and the kick leaning into the places it leaves empty. The bass and the clav still meet it on beat 1, and the rest of the bar moves.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The snare's turn. Ghost notes are felt more than heard: leave them out and the groove goes stiff.

<Editor exercise="ghost-the-snare" />

<OnPass>Loud on 2 and 4, a whisper between: the snare now moves the sixteenths along instead of only marking the beat.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Two lessons ago one bass note pulled against the beat. Here a whole kit pulls, and one shared downbeat keeps it standing. The pocket is yours, from the kick's first pull to the snare's last whisper, and in your song it drives both choruses, where Oak's tune and horns land with it on the one.

<Make exercise="your-funk-pocket" />

</Step>
