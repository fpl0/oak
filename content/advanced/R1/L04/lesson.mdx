<Step id="S1">
Last lesson every part met on the one. Away from the one, funk parts can also keep out of each other's way. Here are the three pitched parts of a groove on Cm7, one at a time, then together.

<Example id="the-machine" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Alone, each part is thin and full of holes. Together the holes close up and the three sound like one busy part, though none of them got any busier.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Now pick one part out of the whole: the clav, played quietly under the bass and the drums.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-clav" />

<OnPass>The clav lands where the bass is silent: never on the one, twice in the last beat of the bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Without the drums, the roll shows which part owns which place.

<Example id="three-parts" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Ten places, and each belongs to one part: the bass takes three with the kick, the clav four, the guitar three. Parts that fit into each other's gaps like this **interlock**. Together they play one rhythm that none of them plays alone.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
When the clav copies the bass, two parts play one rhythm and the gaps stay empty.

<Editor exercise="clav-in-the-gaps" />

<OnPass>The clav now answers the bass instead of doubling it: two parts, one rhythm with more places in it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A third part has to find places the other two have left.

<Editor exercise="add-the-guitar" />

<OnPass>Three parts, none on top of another. The guitar's short chords fill the last gaps next to the beats, and the groove runs on sixteenths no single part plays.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The busy rhythm that opened this lesson is three thin parts, and you know how to build it. In your song it is the break, in C minor, where Oak's bass, clav and guitar already take turns; your three parts take their place, with Oak's horns answering. Keep the bass locked to the kick, and let the clav pull against the beat the way your funk kick did.

<Make exercise="your-machine" />

</Step>
