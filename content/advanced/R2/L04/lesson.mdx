<Step id="S1">
In the last lesson twos and threes took turns, one bar after another. Here they play at the same time.

<Example id="both" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Alone, each pulse is plain. Together they rub against each other inside the bar and agree again at each new bar, so the loop rolls forward instead of marching.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Two pulses at once need two hands, and a well-known phrase helps them fit together.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-three" />

<OnPass>Three even taps in one hand against two in the other. Together they make four places in the bar, nice, cup, of, tea, and only the first is shared.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
On the roll the two and the three sit in rows of their own.

<Example id="both-read" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The two halves the bar, the three cuts it in thirds, and they line up only on the downbeat. Two even pulses at once in the same span, meeting there and nowhere else, are a **polyrhythm**: this one is three against two.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Here the cowbell only doubles the kick's two.

<Editor exercise="write-the-three" />

<OnPass>Three words in the cowbell's string, three equal steps, and they sound in every bar the kick does. Six would have landed on both kicks, and four on the kick's second note. Neither pulls against the two.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The cowbell is the high sound, the kick the low one. Count each layer on its own.

<Choice exercise="which-against-which" />

<OnPass>Three against two, its mirror two against three, four against three, and straight four against two. In the straight loop the kick lands with every other cowbell note; in the other three the layers meet only on the downbeat.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Three against two runs through West African and Afro-Cuban drumming. In your song's verses Oak's kick and bass play two even notes a bar while its cowbell and chords play three, under a tune that sings the three. Here every part plays the two: give the cowbell its three, and your loop takes over both verses, with Oak's tune on top.

<Make exercise="your-three-against-two" />
