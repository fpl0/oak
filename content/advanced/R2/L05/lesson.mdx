<Step id="S1">
Three against two met again at every new bar. This lesson's kick takes much longer to come home.

<Example id="drift" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the second loop the kick and the bass keep an even pace of their own while the snare keeps the bar. They land somewhere new in each bar, and the groove seems to tumble forward until they meet beat 1 again.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Let the snare keep the bar, and follow the kick wherever it goes.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-wandering-kick" />

<OnPass>Eight even taps, three beats apart: on beat 1, then beat 4, then beat 3 of the next bar, then beat 2, then beat 1 again. The kick never changed speed. The bar line moved under it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Four bars on the roll, and the short string that made them.

<Example id="drift-read" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three steps, played at four steps a bar. The cycle runs out before the bar does, so each bar starts somewhere else in it. Only after three bars, twelve steps, do the two line up again. A part that repeats a pattern of another length than the bar, over the same pulse, is a **polymeter**. Curly brackets hold the cycle; `%4` says how many of its steps fill a bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The bass already drifts; the kick still keeps the bar.

<Editor exercise="let-the-kick-drift" />

<OnPass>Kick and bass now share one three-step cycle and land together wherever it goes. Four steps at four a bar, or three at three, would have been the bar again. A polymeter needs a cycle that neither fits a whole number of times into the bar nor fills a whole number of bars.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
One more way to move the beat without touching the tempo line. Listen for what the hi-hat does when the kick changes.

<Choice exercise="faster-or-not" />

<OnPass>The tempo line never changed, and each bar lasts 2.4 seconds on both sides. The kick took the same twelve hi-hat notes in threes, then in twos, and the beat you feel jumped from four to six a bar. Hearing the same pulse in a new grouping, so the beat seems to change speed while the bar stays the same length, is a **metric modulation**: here an implied one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Every pattern in this unit so far fitted its bar. This one runs on a cycle of its own and still comes home. No band: the line that keeps the bar and the line that drifts are both yours, and the check listens to both. Give the hi-hat some metric weight, louder on the beats, so the ear keeps the bar while the kick wanders.

<Make exercise="your-drift" />
