<Step id="S1">
The last three lessons gave you three devices: the tritone substitute, the back door and the aimed ii–V. Here they go under a real tune. Aura Lee's verse, a folk song you have known for a long time, becomes a late-night ballad on the tenor, and not one note of the tenor changes.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The tune never moved, yet bars 1, 3, 6 and 7 take on new colours: a climb in the bass, slides by half steps, a lean into the next chord. Under a fixed melody, the chords set the mood.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The climb in bar 1, close up.

<Choice exercise="a-climbing-bass" />

<OnPass>Card B: on the last beat of bar 1 the bass steps up to C♯, then to D. The chord on C♯ is a diminished seventh, C♯ E G B♭, slipped between two chords a whole step apart. That is a **passing diminished chord**: it adds no new home, only a climb into the next chord.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
A device fits under a tune where the tune agrees with it. Bar 3 shows where a tritone substitute can go, and where it cannot.

<Example id="bar-three" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>On beat 1 the tune's C rubs a half step against D♭7's C♭. From beat 3 the tune plays A and B, the ♭13 and the 7th of D♭7, and the substitute sounds at home. The tune decides on which beat a new chord can start.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The first phrase of the verse, over its sevenths.

<Editor exercise="climb-into-d7" />

<OnPass>Cmaj7 for three beats, C♯°7 on the fourth, and the bass climbs C, C♯, D into D7. The tune's C on that beat sits a whole step above the chord's B♭, a note it can carry.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
By ear alone now. Three bars of the new setting hold two chords; one of those pairs is aimed at the chord after it.

<Choice exercise="find-the-aimed-pair" />

<OnPass>Bar 6: Am7 D7, a ii–V aimed at G7 in bar 7. Under it the tune holds D, the 11th of Am7 and the root of D7.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The verse over its sevenths again, with the drums. The tune is fixed, and the chords are yours.

</Step>
