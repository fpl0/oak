<Step id="S1">
Every ii–V you have written so far aimed at home. Jazz players aim them at other chords too. The trio plays four bars twice, with the same vibes line on top; only bar 2 changes.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the first take bar 3 is only the next chord. In the second, bar 2 leans hard into it, and Dm7 lands like a small homecoming before the loop moves on.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Why does Dm7 sound like home for a moment? Read the roots on the roll.

<Example id="aimed" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>E, A, D: each root falls a 5th, exactly as D, G, C does into home. Em7 A7 is a ii–V aimed at D, and A7 is the V7/ii you already know. For one bar, Dm7 is treated as a home of its own.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The same move in B♭, aimed at Cm7.

<Editor exercise="aim-at-c-minor" />

<OnPass>Dm7 G7 into Cm7: the roots fall D, G, C, and Cm7 lands like a small homecoming inside B♭. Because Cm7 is minor, Dm7♭5 G7 aims at it just as well. A ii–V aimed at a chord other than home is a **secondary ii–V**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Another way to lean into Dm7: one short chord between C and Dm7.

<Choice exercise="a-bass-that-climbs" />

<OnPass>In card B, beat 4 holds C♯°7, a diminished seventh a half step above C, and the bass climbs C, C♯, D. The next lesson puts this chord to work under a tune.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
If you have time today, one more step.

<Editor exercise="slip-it-in" />

<OnPass>Cmaj7 for three beats, C♯°7 on the fourth, and the bass climbs by half steps into Dm7.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A ii–V used to point only at home. Now you can aim one at any chord of a tune and make that chord a small home for a bar. This tune in G with long notes on the vibes is the first A section of your song, played twice, right after the opening turnaround.
