<Step id="S1">
The ♭9 you bent last lesson sat above the seventh, and jazz chords grow upward there all the time. Here is a slow ii–V–I in E♭, twice. The vibes tune does not change.

<Example id="colour-ballad" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>On plain sevenths the tune's long notes sit outside the chords and float above them. In the second take the chords reach up to meet those notes, and the sound turns rich and settled. The roots and the pull home stay the same.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
One chord, two ways. Bar 2 adds one note on top, and the Rhodes leaves the root to the bass.

<Example id="nine-added" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>G is the 9th: a 2nd above F, an octave up, 14 half steps from the root. Keep stacking thirds and the 11th (a 4th up an octave) and the 13th (a 6th up an octave) come next. Each is an **extension**, colour on top of the chord that leaves its job alone. Fm9 is still the ii.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The V can wear other colours, sweet or bent.

<Choice exercise="three-dominants" />

<OnPass>Card B, B♭13: its 9th and 13th are notes of E♭ major, so it sounds sweet. Card A bends the 9th up a half step (B♭7♯9); card C bends the 9th both ways at once and raises the 5th (B♭7alt). Both are altered dominants, darker and more urgent than the 13th, and both resolve home all the same.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Not every note above the seventh is a colour. Hold each of these and judge it against its chord.

<Choice exercise="colour-or-clash" />

<OnPass>9th, 13th, clash, 9th. A♭ over E♭maj7 is the 11th by name, but held there it sits a half step above the chord's G and grinds. A note like that is an **avoid note**: fine on the way somewhere, never somewhere to stay. Over Fm7 the 11th is a colour, because nothing sits a half step under it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The second take of the first step coloured these four chords.

<Editor exercise="colour-the-chart" />

<OnPass>Fm9, B♭13, E♭maj9: the same roots in the bass, the same jobs, and a colour inside every chord.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
A chord used to stop at its seventh. Now you can write the colours above it, and you know which note to pass through rather than hold. The chords already shimmer; let the tune hang on their colours.
