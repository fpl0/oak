<Step id="S1">
Last lesson's tune hung on a few long notes. A jazz line moves in eighths, and it has to follow the chords as they change. Here the vibes play the same eight numbers in every bar of a ii–V–I in G, on two kinds of scale.

<Example id="line-follows" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the first take each bar starts on its chord's root, so the line outlines every chord as it arrives. On G major alone every note still belongs, but each bar starts on G, and the line sounds parked at home while the chords move under it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Where do those scales come from? Three runs, each from its first note to the octave.

<Example id="three-runs" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A Dorian is G major started on A: the same seven notes, so over a ii–V–I in G it fits the ii and keeps the key. G Lydian swaps C for C♯, the ♯11, a brighter colour for home. A scale chosen to fit one chord is its **chord-scale**: a place to start, not a law. Mixolydian fits the V the same way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The same three jobs in another key.

<Choice exercise="name-the-scales" />

<OnPass>C Dorian, F Mixolydian, B♭ major: B♭ major started on each chord's root. Minor-seventh ii, Dorian; dominant V, Mixolydian; major home, major, or Lydian when you want it brighter.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The second take from the first step is back, on one scale.

<Editor exercise="follow-the-changes" />

<OnPass>A, D, G: each bar starts on its chord's root, with the chord's 1, 3, 5 and 7 on the beats. One line of numbers, and it follows the changes.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Four more scales, one bar each, run up from the chord's root. Locrian fits the half-diminished ii of a minor key. The altered scale fits an altered V. Lydian dominant, Mixolydian with its 4th raised, fits a 7♯11. The bebop scale is Mixolydian with one extra passing note between the 7th and the root.

<Example id="four-more" views="code,roll" />
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your line no longer sits on one scale while the chords move. It changes scale with every chord, and it can follow a ii–V–I into a new key.

<Make exercise="your-solo" />
