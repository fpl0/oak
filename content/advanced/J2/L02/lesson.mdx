<Step id="S1">
Every ii–V–I you toured last lesson was major, bright at home. Your trio now plays one into D twice. The rhythm and the shape of the vibes line stay the same.

<Example id="minor-trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The second take lands in shadow: home is a minor chord, and the two chords before it are darker too. Over the V the vibes lean on a note that rubs, then fall home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Find that rubbing note. Read the V's symbol, then listen to the vibes' first note in bar 2.

<Example id="minor-trio-again" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>B♭: the 9th of A bent down a half step, and the sixth note of D minor itself. The V borrows a note from the key it is heading for, so it leans home harder. With the half-diminished ii and the minor home, that is the **minor ii–V–i**: the same pull as the major one, in shadow.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
By ear alone this time, with no symbols. Two cadences home to D minor; only the V differs.

<Choice exercise="which-v-bends" />

<OnPass>Card B's V holds B♭, the 9th above its root A, bent down a half step. A dominant with a bent note like this is an **altered dominant**. Jazz players bend other notes of the V too; you will hear them next.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The bright trio from the first step is back.

<Editor exercise="into-the-shadow" />

<OnPass>Em7♭5, A7♭9, then a minor home, Dm7 (or its cousins Dm6 and D–∆7): the same roots, every chord in the minor key's colour.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
In any key, with no vibes line to point the way. Listen for where it lands.

<EarDrill family="fam.jzz.ii-v" level={2} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Bright or shadowed, in any key: you can hear which ii–V a tune takes before you read its chart.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
You came into this lesson with one colour of ii–V–I. Now you have its shadow too, and the bent note that makes the V lean. Turn the bright starter into a minor ballad of your own.
