<Step id="S1">
The jazz blues is one form every jazz player knows. Here is the other: thirty-two bars in B♭ at a brisk tempo, the trio with a vibes riff on top. The form lens marks the sections as they go by.

<Example id="the-whole-form" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The vibes rest for eight bars while the chords leave home and move in two-bar steps, each one pulling toward the next. When the riff comes back, so does home: the same eight bars you heard at the start.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Read the same thirty-two bars.

<Example id="read-the-form" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Four sections of eight bars, A A B A. Each A is a string of turnarounds in B♭. The bridge is four dominant sevenths, two bars each, every root a 4th above the last, and its F7 pulls home into the final A. These are **rhythm changes**, the chords of George Gershwin's I Got Rhythm (1930), and many bebop tunes are new melodies over them. Players vary the second half of each A: this version turns through E♭ and D–7, and you will also meet B♭ B♭7 | E♭ E♭–.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
You met chains like this in the core: each chord is the V7 of a chord in the key.

<Choice exercise="number-the-bridge" />

<OnPass>V7/vi, V7/ii, V7/V, V7: D7 aims at Gm7, G7 at Cm7, C7 at F, and F7 at home. In the bridge each one moves on to the next dominant instead of landing, so the pull keeps building until F7 lets go into the last A.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Here the bridge still holds the A section's chords.

<Editor exercise="write-the-bridge" />

<OnPass>The bridge in place: after sixteen bars of turnarounds the bass climbs D, G, C, F, two bars each, and the last A lands home. Played twice as fast, one bar a chord, the chain runs out at bar 20 and starts again.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Two lessons ago a form was twelve bars on three chords. Now you can build thirty-two bars with a bridge, from sections you arrange in code.
