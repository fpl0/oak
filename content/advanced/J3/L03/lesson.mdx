<Step id="S1">
You can voice a jazz chord as a shell, rootless or in drop-2. When a pianist plays it matters as much as how. The same trio plays the same chords three times; only the Rhodes' timing changes.

<Example id="charleston-trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Held chords say nothing to the ride. Two short hits a bar, the second just after beat 2, swing along with it and leave space for it. Chords that arrive just before the bar line pull the music forward into each new bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The second take again. Tap with the Rhodes, not with the ride.
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The chords stay the rootless A and B voicings you already play. This time you give them a rhythm.

<Editor exercise="comp-two-hits" />

<OnPass>Beat 1, then the swung "and" of 2: that is the **Charleston rhythm**, one of the figures jazz pianists comp with most. The second hit leans toward beat 3 and leaves the rest of the bar to the ride.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The third take's figure: each chord arrives before its own bar.

<Example id="pushed-trio" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Each chord starts on the swung "and" of 4 and rings across the bar line into the bar it belongs to. The first time through, Cm7 would have begun before the music did, so bar 1 holds only F7, arriving early for bar 2.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The held chords from the first take are back.

<Editor exercise="push-every-chord" />

<OnPass>Every chord a swung eighth early, held across the bar line. The bass still lands on beat 1, and the Rhodes gets there first.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The Rhodes no longer just holds its chords: the Charleston and the push give it two ways to answer the ride. In your song your comping plays the four bars in B♭ each time through, under Oak's drums, bass and vibes tune; a vibes line of your own would take the tune's place there.

<Make exercise="your-comping" />

Next: the bass that walks under this comping, a note on every beat.

<Recap />
</Step>
