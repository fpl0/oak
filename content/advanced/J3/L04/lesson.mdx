<Step id="S1">
Your comping now answers the ride. Under it, the bass has played in two, a root and a 5th a bar. Here the trio plays a ii–V–I in G twice, and only the bass changes.

<Example id="walking-trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the second take the bass plays on every beat and leans into each new bar, and the whole trio starts to travel. The ride and the Rhodes did not change; the bass carried them forward.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Read the second take's bass line note by note.

<Example id="walk-held" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A note on every beat, and beat 1 always the chord's root: A, D, G, G. Beat 3 is another note of the chord. Beat 4 sits a half step from where the next bar starts: E♭ into D, A♭ into G, F♯ into G, B♭ back into A. That is a **walking bass**: an approach tone on every bar line, with notes of the chord filling the beats between. It is the floor a jazz trio stands on.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
This bass walks but jumps at every bar line.

<Editor exercise="lean-into-each-bar" />

<OnPass>Same notes on beats 1 to 3, a new note on beat 4, and the line no longer jumps: it walks into D, into G, down to the low G and back into A.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Three ways a bass can sit under the same chords. Count the notes in a bar first, then listen to the bar lines.

<Choice exercise="how-the-bass-moves" />

<OnPass>In two, the bass holds; climbing, it moves but lands with a jump; walking with approach notes, it never stops leaning forward. That last pull at every bar line is what makes a swing tune feel like it is going somewhere.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The pianist has voicings and rhythms now; the bass is the last chair of the trio. In your song your bass plays the four bars in G each time through, under Oak's drums, comping and tune on Am7 D7 Gmaj7 Gmaj7; the bass starts in two.

<Make exercise="your-walking-bass" />

You can now walk a bass line through a set of changes, under any ii–V–I you write. Next: the turnarounds that bring a tune back to the top, and a walk through a whole jazz chorus.

</Step>
