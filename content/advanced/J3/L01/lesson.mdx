<Step id="S1">
Your shells put the root at the bottom of every chord, where the bass already plays it. Here the trio comps a ii–V–I in F two ways, starting with your shells.

<Example id="rootless-trio" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The second take sits higher, in the middle of the keyboard, clear of the bass. It plays four notes a chord instead of three, and one of them is a colour above the seventh, so the same changes sound richer.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
What changed? The second take's chords are held a bar each here, over the bass, so the roll can show them.

<Example id="rootless-held" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The root has gone to the bass. Gm7 is B♭ D F A, its 3rd, 5th, 7th and 9th stacked from the 3rd: an **A voicing**. C7 keeps three of those notes and moves one. B♭ D E A, its 7th, 9th, 3rd and 13th stacked from the 7th, is a **B voicing** (on a dominant the 13th stands in for the 5th). Fmaj7 is an A voicing again, so the hand hardly moves.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now with your hands. Fmaj7 is F A C E, and its 9th is G.

<OnPass>A C E G: Fmaj7 without its root, standing on its 3rd. Over an F in the bass it is the whole chord with a 9th on top.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The second take's comping, in its rhythm.

<Editor exercise="shells-to-rootless" />

<OnPass>A B A by hand. Between Gm7 and C7 a single note moves a half step, and the chords still change colour.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The left-hand dictionary holds both shapes of each chord, so code can do what your hand just did.

<Editor exercise="offset-to-b" />

<OnPass>`.offset(1)` moved each chord one place up. In `lefthand` the next place is the other shape, so you hear B A B, the hand starting on F. `.offset(2)` would bring back the first shapes, an octave higher. Without an anchor the dictionary keeps every top note at or below C5, and in F that makes Fmaj7 jump to a B voicing. Set the anchor and the alternation holds.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
With a melody on top, the comping's top note can land on the tune's own note.

<Example id="duck" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>`.mode("duck")` leaves out the one note equal to the anchor, so the comping never doubles the tune: Gm7 and C7 play three notes under the vibes. The A3 in Fmaj7 stays, because only the anchor's own note is left out, not the same letter an octave lower. Duck takes nothing else away: the root was never in these shapes.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your shells gave the left hand three notes on top of the root. Now it plays four, leaves the root to the bass, and alternates between two shapes that sit close together. The band plays the drums and the bass in F; the comping starts in shells.

</Step>
