<Step id="S1">
Last time a tune learned to face the clave. In a son band the bass and the piano line up with it too, each with a figure of its own, while the clave and the cowbell hold the two-bar frame.

<Example id="son" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>With the bass and the piano back, the groove leans ahead. The bass never touches beat 1, while the clave and the cowbell hold the frame steady under it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Start with the bass. It plays two notes a bar, and neither is where a bass usually starts.

<Example id="the-bass" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The bass leaves beat 1 empty, plays the chord's 5th on the “and” of 2, and on beat 4 it already plays the root of the next chord, a beat early. That figure is the **tumbao**, the bass of son and salsa. Each bar it pulls the harmony one beat forward, an anticipation in every bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Take a bass that sits on the roots and make it pull.

<Editor exercise="pull-the-chords-forward" />

<OnPass>Two notes a bar, and the second one already belongs to the next chord. Listen to how the chord change seems to arrive before the piano plays it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the piano. Its two bars are a pair, like the clave's, and the order of the pair matters.

<Editor exercise="turn-the-piano-round" />

<OnPass>Turned round, the piano lands with five of the clave's strokes in every two bars. Crossed, it met one. A repeating two-bar piano figure that faces the clave like this is a **montuno**. This one is in thirds, and its last pair of notes arrives on the “and” of 4, ahead of the C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The notes never change; only the order of the piano's two bars does.

<Choice exercise="with-or-crossed" />

<OnPass>Facing the clave, the piano locks with the rimshots and the groove sits. Crossed, the same notes keep landing between them, and the groove pulls against itself.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
One bass figure opened this unit. A son band also plays hand drums: the congas, tall drums struck with the palm and the fingers, and the bongos, a pair of small ones. In Strudel they are `conga` and `bongo`.

<Example id="hand-drums" views="code,roll" />

Your piano and bass take over the vamp of your song, after the break, where Oak plays a montuno and a tumbao now; Oak's tune keeps singing over them. For the piano's bar under the clave's three, the tresillo you started with is a good place to begin.

<Make exercise="your-son" />

<Recap />
</Step>
