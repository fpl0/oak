<Step id="S1">
Any groove you love on a record is a stack of lines like these, and each can be taken down by ear. Today is a lab. The tape is a son groove like the last lesson's, and you take it apart one line at a time.

<Example id="tape" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Together the lines blur into one groove. Alone, each is a handful of hits a bar, and the rimshot, the cowbell, the bass and the piano all play figures you built in this unit.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
First, name what you hear, from every corner of the rhythm track. Each item has a ruler to count against: the cowbell, the hi-hat or the kick.

<Choice exercise="name-the-rhythm" />

<OnPass>The clave by the bar that holds three, the seven by where its long group falls, three against two by how often the two pulses meet, the swing by the length of the pair. Every one of them is a count against something steady.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now write one down. Count the bass in eighths, 1 to 8, with the cowbell to keep you on the grid.

<Editor exercise="take-the-bass-down" />

<OnPass>Two notes a bar, neither on beat 1: the tumbao, taken down by ear. With the right slots the given notes fall into place, and your bass is the tape's.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
One line of the tape has changed.

<Editor exercise="take-the-bell-down" />

<OnPass>The cinquillo's five hits, each one an eighth later: the same figure, rotated by a step. It no longer marks beat 1, so the groove leans further forward.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The rhythm track began with one question: which places in a bar are heavy? It ends with the tape you have just taken apart, which now plays the eight bars of your song where Oak's horns answer the chorus tune. There Oak's cowbell states the cinquillo for two bars, then brings it back an eighth later: reach back to the track's first unit and do the same with the tape's cowbell, while the rest of the tape holds still.
