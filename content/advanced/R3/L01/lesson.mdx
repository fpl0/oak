<Step id="S1">
This unit turns to the rhythms of Cuba and Brazil. It starts with one bass figure, three notes a bar, that you have heard in countless pop songs.

<Example id="groove" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The first loop leans forward and the second stands still, though the drums and the chords never change. The bass's rhythm alone did that.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Nod on the beat with the kick and the snare, and let your taps follow the bass.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-bass" />

<OnPass>Three taps a bar: on beat 1, between beats 2 and 3, and on beat 4. The one between the beats is what makes each bar lean into the next.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Here the bass sits on beats 1 and 3.

<Editor exercise="three-three-two" />

<OnPass>Three, three, two: three notes across the eight eighth notes of a bar. That is the **tresillo**, the bass rhythm under habanera, son, rock and roll and a great deal of today's pop.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Strudel has a shorter way to ask for the same thing: a number of hits and a number of steps, in brackets after the sound.

<Predict exercise="three-over-eight" />
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
`(3,8)` is the tresillo you wrote by hand. The two numbers are yours to change.

<Editor exercise="five-over-eight" />

<OnPass>`(5,8)`: the tresillo's three hits, with one more just before the second and the third. That is the **cinquillo**, the rhythm at the heart of the Cuban danzón, and it still leans the way the tresillo does.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The kick and the hats are your ruler. Count the rimshots against them.

<Choice exercise="three-five-or-four" />

<OnPass>Three uneven hits is the tresillo, five is the cinquillo, four even ones on the beat is straight. The kick is the same in every groove; only the spacing changes what you feel.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The tresillo is yours now, by hand or from two numbers, and you can tell it from the cinquillo by ear. Its middle note is a pull like the ones in your funk pocket: a light place, with the bass silent on the beat after it.
