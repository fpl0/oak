<Step id="S1">
A motif comes back inside a tune. The part of a song you still hum on the way home is its hook, and this one is built from a single bar.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>No. The last note of bar 4 plays on some repeats and drops out on others. The hook stays the same tune, and the small change keeps its repeats from sounding copied and pasted.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
What makes a tune stick? Here are two over the same chords, and both of them fit.

<Choice exercise="which-one-sticks" />

<OnPass>Card A. It is short, it comes back, and it has room to breathe: a few notes, one high point, the same rhythm each time. Card B fits just as well and is much harder to keep.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The note that comes and goes is in the code.

<Example id="now-and-then" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Two loops out of four. A `?` after a note plays it about half the time; which loops get it is chance, but the same chance every time you run the code.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A hook is made by bringing an idea back. Here the idea is there in bar 1, and then forgotten.

<Editor exercise="tighten-it" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 in every bar, moved where the chord needs it. A hook can be this plain: the repetition is what makes it stick, and the moves keep it from standing still. A listener who can guess the next bar can sing along with it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Three ways to let chance in: `?` on one note, `.degradeBy()` on the whole line, `.sometimesBy()` with a change of your choice. Chance is good at ideas. Keeping the good ones is your job.

<Editor exercise="freeze-it" />

<OnPass>Chance proposed, you chose. The hook now plays the notes chance found, the same way every time, with your own fixes on top. Writers use chance like this: to hear what they would not have written, then to keep it on purpose.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Hooks are made to be caught by ear. Count before you write: does it start on a 1?

<Editor exercise="catch-the-hook" />

<OnPass>A pickup on 5, a leap up to the high home, then a step down at a time. Two bars, one high point, and you could sing it after two hearings: that is a hook.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your hook plays the last loud part of your song, twice through over I V vi IV, right after the one-bar stop. Everything from this unit can go in: a motif moved by `.scaleTranspose()`, one high point, chord tones on the strong beats. At the start of the unit a tune was notes that fit the chords; now you can shape one, build it from one idea, and make it stick.
