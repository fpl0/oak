<Step id="S1">
In Question & Answer one line asked and the same line answered. Here two instruments share the job.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>They take turns: each one plays while the other is silent. Two voices trading short phrases sound like a conversation, and the gaps are what make room for the reply.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Two melody lines this time: `mel` on the piano and `vox` on the Rhodes.

<Predict exercise="whose-bar" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Mute one part at a time. A part on its own tells you what it leaves for the other.

<Example id="take-turns" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The piano plays three notes and leaves five beats of silence; the Rhodes fills them and, in bar 4, takes the tune home. One part plays a phrase and another answers it in the gap: **call and response**. It comes from West African singing and lives on in gospel and soul: a lead singer and the backing vocals, a horn section answering a guitar, a crowd answering a singer.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now you answer. The call is written; the gaps are yours.

<Editor exercise="answer-the-call" />

<OnPass>A response that starts on a note of the new chord sounds like it was waiting for its turn. Ending on 1 in bar 4 gives the four bars their full stop.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The quickest answer is an echo: the same line again, moved. `.off()` plays a copy of the line, later by the time you give it, changed by the function you give it.

<Editor exercise="an-echo" />

<OnPass>An echo answers right away, inside the call's own bar or just after it. A second part with its own line answers in its own words. Songs use both.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
By ear now. The call is given; the reply in the gaps is yours to catch.

<Editor exercise="hear-the-reply" />

<Callout kind="tip">Switch between the source and your code at the same bar to compare the reply note by note.</Callout>

<OnPass>B, G, D falls through the notes of V; A, F, C falls through the notes of IV and lands home. A reply often does this: it runs down the chord under it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your two lines play the quiet part of your song twice, before the one-bar stop, where Oak's tune calls and a second line answers. This call climbs twice and leaves room after each climb. Your response can borrow its motif, moved, or run down the chord under it like the reply you just caught.
