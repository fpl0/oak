<Step id="S1">
In the last lesson every note outside the chord sat on a weak beat and slipped by. In this slow tune, two of them land on beat 1 and stay a moment.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>A rub, then relief. Both times the tune meets the new chord on a note the chord does not have, holds it, and only then steps down into it. The tension on the beat makes that step down sound like an arrival.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The same two bars, twice, with one difference at the moment the chord changes.

<Choice exercise="which-one-leans" />

<OnPass>The held C makes the G chord wait. When it falls to B, the chord settles.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The tune is written as one slowed sequence, so a note can last across a bar line: `7@4` is four beats long, wherever the bar ends.

<Example id="held-over" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The C belongs to the IV chord in bar 2. The chord changes under it, and against G it rubs; on beat 3 it steps down to B, the chord's 3rd. A note held over from one chord into the next, where it clashes, then falling a step into the chord, is a **suspension**. Counted from G, C is the 4th and B the 3rd: a 4–3 suspension. In the key, degree 1 (C) falls to 7 (B).</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
This tune meets the G chord on B at once, so nothing leans.

<Editor exercise="make-it-lean" />

<OnPass>`7@4 6@2`: the C carries on into bar 3 and B arrives two beats late, on beat 3. The total is still sixteen beats, four bars. Guitarists play the same move inside one chord: Gsus4, with C in place of B, settling into G.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The tune's second lean comes in bar 4.

<Example id="leapt-to" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>D is not held over: the tune leaps up a 3rd from B, lands on beat 1 against the C chord, and falls to C. A note outside the chord on the strong beat, reached by a leap, that leans and then steps into the chord is an **appoggiatura**, from the Italian for leaning. Counted from C, D is the 9th falling to the 8th, the octave: a 9–8 lean.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Four kinds now. The staff marks the note; the notes around it tell you which kind it is.

<EarDrill family="fam.mel.nct" level={2} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Held over and falling, or leapt to and falling: two ways to lean on a strong beat. Passing and neighbour tones only pass by.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your tune and your I IV V I end the song, its last four bars, over Oak's drums and bass; every note of the starter's tune is a chord tone, so it never leans. Oak's ending holds an A from the IV bar into the V bar and lets it fall to G. A lean counts most into the V chord, onto the final I or on the high point.
