<Step id="S1">
In Motif & Variation you moved a motif to another degree and read it backwards. There are more ways to turn one idea into another, and each one keeps something the ear still knows.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Every copy keeps the motif's rhythm: short, short, long, short, short, rest. What changes is where the notes go: higher, the other way up, back to front, or the same order begun from a later note. That kept rhythm is why each copy still sounds like the motif.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Two lines now, the motif and a copy, playing together.

<Predict exercise="which-copy" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The simplest copy first: the one you already know how to make.

<Example id="moved-up" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Two degrees up, every note at the same place in time, every move the same way as the motif's. A copy moved like this keeps both the rhythm and the up-and-down shape, so it sounds like the motif in another place.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now a copy that keeps the rhythm and turns the shape over. Both lines play at once, so you hear the mirror as you write it.

<Editor exercise="upside-down" />

<OnPass>Where the motif climbs, the copy falls by the same number of degrees: 1 3 5 against 5 3 1. A motif turned upside down like this is a **melodic inversion**. Mirrored around its middle note, as here, the copy keeps the motif's range. The book's inversion keeps half steps exactly; the degree mirror keeps the key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Backwards can mean two things, and they sound different.

<Choice exercise="backwards-two-ways" />

<OnPass>Card B keeps the motif's rhythm and only reverses its notes. `.rev()` reverses the whole bar, rests and lengths too, so its rhythm comes out back to front as well. That full reversal is the book's retrograde: the gaps between the notes are reversed too.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
One more copy that keeps the rhythm: the same notes in the same order, begun from a later one.

<Editor exercise="start-it-later" />

<OnPass>5 4 2 1 3: the motif's order, begun on its third note and wrapped round, on the motif's own rhythm. Starting a motif's notes from a later one like this is a **pitch rotation**. Strudel has no call for it, so you write it by moving the numbers.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
The upside-down copy from earlier crosses the motif in the middle of the bar. Played lower, it becomes a second voice under the tune.

<Editor exercise="under-the-motif" />

<OnPass>Now the copy sits under the motif all bar, moving the opposite way: one line climbs while the other falls. A transformation can be a harmony as well as a new bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Every copy so far kept the motif's place in time. This one keeps everything else and moves the time. Your motif and its later copy play in the first loud part of your song. In Motif & Variation one idea carried four bars; now you can turn it over, start it later in its order, or move it in time.

<Make exercise="later-in-the-bar" />

<Recap />
</Step>
