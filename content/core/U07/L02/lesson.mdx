<Step id="S1">
The tune in Shape took eight bars of notes to draw its line. This one is four notes, one line of code over the four-chord loop, and those four notes make the whole tune.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The same up-and-down shape four times, each time starting somewhere new, on a new chord. You hear one tune, not four, because the shape stays put while the notes move.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The four-bar tune has one more function in it: `.scaleTranspose()`, after `.scale()`. Find out what it does from what you hear.

<Example id="one-idea-moved" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>One shape, 1 3 5 3, started on the root of each chord: degree 1, then 5, 6 and 4. `.scaleTranspose("<0 4 5 3>")` moves it that many degrees, a bar at a time, the way `.add()` moved a chord onto a new root. A short idea that comes back like this is a **motif**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The same motif over the drums, stuck in place: four bars of 1 3 5 3.

<Editor exercise="climb-a-step" />

<OnPass>`.scaleTranspose("<0 1 2 3>")`: the motif one degree higher each bar. A motif repeated from another degree, so the same shape climbs or falls, is a **sequence**. Because the steps are degrees, the 3rds inside it change size to stay in the key: C E G, then D F A.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A motif can also come back turned round. Here bar 1 asks, and bar 2 answers by retracing its steps.

<Editor exercise="turn-it-round" />

<OnPass>`[7 4 2 0]`: up in bar 1, back down the same way in bar 2. A motif played backwards, last note first, is a **retrograde**. `.rev()` does the same to a whole cycle: on a line that changes bar by bar, it turns every bar round.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Repeat, move, turn round: three ways to keep an idea alive. Hear what each one adds.

<Choice exercise="which-one-keeps-you" />

<OnPass>Repetition makes a motif stick. Moving it and turning it round keeps it interesting, and lets it fit each new chord: in card A, bar 2 starts on C against the G chord and bumps. Good tunes usually do both.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your four bars play twice in the first loud part of your song, over the four-chord loop, where Oak's tune moves one motif onto each chord. The starter's four bars fit their chords but share no shape. Where every bar used to be a new idea, one idea can now carry four.
