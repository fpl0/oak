<Step id="S1">
In Motif & Variation the motif started on each chord's root, so it fitted every chord by design. This tune moves more freely over the same four-chord loop.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Settled, even though it never stands still. Whatever the tune does in between, it keeps coming back to notes the chord under it agrees with.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Same chords, same rhythm, two tunes a step apart.

<Choice exercise="which-one-fits" />

<OnPass>Card A. Card B is the same tune one degree higher, and at the start of every bar it lands on a note the new chord does not have.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Beats 1 and 3 are the strong beats of a 4/4 bar, the ones you would count loudest: ONE, two, THREE, four. Check what the tune plays there.

<Example id="beats-one-and-three" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Every note on beats 1 and 3 belongs to its chord: G and E over C, G and D over G, E and C over Am, C over F. A melody note that belongs to the chord sounding under it is a **chord tone**. The notes between, such as the F in bar 1 and the D in bar 3, are not, and they do not need to be. They pass by on the way to the next chord tone.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now you check. The chords are the loop's four; the tune is new.

<Choice exercise="in-the-chord-or-not" />

<OnPass>E is in C, and A is in F. A over G and G over Am are not chord tones: on a strong beat, each one leans against its chord.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Bars 1 and 3 of this tune land well. Play it and listen for the bump at the start of bars 2 and 4.

<Editor exercise="fix-the-landings" />

<OnPass>B over G and F over F: now the start of every bar agrees with its chord, and the tune sounds as settled as the first one. The ear weighs the strong beats most when it hears a tune against its chords; the notes between can stray and come back.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
By ear now. Listen for the strong beats first: there, the tune is inside the chord you hear.

<Editor exercise="hear-where-it-lands" />

<OnPass>Degree 5, then 3, inside the C chord; 2 over G, down to 7 below home, then up to home as the C chord returns. 7 and 2 are both notes of G, and both sit a step from home, so the line falls into 1 cleanly.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your tune and your `chords` line play the second loud part of your song, twice through, and Oak's bass plays the root of each of your chords under them. Every note on beats 1 and 3 of the starter sits a step away from its chord. A motif from the last lesson can carry the tune: bring your bar-1 idea back, and let its strong beats land on each new chord.
