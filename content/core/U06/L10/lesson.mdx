<Step id="S1">
Every chord so far has been a triad, three letters stacked in 3rds. Here is a loop on 1, 6, 2 and 5, on the Rhodes over an upright bass, and then the same loop in plain triads.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Loop 1 sounds richer and softer, as if each chord were still leaning somewhere. Loop 2 sounds plainer and more settled, though its roots are the same. Something inside each chord changed.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">

<Step id="S3">
The hook's four chords alone, over the drums. Each is the same stack, moved onto a new root by `.add()`.

<Example id="the-four-stacks" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Four notes: 1, 3, 5 and 7 on the first chord, and the same shape on the others. The top note is one more 3rd, a step under the root's octave. A triad with one more 3rd on top is a **seventh chord**, named after that top note, the 7th above the root. Its kinds and names come later in the course; here it is a colour.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
One stack feeds every chord of the loop, so one change colours all four.

<Editor exercise="colour-the-loop" />

<OnPass>`[0,2,4,6]` on 1, 6, 2 and 5: the I vi ii V loop, now with a 7th on every chord. That is the neo-soul sound of the hook.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The 7th sits a step below the root's octave: B is one key under the C above it. A 7th is a 2nd turned inside out, so it keeps a little of a 2nd's rub, a softness a triad does not have.

<Choice exercise="three-notes-or-four" />

<OnPass>Card A, with the extra 3rd. That soft rub at the top is a colour jazz and neo-soul players give to nearly every chord.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now with your hands, four notes this time.

<OnPass>D, F, A, C: the chord of bar 3, ii with its 7th on top. Its 7th, C, is degree 1 of the key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your oldest loop, I V vi IV, has been stacked, broken, inverted and voiced, and your version of it from The Four-Chord Schema plays the eight bars after the opening of your song. Now it can take a new colour. A 7th on every chord is the neo-soul sound; a 7th on only some of them gives the loop light and shade. A bass on the roots, as in Hear the Bass First, keeps the colours anchored.
