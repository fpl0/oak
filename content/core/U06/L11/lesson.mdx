<Step id="S1">
In Hear the Bass First the bass sat on each chord's root. In this slow ballad the chords are plain triads; listen to what the bass does underneath them.

<Example id="hook" views="code,staff" autoplay />

<OnPass>The bass walks down a step at a time for seven bars, C all the way to D, then turns up for the last chord. The chords change as usual, but over that falling line the ballad seems to descend a staircase.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
In Inversions you put a chord's 3rd or 5th at the bottom of a stack. Here the stack is a chord symbol, and a separate `bass:` line plays the lowest note.

<Predict exercise="which-chord-now" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The example plays the same four chords with `.mode("root:g3")` and no bass line: every chord's root goes to the bottom, at or below G3, so the lowest note leaps. The editor holds the hook's version, to compare.

<Example id="roots-low" views="code,staff" />

<OnPass>One half step, C to B, and bar 4 stands on G. The chords above are the same as in root position, so the bass can walk instead of leap. Under G it plays another note of the chord, its 3rd, B. Under the second Am it plays G, which is not a note of the A minor chord: it adds one, and A C E with G below is a seventh chord standing on its 7th. Written as a chord name, a bass note that is not the root goes after a slash: C, G/B, Am, Am/G.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
This time the walk comes as a bass player gets it: on paper, with no code and no roll.

<Staff example="the-page" />

<Editor exercise="walk-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>`c3 b2 a2 g2`: one step down every bar, read straight off the bass clef. A falling bass under changing chords is the sound of a great many ballads.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Numerals can carry the bass too. A small 6 after the numeral says the chord's 3rd is at the bottom; 64 says its 5th is.

<OnPass>I, V6, vi. The 6 says V stands on its 3rd: G/B in C. The walk C B A reads I V6 vi in any major key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The bass can also do the opposite of walking.

<Choice exercise="the-bass-that-stays" />

<OnPass>Card B's bass plays C under C, F, G and C. The chords move; the bass stays. A bass note held or repeated while the chords above it change is a **pedal**. It builds tension under the moving chords until they come back to it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Now the other way round: the chord waits, and one voice moves.

<Example id="half-steps-down" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The bass slides C, B, B♭, A, one half step at a time, while C, E and G wait above it; then the chord moves to F over the A. Songwriters call moves like this **line clichés**: one voice sliding down by half steps through a held chord. They make one chord last for bars without standing still.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
The starter is the four-chord loop with its bass on every root. Walk the bass down, C, B, A, and the loop you know turns into a ballad. In Inversions you rearranged a stack to make the bottom walk; now a bass line does it under any chords, and a held pedal or a sliding line cliché can join in.
