<Step id="S1">
Your chords so far have held each bar, or broken into notes. Here the four-chord loop meets a drummer and a bass player, and the piano picks its moments.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The piano no longer fills the bar. It strikes with the snare, then once more just before the next bar, and leaves the rest to the drums and bass. The chords are the same; the loop has become a groove.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
`.struct()` takes a rhythm written like a drum line and plays the chord on every `x`.

<Predict exercise="count-the-hits" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The hook's chords follow the rhythm in `.struct()`. Swap in other rhythms and hear how each one sits with the drums.

<Example id="the-hook-again" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>`"~ x ~ x"` lands with the snare, beats 2 and 4. The hook's `"~ x ~ [~ x]"` hits twice: with the snare on 2, then half a beat after the snare on 4.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Hits with the snare stay out of the kick's way.

<Editor exercise="lock-to-the-snare" />

<OnPass>`"~ x ~ x"`: the chords answer the kick and hit with the snare, and the bar has space in it. Playing the chords in a rhythm that locks with the drums, behind the melody, is **comping**. Pianists and guitarists in soul, funk and jazz spend most of a song doing it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Comping is not only about the drums. A tune needs space too.

<Choice exercise="room-for-the-tune" />

<OnPass>With the chords on 2 and 4, most of the tune's notes start on beats the chords leave free. Fewer hits often make a part easier to hear, and the whole band clearer.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The last hit of the hook comes half a beat late, just before the bar line. It pulls you into the next bar.

<Editor exercise="push-into-the-next-bar" />

<OnPass>`"~ x ~ [~ x]"`: the second hit now lands on the "and" of 4, the last eighth of the bar. A push like this gives a groove its lean; funk and soul players use it all the time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
At the start of this unit a chord was a stack in a bar; now you choose its numeral, its job, its pace, its colour and its rhythm. Your rhythm plays the last two loud parts of your song, under Oak's tune, and Oak's kick and snare land in different places in each style, so listen before you choose. Room for the tune on top, and the hook's push into the next bar, both help.
