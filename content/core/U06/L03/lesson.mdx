<Step id="S1">
Last lesson gave every chord a numeral. This lesson asks what each one does, starting with I, IV, V and I.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The loop leaves home in bar 2 and is back in bar 4, just as the tune lands on 1. The road between is not flat: one bar leans harder than the others.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Some chords feel like a place to rest. Others make you lean forward, waiting for what comes next.

<Predict exercise="where-would-you-wait" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Run all seven chords of the key and listen for three things: rest, motion and pull.

<Example id="the-seven" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The job a chord does in a phrase is its **harmonic function**, and there are three. Home is I, with vi beside it: the two share two notes, and iii sits there too, though less settled. That job keeps the name tonic, your word for the home note. Away is IV and ii, the **predominant** job, since it comes before the tension. Tension is V and vii°, which pull towards I: the **dominant** job, named after V, the chord on degree 5.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
No sound this time: a numeral and a key. Decide its job from what you just heard.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.function" level={1} trials={8} />

<OnPass>Home, away or tension, read straight from the numeral with no sound at all. Next you hear what each job does to a tune.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A phrase that never leaves home has nowhere to come back from.

<Editor exercise="send-it-away" />

<OnPass>Home, away, tension, home: T, PD, D, T. IV and ii both work here, and both hold the tune's F and A.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now a whole loop, one job per bar.

<OnPass>I ii V I: tonic, predominant, dominant, tonic. A great many phrases take this path, from Bach chorales to pop songs.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your loops used to be lists of chords; now each chord has a job, and you can plan the road home. The starter goes home, away, home, away, and never builds any tension. V brings that pull, through its B, the leading tone from Leading Tone & Six.

</Step>
