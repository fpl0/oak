<Step id="S1">
In Hear the Bass First you took down a bass line. Today you take down the chords above it too, one layer at a time.

<OnPass>You already hold two of the three layers: a bass note under each bar, and a colour, bright or dark, on each chord. Together they are enough to name the loop.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The same loop, now to take down. Each pass opens when the one before is right, and the chord band fills in as you go.

<OnPass>Bass 1 3 6 4, then major, minor, minor, major, so I iii vi IV: in C, that is C, Em, Am, F. You never named a chord directly. The bass and the colour named it for you.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now in code. The bass first, then the chords, and switch between the loop and yours at the same bar until they match.

<Editor exercise="by-ear-bass-first" />

<OnPass>I V vi iii: C, G, Am, Em. The bass told you every root; the chords only had to be built on them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
New loops, new keys, and only the numerals to write. The bass and the colours now happen in your head.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.progression" level={1} trials={4} />

<OnPass>Whole loops by ear, in any key. Bass plus quality gives the numeral every time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Three lessons ago a bass line by ear was the whole task. Now a whole loop comes off the recording, chords and all, and you can turn it into a track. A rhythm for the chords, a bass driven as in Hear the Bass First and a tune on top can make it a track.

<Make exercise="make-it-a-track" />

<Recap />
</Step>
