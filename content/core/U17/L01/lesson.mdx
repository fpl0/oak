<Step id="S1">
Every chord from outside the key has been a visitor so far: the music soon went home. Today a visitor stays. Here are two verses on the Rhodes over an upright bass.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The first G was a pause: it still wanted to go on to C. After D7 the same G sounded like home. One chord in bar 7 moved the centre of the whole verse.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Bar 7's new chord is one you know from Five of Five, where it leaned into G.

<Predict exercise="the-new-note" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The second verse again. The tune's `.scale()` holds a plan: six bars of C major, then two of G major. The chord band reads every bar in C.

<Example id="the-door" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Am belongs to both keys. Bar 7 brings F♯, the one note G major has and C major lacks. In Five of Five the same D7 was a visit, and the music went straight back to C; here it stays. Changing key in the middle of a piece, so that a new note sounds like home, is a **modulation**. A chord both keys own, used as the door between them, is a **pivot chord**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The door has two names, one on each side of it.

<OnPass>vi in C major, ii in G major. In C, Am is the minor chord on 6; in G it is the chord before V, the one that usually leads to D7. That second name is why D7 can follow it so smoothly.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Run this one first and listen to bar 7.

<Editor exercise="write-the-door" />

<OnPass>C, Am, then D7 and G: the door, G's own V7, and the new home. The tune and the chords now agree on where home is.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now a key change read from the page, with no code for the tune and no roll. It starts in F major, one flat, and the B♮ in bar 3 tells you the key has moved.

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>F, Dm, G7, C. Dm is vi in F and ii in C, and G7 is C's V7: the same door as in C to G, a 5th lower. Any move to the key a 5th up can use the same door: the old vi is the new ii.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
This is the verse from the start of the lesson: it goes round twice and never leaves C. You know every piece the move needs: a pivot chord, the V7 of Five of Five, and a key plan in the tune's `.scale()`. Once it lands in G, try another door, a new tune or other sounds.

<Make exercise="a-verse-that-leaves-home" />

<Callout kind="tip">Am is not the only door from C to G. C, Em and G belong to both keys too: try Em before D7 and hear how the move changes colour.</Callout>

You can now take a song into a new key through a chord both keys share, and write the key plan that keeps its tune in step. Next: the key change that needs no door at all, the last chorus that lifts.

</Step>
