<Step id="S1">
Last lesson a chord both keys share led the music into its new key. Pop records often skip the door. You know this from the last minute of many of them: the chorus has been sung, and it comes round once more.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>At bar 9 everything moved up a whole step at once. The same chorus a step higher feels bigger, though not one note of it is new.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">

<Predict exercise="two-keys-one-tune" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The whole chorus, twice. The bass and the tune are written once, in numbers, and their `.scale()` says which key the numbers mean. The chords are written by name, so the lifted bars need new names.

<Example id="chorus-lift" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>C G Am F, then D A Bm G. Every chord, every bass note and every note of the tune rose two half steps, with no chord in between to prepare it. A key change that arrives without warning is a **direct modulation**. When it lifts a last chorus by a half step or a whole step, players call it the truck-driver's gear change. In the code the numbers did not move; the key did.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Something is left behind at bar 5 of this one. Hear it before you fix it.

<Editor exercise="lift-the-tune" />

<OnPass>`<C4:major!4 D4:major!4>`: one tune, two keys, each held for four bars. That string is the key plan of the line.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A whole step is the wide lift; some songs take the narrow one. Hear both before you choose for your own song.

<Choice exercise="half-step-or-whole-step" />

<OnPass>Card 2: C to D♭, a half step. Card 1 is the whole step you wrote. The half step is the smaller jump but the stranger one: D♭ major shares only two notes with C major, F and C, where D major shares five.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now the song from the start of this lesson, in sections. A key plan counts the bars of the whole song. A section inside `arrange()` starts again from its own first bar each time it plays, so a plan written inside a section would never reach its second key.

<Editor exercise="after-arrange" />

<OnPass>Bare numbers inside the sections, one key plan after `arrange()`: `<C4:major!8 D4:major!4>`, eight bars of C and four of D. The chords cannot follow a plan, because a chord name is not a number. That is why the lifted chorus has its own section, `chordsChorus2`, with every name moved up.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The whole song sits in C, and `chordsChorus2` is still a copy of the chorus. A lift is also a good moment for a new part to come in, the way parts have entered your arrangements since Arranging in Code. Once the lift works, try a new chorus tune, strings on a `pad:` line for the lift, or a longer song.
