<Step id="S1">
You have changed key twice in code, once through a door and once with a lift. Now the other side of it: hearing a song do it. The first test is the simplest there is, and it needs only your voice.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>By the end of both pieces C had stopped sounding like home. The chorus moved home up a whole step at bar 5. The verse found a new home on A, after a G♯ in bar 7.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Now the same test on two verses from Pivot Chords, with nothing on screen.

<Choice exercise="home-or-away" />

<OnPass>Card 1 moves. D7 brings F♯ in bar 7, the verse stops on G, and C sounds like a stranger afterwards. Card 2 goes the same way for six bars, then comes home. That is the first question, and the one your ear answers before anything else: is the old home still home at the end?</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Movable do means what it says. When home moves, do moves with it.

<Sing exercise="sing-home-twice" />

<OnPass>Do before, do after: C, then G. The second do is the same note the verse ends on, and nothing about singing it feels foreign. That is what a modulation does to your ear.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The second question: where. A lift starts the phrase again higher, and the bar where it starts is where the new key begins. Some of these pieces never change key at all.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.modulation" level={1} trials={4} />

<OnPass>Hum the bass, feel the jump, mark the bar. The drill keeps coming back in your warm-ups, and later it adds moves through a door.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The third question: how. A chord from outside the key is not yet a key change. The music has to stay where it went.

<Choice exercise="how-did-it-get-there" />

<OnPass>A visit, a door, a lift. In piece 1, D7 went to G for a bar and came home. In piece 2, Am opened the door and E7 kept the music in A minor. Piece 3 rose a half step with nothing in between. Three questions, in order: is home still home, where did the first new note arrive, and was there a door?</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your code has a verse and a bridge, and the bridge sounds like more verse. In your song only your bridge plays: it takes the eight bars after the second verse, where Oak's bridge already plays in A minor, with E7 right before Am. The nearest new home is A minor, the relative minor, and the way in is the major V you built in Raise the Seventh.

<Make exercise="a-bridge-somewhere-else" />

<Callout kind="tip">Am is the door here, as it was from C to G: it is vi in C and i in A minor. When the loop comes round, the bridge's last Am leads back into C with no help, because C major owns it too.</Callout>

<CanDo id="cd.hear.key-change" />

In code you could already move a song between keys. Now you can hear one do it: whether home moved, where, and whether it went through a door. Next: writing a tune down through its key change, in a pop chorus and in a ballad shaped like the standards of 1930.

</Step>
