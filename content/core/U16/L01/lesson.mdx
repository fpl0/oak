<Step id="S1">
In the last unit every chord from outside the key leaned into the chord after it. Today's leans nowhere. It changes the light instead, as it does at the end of many ballads.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Bar 3 darkened for a moment, and the tune's long notes fell a half step at a time: A, A♭, G, into home. Nothing was pulled toward a new chord. The light dimmed and came back.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
In code the two loops differ by one letter in the chord string.

<Predict exercise="f-becomes-f-minor" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The two chords on their own, one a bar.

<Example id="two-fours" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>F A C became F A♭ C: one note, down a half step. C major has no A♭, so F minor is not one of its chords. C minor has it, and there F minor is the chord on degree 4. A chord taken from the minor key on the same home note and used in major is a **borrowed chord**. This one is written iv, lower case because it is minor.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the same turn in G major, in code.

<Editor exercise="turn-the-second-one" />

<OnPass>G, C, C minor, G: I, IV, iv, I. The E in the C chord dropped to E♭, and the loop comes home through the shade. You have turned a plain ending into the sad one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The numeral does not change its number when the chord is borrowed. Only its case does.

<OnPass>I IV iv I. Same root, same degree 4; upper case for the key's own major chord, lower case for the minor one it borrows.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The note that moves is the whole effect. Sing it, and the way it goes home.

<Sing exercise="la-le-sol" />

<OnPass>La, le, sol over IV, iv and I. Le does not belong to the key, and it does not stay: it falls a half step into sol, the 5th of the home chord. That fall is what you hear as the ache.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
This loop is bright all the way through, and you choose where it aches. Since Chromatic Degrees you can bend a note down a half step in code, so the tune can lean into the sad IV as well.

<Make exercise="your-sad-turn" />

<CanDo id="cd.compose.borrowed-iv" />

One lowered note, and a bright loop darkens exactly where you choose. Next: two more chords from the same minor key, and the ending that climbs home from below.

<Recap />
</Step>
