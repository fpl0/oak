<Step id="S3">
Home, the new chord, IV and the sad IV, each held for a bar so you can read every note.

<Example id="four-chords" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Of E♭'s three notes only G belongs to C major. E♭ is C minor's chord on the lowered 3rd, written ♭III, and F minor is C minor's too. With ♭VI and ♭VII you now have four of C minor's chords for a C major song: iv, ♭VI, ♭VII and ♭III. Mixing the chords of a major key with those of its parallel minor is **modal mixture**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the riff's shape in D major, read from the page. There is no code for the tune and no roll: only the staff and its first chord.

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>D, F, G, G minor: I, ♭III, IV, iv in D major. The tune's C natural sat inside the F chord and its B♭ inside G minor, the same shape as the riff in C. You read four bars of mixture off the page.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The numerals carry the borrowing: a flat on the root, and the case of the chord.

<OnPass>I, ♭III, IV, iv. The flat says the root is a lowered degree; the case says major or minor, as always.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Three ways into home. V pulls up; IV and iv both fall, and only one of them is minor.

<OnPass>V to I, IV to I, and iv to I twice. iv to I is the minor plagal cadence, the close you first wrote with the sad IV, now with a name. In IV to I, la falls a whole step to sol; in iv to I, le falls only a half step, the ache you sang in The Sad IV.</OnPass>
</Step>
