<Step id="S1">
A folk tune slowed into a soul ballad: Aura Lee's verse on a tenor sax. If you took it down in Folk Transcription, you wrote its plain chords yourself. Today the tune stays exactly as it is, and the chords under it change.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Not one note of the sax changed, yet the verse sounded new. D7 in bar 2 leaned into G7, and the A♭ and B♭ under the long C of bar 4 changed its colour twice before home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
A new chord works under a tune when the tune's long notes belong to it.

<Predict exercise="three-ds" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The new version, on the page.

<Example id="the-hook-on-the-page" views="code,staff" />

<OnPass>D7 is a secondary dominant: it leans into G7, a 5th below. A♭ and B♭ are borrowed from C minor. They lean nowhere and change the light for half a bar each. The tune is the same in every bar. Putting new chords under a tune without changing the tune is **reharmonization**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now by ear, on the verse's second phrase. The bass is your way in, as it has been since Hear the Bass First.

<Editor exercise="borrow-by-ear" />

<OnPass>C, B♭, G, then C and F minor in one bar: I, ♭VII, V, I and iv. B♭ holds the tune's three Ds as its 3rd; F minor holds the long C as its 5th and lets the bar sigh before the loop comes round.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Each borrowed chord has a colour and a bass of its own. Use both.

<OnPass>That is the ear the lab needs: a chord from outside the key, found by its colour and named by its bass.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Aura Lee's verse again. The tune is fixed; the chords are yours. You can put a secondary dominant or a chain of them under it, any of the four borrowed chords, or the climb ♭VI ♭VII I.

<Make exercise="your-reharmonization" />

<CanDo id="cd.compose.reharmonize" />

<CanDo id="cd.hear.chromatic" />

A tune like this once came with one set of plain chords. Now you can give it others from outside the key, and tell by ear a chord that leans from one that only changes the light. Next: the key itself moves, and stays somewhere new.

<Recap />
</Step>
