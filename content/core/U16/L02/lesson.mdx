<Step id="S3">
Here is the climb on its own, one chord a bar, slow enough to read.

<Example id="the-climb" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three major chords, each root a whole step above the last. Both new chords come from C minor, like the sad IV. A♭ stands on the lowered 6th and is written ♭VI. B♭ stands on the lowered 7th and is written ♭VII, the chord you played in Mixolydian Rock. No leading note pulls up into home, and that is why the ending sounds wide open rather than tight.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the climb in G major, in code.

<Editor exercise="climb-home-in-g" />

<OnPass>E♭, F, G: ♭VI, ♭VII, I in G major. The tune climbed do re mi on top, the 3rd of each chord in turn. You can write the climb in any key now.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Away from the code: the same chord on the keys, in D major.

<OnPass>B♭, D, F: the major chord on ♭6 of D major. The key's own chord on 6 is B minor, B D F♯: two of its three notes differ from B♭ major, so ♭VI is not vi with one note moved.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The bass of the climb is a tune in its own right.

<Sing exercise="le-te-do" />

<OnPass>Le, te, do under ♭VI, ♭VII and I. The bass climbs from outside the key straight into home, and the ear hears it arrive.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
