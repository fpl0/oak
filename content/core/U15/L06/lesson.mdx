<Step id="S1">
From here on you will meet these chords the way a player does: on a record, with nothing on the screen. The standard you are taking down leans out of its key in just this way. Today you catch each lean by ear and name it by where it goes.

<OnPass>The leans came in bars 2 and 6. Both times the harmony stepped outside the key for one bar and came straight back, the way every visitor of this unit does.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The method has three moves: hear the note that does not belong, name it (fi, si and di lean up, te leans down), and follow it into the next bar, where its target arrives. These loops are made up for you, a new set each time.

<Choice exercise="find-the-bar" />

<OnPass>Each time, the bar that leaned sat right before a bar that sounded like an arrival. That pairing is what you listen for: a chord a shade outside the key, then the chord it was pointing at. Oak will bring these loops back in your warm-ups.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now the second and third moves. The bar is given; the question is where the visitor points. Name the outside note, then follow it.

<Choice exercise="name-the-visitor" />

<OnPass>Si into la in G: the V7 of vi, B7 into E minor. Di into re in F: the V7 of ii, D7 into G minor. Te down to la in B♭: the V7 of IV, B♭7 into E♭. One note, one direction, one target.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the ballad from the start of the lesson, written down in the order of Chord Dictation Workshop. A numeral with a slash marks a chord aimed somewhere other than home.

<OnPass>Bass 1 6 2 5; I, V7/ii, ii, V7. The bass gave you every root. On degree 6 the key's own chord is A minor, but bar 2's chord was major with a 7th. Its C♯ rose into the D of bar 3, so it is the V7 of ii.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The two visitors of the ballad each bring one note. Sing each one with the step it takes.

<Sing exercise="sing-them-home" />

<OnPass>Di to re, a half step up; te to la, a half step down. The first is the C♯ of A7 resolving into D minor, the second the B♭ of C7 resolving into F.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
This is Oak's staircase of sevenths, like the one you built in Diatonic Sevenths in Major, and it plays your song's solo, the eight bars after the first chorus. Every chord in it belongs to the key, which is why it never leans. A leading-tone chord from Tonicization works as a door too.

<Make exercise="open-two-doors" />

<Callout kind="tip">A door does not need a whole bar. `[Em7 C7]` gives Em7 two beats and the door two more, and the staircase keeps its shape.</Callout>

At the start of this unit a note from outside the key was a bend in a tune. Now it can carry a chord, and you can find that chord by ear and say where it is aimed, in any major key. Next comes the other way to colour a key from outside: chords taken from its minor, starting with the sad IV.
