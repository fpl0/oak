<Step id="S1">
Your blues tunes bent ♭3 and ♭7 for colour, and the modes gave ♭7 and ♯4 a home of their own. Today the key stays plain major, and notes from outside it only visit. A singer does this all the time, sliding into a note from a half step away.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Each bent note sat a half step from the note after it and slid straight in. A half step is the smallest move there is, so a note that close to its neighbour sounds as if it were already on its way there.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
In code a bent note keeps the number you know and takes a sign after it.

<Predict exercise="sol-something-sol" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The same hook, with every note named on the roll.

<Example id="hook-labelled" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Te falls to la, and ♯5 is written `4#`. A raised note leans up into the next degree; a lowered one leans down. A note from outside the key, named by the degree it bends, is a **chromatic degree**. In code the number is still the step, one less than the degree, and the sign after it does the bending.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now bend two notes yourself. In bar 2 the tune's B grinds against the B♭ of C7; hear what the target does with it.

<Editor exercise="fi-and-te" />

<OnPass>`3#` and `6b`: fi climbs back to sol, te falls to la, and bar 2 stops grinding, because te is the B♭ that C7 already holds.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now let the pull work for you. A bent note wants to go somewhere; sol and do are content to stay.

<Choice exercise="bent-or-not" />

<OnPass>Fi leans up into sol, te leans down into la. These two come back in your warm-ups, joined later by me, le, ra, si and di.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Two leans, one a bar. Sing them and you will feel where each note wants to go.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-two-leans" />

<OnPass>Sol fi sol, do te la. Fi pulled you back up to sol; te let you down onto la.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A soul turn, a bass on its roots and a plain tune: this loop plays the intro of your song, under Oak's drums. Bend the tune the way a singer would. You have bent a line this way before: the chromatic approach notes of Walking Up to It made the same half-step lean, down in the bass.

<Make exercise="bend-a-tune" />

<Callout kind="tip">A bent note sounds best a half step from where it lands, on its way there. Bend a note that is about to move to its neighbour.</Callout>
