<Step id="S1">
Last lesson every visitor arrived with the bass falling a 5th. A gospel pianist has another way in: walk the bass up into the next chord, a half step at a time.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Under A7 the bass fell a 5th into D. In the walk-up it climbed C, C♯, D, then F♯, G, G♯, A, and a tight chord rode each half step. A bass note a half step below its target pulls up the way a leading tone does.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The tight chord over the climbing bass is one you built in Half-Diminished & Diminished 7.

<Predict exercise="take-the-root-away" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Three chords side by side: the visitor you know, the chord from the walk-up, and D minor, where both of them lead.

<Example id="three-stacks" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A7 and C♯°7 share C♯, E and G, and C♯ is D's leading tone. So C♯°7 is D minor's own leading-tone chord: built a half step below D, it lets the bass climb into D instead of falling. Making a chord sound like home for a moment, through its own V7 or its own leading-tone chord, is **tonicization**. Home has not moved: two bars later C is home again.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build one on the keys, aimed at G.

<OnPass>F♯, A, C, E♭: three half steps between each note and the next. F♯ is G's leading tone, the note that climbs into G.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the walk-up, where two bars still wait for their push.

<Editor exercise="two-more-pushes" />

<OnPass>`F#o7` into G7 and `G#o7` into Am7: the bass climbs F♯ G G♯ A, and each chord arrives a little like a home. If you wrote `Gbo7` or `Abo7`, they are the same keys; the sharp names say which way the root is going.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Both pushes do the same job. Your ears can still tell them apart, if you listen low.

<Choice exercise="which-push" />

<OnPass>V7, then the leading-tone chord twice. A V7 arrives with the bass falling a 5th; a leading-tone chord arrives with the bass climbing a half step.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
You have two ways into a chord now: its V7 from a 5th above, its leading-tone chord from a half step below. This organ turn waits two bars on each of its first three chords, room for a push into the next one. A line on top, landing on chord notes as in Landing on Chord Tones, is welcome too.

<Make exercise="your-own-walk-up" />

<Callout kind="tip">A push does not need a whole bar. `[C C#o7]` holds C for two beats and climbs into Dm7 in the last two.</Callout>

<CanDo id="cd.write.tonicization" />

You can now make a chord sound like home for a moment from below, with its own diminished seventh, and tell that push from a V7 by the bass. Next: a dance band of 1925, horns and a trumpet, and dominants that lean into dominants.
