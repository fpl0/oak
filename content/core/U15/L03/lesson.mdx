<Step id="S1">
In Five of Five one chord leaned into V. A soul ballad does the same to other chords of the key.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Three bars leaned, and each time the next chord sounded like a small home of its own: A minor, then F, then D minor. Then the turn went on in C as if nothing had happened.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Bar 2 of the visited turn holds E7, a chord C major does not own.

<Predict exercise="where-e7-lands" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The visited turn again, with every visitor's notes on the roll.

<Example id="hook-chords" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A7 brings C♯, di, a half step under D; E7 does the same for A with si. Three visitors in eight bars, and G7 is the key's own V7. C7 works the other way round. Its 3rd, E, is already in C major. Its outside note is the 7th, B♭, te, which falls a half step onto the A of F.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build the first visitor yourself, on the keys.

<OnPass>E, G♯, B, D: the key's own E minor seventh with G raised to G♯, the leading tone of A. It is written V7/vi and said "five-seven of six".</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The same three doors in G major, read from the page. Each accidental in the tune is a note a visitor brings, on its way into the next bar.

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>G, B7, Em, G7, C, E7, Am, D7. Each accidental moved a half step into the next bar: D♯ up into E, F♮ down onto E, G♯ up into A.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No code and no roll now: only each visitor and the bar it lands in.

<Choice exercise="aimed-at-which-chord" />

<OnPass>Si into A minor, di into D minor, te down into F. The note the visitor brings tells you where it is going.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The ballad turn from the start of the lesson, on the key's own chords. Five of Five gave you one door, into V; this turn has three more. It plays both choruses of your song, with Oak's drums and horns around it.

<Make exercise="three-doors" />

<Callout kind="tip">When a visitor arrives, the tune's old note often rubs against it. Give the tune the visitor's note and the rub becomes a lean.</Callout>

<CanDo id="cd.write.applied-chords" />

You can now aim a dominant seventh at vi, ii or IV, not only at home and V, and hear where it lands. Next: the changes of your standard; then a gospel organ walk-up that climbs into each chord from a half step below.

<Recap />
</Step>
