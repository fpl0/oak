<Step id="S1">
You can aim a V7 at V, vi, ii or IV now. Aim one at another V7, and that one at another, and the harmony tumbles home. Two songs from 1925, played by a band of the time. If you chose Five Foot Two for your transcription, you may know the first already.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Every chord before C was a dominant seventh, and each one resolved only into another that wanted to move on. The tension never let go until home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Under the guitar and the horns, a chain has a thread you met in Seventh Chords: the 3rd and the 7th of each chord. Here they are on their own, over the bass.

<Example id="thread" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Both voices fall one half step at every change. The 3rd of each chord becomes the 7th of the next, and its 7th becomes the next 3rd: G♯ slides to G, D to C♯. While the roots walk the circle, E A D G, the guide tones slide down a half step at a time. That slide is why a chain sounds as if it were rolling downhill.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Oak's numerals name each chord by its job, so each link says where it is going.

<OnPass>V7/vi, V7/ii, V7/V, V7, I: every chord the V7 of the next, and only the last two belong to the key. A chart's III7 VI7 II7 V7 says where each chord stands; Oak's numerals say where each one goes.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Sweet Georgia Brown's chain, in F. On the record each link lasts four bars, which is what makes the chain feel so patient. Here each lasts two.

<Editor exercise="sweet-georgias-chain" />

<OnPass>D7, G7, C7, F. Each link leans a 5th down into the next, and the chain lands on home in bar 7. Worked backwards it is the same question each time: what is the V7 of the chord after me?</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now a chain by ear, and only its bottom line.

<OnPass>1, 3, 6, 2, 5, 1: do, mi, la, re, sol, do. After 1 up to 3, each root lies a 5th below the one before; where the bass climbs a 4th instead, it reaches the same note an octave higher. All six bass notes belong to the key. What leaves the key is the 3rd of each chord above them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
This turn's roots already fall by 5ths, from E down to C; it only needs to lean. The horn stabs work the way you comped in Comping Patterns. Your loop plays the ending of your song, the eight bars before its last chord.
