<Step id="S1">
You can find any white key by its letter. Today the letters go on paper. First, four notes, slow enough to follow.

<Example id="hook" views="code" autoplay />

<OnPass>Each note skips one white key: `e4 g4 b4 d5`, every other letter. Written down, that even climb takes a shape you can see.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The same four notes, written the way musicians have written them for centuries. Each note sits on a line, and each one sits a line higher than the last: the paper climbs as the sound does.

<Staff example="climb-on-paper" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Code, sound and picture are linked: change a letter or a number and the note moves up or down the lines.

<OnPass>Those five lines are the **staff**, five lines like the ones your drums sat on in Seeing Rhythm. Here each line and space is a pitch: the higher a note sits, the higher it sounds. The curly sign at the start is the **treble clef**. It curls around the second line from the bottom and fixes that line as `g4`; every other line and space follows from it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Read the page: lines and spaces alternate, one letter each, from the bottom line `e4` upward.

<Staff example="three-to-name" />

<Choice exercise="name-three-notes" />

<OnPass>g, e, c. The last note, `c4`, does not fit on the staff, so it gets a short line of its own: a **ledger line**. Ledger lines extend the staff above and below, one line per two letters.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now write: from the name to the page.

<StaffInput exercise="write-c-e-g-c" />

<OnPass>`c4 e4 g4 c5`: ledger line, bottom line, second line, third space. They are the notes of the band's first chord, and you will meet them in nearly every tune in C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Play from the page. No code to read, no roll: only the staff, the way a musician gets a part.

<Staff example="the-page" />

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>`e4 g4 f4 a4`: line, line, space, space. Reading music is this, a note at a time, until the shapes become familiar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
This lesson began with a picture you could not read; now you can write into it. A melody that stays between the bottom line `e4` and the top line `f5` can be read straight back from the page. `@` holds a note longer, as it held drum hits in Seeing Rhythm, and the staff draws the longer note in its own way.

</Step>
