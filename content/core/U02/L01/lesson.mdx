<Step id="S1">
For a whole unit you placed drum hits in time. Today a piano plays over the band, and its notes climb and fall.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Three heights, up and back down, and the code calls every one of them `c`. Low or high, they sound strangely alike.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Two of those heights now, each followed by a rest. The letter stays the same; only the number differs.

<Predict exercise="which-is-higher" hum />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
`note("…")` plays the notes you name, and `.s("piano")` picks the instrument. <CodeRef fn="note" />

<Example id="three-heights" views="code,roll" />

<Callout kind="tip">Every note name in Oak carries a number. A `c` on its own would quietly pick a height for you, and Oak asks you to choose.</Callout>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The letter never changes here, so the number must be doing the work.

<OnPass>How high or low a note sounds is its **pitch**. In `c4`, the number sets the height: the bigger the number, the higher the pitch.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
This line sits low, down near the bass.

<Editor exercise="lift-the-line" />

<OnPass>The same line, one **octave** higher: every `c` moved up to the next `c`. The two sound so alike that when men and women sing a tune together, they usually sing it an octave apart and hear one tune.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No code and no roll now: only your ears.

<EarDrill family="fam.pit.compare" level={1} trials={6} />

<Callout kind="tip">Humming helps. Your voice already knows whether it had to go up or down.</Callout>

<OnPass>Higher, lower or the same, by ear alone. Every melody you write from now on rests on that judgement.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A riff is a short line that repeats. Yours opens your song: it plays the first four bars, while the band comes in under it. Oak's tune there jumps between `c4`, `c5` and `c6`, and yours takes its place. It has only `c` to work with, so its life comes from the heights you choose and from the rests you leave, as your drum loops got theirs. Until today a sound you wrote had only a place in time; now it has a height too.
