<Step id="S1">
So far your ears judged and your hands wrote. Today your voice joins in. A call comes first, then room for an answer.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Two notes, up from `c4` to `e4`, then a bar to give them back. Musicians learn tunes this way long before they read them: hear, hold, sing back.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Start with a single note.

<Sing exercise="match-one-note" />

<Callout kind="tip">Any vowel works: "ah" or "oo". If the note sits too high or too low for you, sing the same note in a comfortable octave; Oak counts it.</Callout>

<OnPass>One note heard, held and matched. Every echo you sing is made of this, one note at a time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The notes sit closer together than in High & Low, so the difference is smaller. Trust the direction you feel.

<EarDrill family="fam.pit.compare" level={2} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Up, down or the same, even when the two notes are neighbours. That sense of direction is what your voice follows when it echoes.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now longer calls: two notes, then three. Let each call finish before you answer.

<Sing exercise="echo-the-call" />

<Callout kind="tip">No microphone today? Each call becomes a listening question instead, and nothing is lost.</Callout>

<OnPass>Two echoes: one that climbs, one that steps down through three notes. A short tune held in your head long enough to give it back.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
What you can sing back, you can write down. Your voice already knows which way the line goes.

<Editor exercise="write-the-echo" />

<OnPass>`e4 d4 c4`: three white keys in a row, walking down to `c4`. You heard it, sang it and wrote it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your voice is now part of the loop: you can hear a call, sing it back and write it down. Your call fills the quiet part of your song, the eight bars before the last loud part, where the band drops back and then builds. In Oak's version a second line echoes each call in the next bar; in yours, the echo is you. A call short enough to remember, placed where your voice is comfortable, is one you can echo. `< >` gives the call one bar and your echo the next.

<Make exercise="a-call-you-can-sing" />

<Recap />
</Step>
