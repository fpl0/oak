<Step id="S1">
You can read single notes off the staff. Now a whole tune: Beethoven wrote this one in 1824 for the last movement of his Ninth Symphony. Here are its first four bars, in a plain rhythm, on the staff and the roll at once.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Bar 4 stops on `d4`, and the tune sounds unfinished, as if it needs one more note. Beethoven's next four bars end on `c4` instead, and that ending sounds finished.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The opening tune ended with something new: `.room(.4)`. Here is a short line with the room turned off. <CodeRef fn="room" />

<OnPass>That tail is **reverb**: the sound bouncing off the walls of a room, so many echoes so close together that they blur into one fading wash. More `.room()` gives more of that tail, and the piano seems to sit farther away in a larger space.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">

<Callout kind="trap">Inside `< >` each item is one bar, and `@` outside brackets stretches an item over more bars: `g4@4` on its own lasts four bars. Keep the whole note in its brackets, `[g4@4]`.</Callout>

<OnPass>The first phrase of the Saints, from the page: three quick notes, then a long `g4` that fills the bar, twice.</OnPass>

<Step id="S5">
The page says how long a note lasts on paper. How long it rings is your choice. <CodeRef fn="clip" />

<OnPass>`.clip(.5)` cuts each note to half its length, so the tune sounds detached and bouncy; `.clip(1)` joins the notes into a smooth line. Same notes, same staff, a different character.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
At the start of this unit every sound you wrote was a drum. Now you can read a tune off the page and decide how it sounds. Your tune ends your song: it plays the eight bars of the ending, where the drums drop to half-time, and then the band lands on one last chord. In Oak's version the riff from the opening comes back there, in a bigger room. A doubling line from Tones & Timbres, or a copy moved with `.transpose()`, is welcome too.
