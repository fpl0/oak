<Step id="S1">
Five lessons gave you four new colours, each one note away from major or from minor. Here all six stand in a row on one home note.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Brightest to darkest, a bar each: Lydian, major, Mixolydian, Dorian, minor, Phrygian. The first three bars sound bright and the last three dark.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Bar 3 still sounds bright, bar 4 does not. Something crossed from the major side to the minor side.

<Predict exercise="one-note-down" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The same six bars with note names on the roll. Read them as a ladder.

<Example id="ladder" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>One note a bar, every time: G♯, C♯, F♯, B and E each drop a half step in turn. Lydian is the brightest, with its raised 4; Phrygian the darkest, with its lowered 2. Major, Mixolydian and Lydian have a major 3rd; minor, Dorian and Phrygian a minor one. Once you know home, that 3rd is the first thing to listen for.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Before you can name a colour, you need its home.

<Sing exercise="sing-home" />

<OnPass>Loop 1 rests on A: A Mixolydian. Loop 2 rests on E: E Dorian. Both are made of the same seven notes, the notes of D major, with another home. Only home decides which colour you hear. The four modes of this unit can all be played on the white keys, on G, D, F and E.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the ear on its own.

<Choice exercise="name-the-colour" />

<OnPass>Six colours, named by ear. A unit ago you heard two, major and minor; every other one turned out to be a plain scale with a single note moved.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Two small tools before the vamp, both about where a sound sits between your ears.

<Example id="wide-vamp" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The right. `.jux(rev)` plays the line as written on the left and the backwards copy on the right, so one riff fills both sides. One thing to avoid: a `.pan()` written after `.jux()` on the same line pulls both copies to one place, and the width is gone.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The vamp sits on D and could be three different modes; choose the one you want and make it obvious. It ends your song in place of Oak's Dorian vamp, after sections that all sit on the white keys, each around its own home. Then bring in what the last units gave you: seventh chords by name, a pushed chord, the width of `.jux(rev)`.

<Make exercise="your-modal-vamp" />

You can now name Mixolydian, Dorian, Lydian and Phrygian by ear and write a vamp in any of them. Next: notes from outside the key, sung and bent; then the modes checkpoint, and Modal Vamp, where a vamp like this one becomes a sixteen-bar track on your album.
