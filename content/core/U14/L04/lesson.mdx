<Step id="S1">
The colours so far were rock riffs and soul grooves. This one is a sound often heard in film scores: one held bass note, a slow pad, a line with an echo.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the second half the music lifts and stays up, as if it never quite lands. The pad's second chord brightened too, though the bass never left F. One raised note did both.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">

<Step id="S3">
The pad changed in the second half too.

<Example id="degree-two" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>B♭ became B, and the chord on degree 2 turned from G minor into G major, written II. A major scale with its 4th raised a half step is **Lydian**. Its raised 4th sits a half step under 5 and leans up into it instead of weighing on 3, so the music never quite lands: it floats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the scale itself, on the degree pad.

<OnPass>1 2 3 ♯4 5 6 7: six notes shared with F major and one that is not. The ♯4 is Lydian's characteristic tone, the note that makes it float, as ♭7 is Mixolydian's.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the pad piece, in F major again.

<Editor exercise="make-it-float" />

<OnPass>One word and one chord: F to G and back over the held F, a pedal, with B in the line. I to II over one bass note is the Lydian shimmer. The bass never moves, so in bar 2 you hear G major floating above F.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The colour is not tied to F, and there are now two ways to bend major. Listen to the middle of the scale and to the top.

<Choice exercise="which-colour" />

<OnPass>A raised 4th floats: Lydian. A lowered 7th leans back: Mixolydian. Major has neither. Both modes are major with one note changed, on opposite sides of the scale.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Let the raised 4th ring: a note held across the beat with `@`, as in Ties Across the Beat, gives the ear time to float. Your loop plays the opening of your song, the eight bars before the first verse, in place of Oak's pad, held F and floating tune. Oak's drums leave it room: a cymbal on bar 1, and the beat only from bar 5.
