<Step id="S1">
Mixolydian, Dorian and Lydian each moved one note. The last of the four colours is the darkest. An acoustic guitar strums three-three-two under a riff.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The second half turns darker and tenser, and the riff's returns to home land harder, as if pushed down onto it. That colour runs through flamenco guitar and many metal riffs.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Same numbers, a new scale under them. This time the change is on the minor side.

<Predict exercise="which-note-moves" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The chord under the riff moved too.

<Example id="degree-two" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>F♯ became F, and the diminished chord on degree 2 turned into F major. A minor scale with its 2nd lowered a half step is **Phrygian**. That ♭2 is its characteristic tone, a half step above home. Major's leading tone pulls up into home from below; this note falls into it from above, and that fall is the weight you heard.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The numerals of the loop. Minor-side modes are written as minor is, so the home chord is i.

<OnPass>i ♭II i ♭II. The ♭ says the root sits a half step lower than minor's own 2nd; upper case says the chord on it is major. Two chords a half step apart, and the top one falls back onto home every time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the riff, in plain E minor again.

<Editor exercise="give-it-the-edge" />

<OnPass>One word and one chord: the riff's F♯s became Fs, and F major sits under the second bar. The F in the riff and the F chord under it press on the E that follows.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
With Phrygian you have all six colours: three on the major side, three on the minor side. Each one is a scale you know with at most one note changed.

<Choice exercise="all-six-colours" />

<OnPass>Home, then the 3rd, then the one note. That order narrows six colours to three, and then to one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Give the riff its edge first. In your song it plays the bridge, the eight bars before the last chorus, in place of Oak's chords on E and F and its riff. After that, the strum's three-three-two can take the rhythm unit's tools: a push with `.early()`, or quieter hits with `.velocity()`.

You can make a Phrygian sound with ♭2 over i and ♭II, and you now own four colours beyond major and minor. Next: the lab, where you name any of the six by ear and write a vamp in the one you like best.
