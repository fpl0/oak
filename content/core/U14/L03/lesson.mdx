<Step id="S1">
Mixolydian was major with one note moved. This lesson makes the same kind of move on minor.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The second half is still minor, but lighter, with air in it, and its second chord brightens too. The numbers never changed: one note of the scale did all of that.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The groove lifted, and the numbers in `n` never changed. One note of D minor did.

<Predict exercise="which-note-moves" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
That note is in the groove's second chord too.

<Example id="stack-on-four" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>G B♭ D became G B D: G minor became G major. The note that moved is degree 6, now a whole step above sol instead of a half step. Minor with its 6th raised is **Dorian**: 1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 ♭7. That raised 6 is its characteristic tone and the lift you heard: it brightens the scale while the ♭3 keeps it minor. Its own chord is the major IV, and i–IV is its groove.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Back to the groove, in D minor again. Two edits make it Dorian.

<Editor exercise="make-it-dorian" />

<OnPass>Dm7 to G7, i to IV, with B in the tune and in the chord. Two chords back and forth, and it never needs to go anywhere else.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The colour is old. On the page, D Dorian has no sharps or flats in its signature, and home is D.

<Example id="the-shanty" views="staff" />

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>A B C D, C A G E, D D. The one B in the tune is in bar 6, on the climb: that is the raised 6th, and it is what makes the shanty Dorian rather than minor.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Sing the shanty's climb, and the raised 6th is in your own voice.

<Sing exercise="sol-la-te-do" />

Now the same climb in D minor, sol le te do. Hear how much darker one note makes it.

<Example id="the-minor-climb" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Sol, la, te, do: in minor that second note is le, a half step lower. La is the Dorian note; you have sung it since major.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your groove sits on one chord and never touches the 6th; give it the lift. In your song it plays all three choruses, in place of Oak's chords on D and G and its tune that leans on B. Two chords going back and forth leave plenty of room for the rhythm unit's tools.

<Make exercise="your-dorian-groove" />

<CanDo id="cd.compose.dorian" />

One raised note, a major IV, a groove that can loop all night. Next: back to major, where one raised note makes it float.

<Recap />
</Step>
