<Step id="S1">
In Arranging in Code you opened a song by bringing its parts in one at a time. A song also has to stop, and how it stops is the last thing a listener remembers. Here are three songs on the same chords, each ending a different way.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The first song leaves the way it came in, the last part in first out, until one kick is left. The second loops its last bar and keeps cutting it shorter until a single note is left. The third fills, lands on C after G and stops. None of them just runs out of bars.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The first song's last four bars, in code. Each part has a mask there.

<Predict exercise="who-goes-first" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now follow the parts out on the roll.

<Example id="the-way-out" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The tune leaves after bar 9, the chords after bar 10, the bass after bar 11, and one kick ends the song in bar 12: the intro in reverse.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A song whose parts arrive in a different order. Its ending should send them away in that order turned round.

<Editor exercise="leave-in-turn" />

<OnPass>The parts now leave one at a time, the last to arrive going first, so the song ends the way it began. The closing section of a song is its **outro**, and parts leaving in turn is one of the oldest ways to write one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A second way: loop the last bar of the phrase and cut the loop shorter each time round.

<Editor exercise="shrink-the-loop" />

<OnPass>G E D C, then D C D C, then C C C C: each pass is a shorter piece of the same bar, and the shrinking itself tells you the end has come. One shortening is already enough to be heard.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
A third way turns the level down instead of taking notes away.

<Choice exercise="fade-a-loop" />

<OnPass>Card A: one bar on C keeps coming round while it gets quieter, so you hear the song drift away. That is a **fade-out**, and it works best on a loop. Over new music, as in card B, a fade sounds as if someone turned the song off.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your song from earlier lessons, intro and verse, with an eight-bar outro that just stops. Your outro becomes the ending of the unit's song, the last eight bars. Pick one way of ending from today, or the cadence you have known since the start: G, then a C that is new to the song.
