<Step id="S1">
In Verse & Chorus the chorus lifted because it changed at once: new chords, a higher tune, a tambourine. Many records prepare that moment instead, over a few bars. Hear the same verse and chorus twice, first back to back, then with four bars between them.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The second. Its four extra bars keep getting busier and louder, so you feel something coming, and the chorus arrives as the release of that wait.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Those four bars are drawn, not written note by note. A few new words in today's code are values that move smoothly over time instead of playing notes.

<Predict exercise="read-the-curve" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now take those middle four bars apart.

<Example id="four-bars-that-rise" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Bars 5–8 thicken and climb: twice as many hats, getting louder, chords that swell and eight notes that walk up the scale, so bar 9 arrives with a jolt. A stretch where the energy rises into the next section is a **build-up**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The hats carry most of the rise. Here they sit at one level.

<Editor exercise="make-the-hats-rise" />

<OnPass>The hats now climb from .2 to .9 across the whole build: one `saw`, given its start and end by `.range()` and stretched over four bars by `.slow(4)`. That is a build-up you drew in one line.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A build only works if the next section keeps what it promised. Two versions of the same song.

<Choice exercise="which-song-rises" />

<OnPass>In card A the energy climbs through the build and the chorus stays up there: louder hats with open hats on beats 2 and 4, a bass on every beat, a tune. In card B it climbs, then falls back to where the verse was. How a song's intensity rises and falls from section to section, in loudness, in how many notes and parts play, in how high they sit, is its **energy curve**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
A curve can also play notes, once you cut it into steps.

<Editor exercise="step-the-riser" />

<OnPass>Eight steps of one climbing curve: 0 to 7, which `n` plays as C4 up to C5, two a bar. `n` counts from 0, so step 0 is degree 1 and step 7 is degree 1 an octave up. A line like this, a climb that exists only to lift into the next section, is often called a riser.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The Band plays kick and snare; the chords, bass and hats are yours. Tools you already have keep a chorus up there: a bass on every beat, a hook on top, a fill in the build's last bar. Your songs could already change section; now they can rise into one.
