<Step id="S1">
Your four-chord song from C2 is eight bars long: one piece of a song. This unit builds whole songs out of pieces like it. Start with eight bars that ask and answer, once plain, then once with a cymbal added.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>After bar 4, where the question stops open and the answer begins the way the question did. The crash marks the start of each half, so you feel that seam coming.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The cymbal is one line of code you already know how to read.

<Predict exercise="read-the-crash" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The crash is in the code now. Use it as a landmark on the roll.

<Example id="count-the-phrases" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Two four-bar halves, the same opening, a new ending: eight bars before it repeats. Beats group in fours inside a bar, and bars group in fours the same way. The first bar of each four feels strong, the way beat 1 of a bar does. That grouping of bars is **hypermeter**, and it is why you can feel a phrase start before anything new happens in it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Feel the grouping in your hands before you mark it in code.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-bars" />

<OnPass>One, two, three, four, then one again: each "one" landed as the tune started a phrase. Players count songs this way, in bars rather than beats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Drummers use the crash to say where a phrase begins. On every bar, it says nothing.

<Editor exercise="mark-the-phrases" />

<OnPass>A crash on bar 1 and bar 5: four steps inside `< >` take four bars to come round, so the cymbal now says where each phrase begins. Two phrases, eight bars, one section.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Sections are built from phrases, and a song is built from sections. Count in fours.

<Choice exercise="where-it-moves-on" />

<OnPass>Bar 9: new chords and a higher tune, on the first bar of a group of eight. Bar 5 starts a phrase, but it opens the way bar 1 did; bar 9 is where the song moves on to something else.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
These eight bars are the ending of your song. The band plays C F C G, then C F G C, and the tune asks twice without an answer. In Oak's version the answer starts like the question and lands on C, with a crash on bars 1, 5 and 8. Your melody tools all fit the answer: a motif that comes back, one high point late, a lean onto the last chord.
