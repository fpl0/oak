<Step id="S1">
So far you have built songs up, adding an intro, a build, an ending. Many producers work the other way round: they let every part play all the way through, then cut parts away until the sections take shape. Here is one loop, played both ways.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In the first version nothing changes from section to section, so after a few bars nothing new can happen. In the second the same notes are there, but the opening is the chords alone, the drums and bass arrive for the verse, the tune is saved for the chorus, and the end thins out again. Every section is the chorus with something taken away.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The second version, in code. Nothing in it is new: only `silence` where a part sits out.

<Predict exercise="who-is-left" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now follow each part through the four sections.

<Example id="where-the-bass-rests" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The chorus plays every part, and the bass rests in two sections, the intro and the outro. The tune sits out until the chorus, so the chorus is the first time you hear it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Your turn with the knife. The loop plays everywhere; decide what each section loses.

<Editor exercise="take-parts-away" />

<OnPass>You wrote no new notes, yet the song now has sections that differ. Taking away is a fast way to find an arrangement, because the full loop already tells you what fits.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The carved song again, with hats of its own. At each seam every part changes on the same downbeat, a hard cut.

<Editor exercise="blur-a-seam" />

<OnPass>The hats drop out two beats before the chorus while everything else plays on, so the change starts before the bar line. A section change that does not happen in every part at once is a **fuzzy boundary**. The silence pulls you into the chorus.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
A song whose verse and chorus each come back once. Right now the second time round is the first one again.

<Editor exercise="once-only" />

<OnPass>That bar happens once in the whole song, so it is the one a listener remembers. A moment heard only once is a **unique event**. It works because everything around it comes back.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your song, with every part playing all the way through over Oak's drums. Carve it into sections, and if you like, blur one seam or let one bar happen once. Your intro, verses and choruses will play in the unit's song with exactly the parts you keep.

<Make exercise="carve-your-song" />

<CanDo id="cd.compose.carve" />

<Recap />

You can now arrange by adding parts or by taking them away, and blur the seams between sections. Next: minor keys.
</Step>
