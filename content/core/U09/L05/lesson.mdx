<Step id="S3">
Now the whole intro, every part with its own mask.

<Example id="who-comes-in-when" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Drums from bar 1, the bass at bar 2, the chords at bar 3, and the tune last: two notes at the end of bar 4 that lead into the verse. Each entry is a mask inside an intro const, and because sections restart, the intro plays the same way every time it comes round.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Here the chords arrive too early.

<Editor exercise="hold-the-chords-back" />

<OnPass>The chords now wait two bars and enter on F, the third chord of the loop. One mask per part, one item per bar: that is a whole intro.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
A mask can also take a part away. A bar with less in it, right before a chorus, makes the chorus land harder.

<Editor exercise="make-room-for-the-chorus" />

<OnPass>The bass rests under the verse's last chord, G, and comes back with the chorus on F. Taking a part out for a moment before a section lands is a common arranging move; you can do it with the drums, the chords, or everything but one line.</OnPass>
</Step>
