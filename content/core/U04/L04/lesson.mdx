<Step id="S1">
No new idea today, only many more examples. Ears learn distances the way you learn faces: you meet each one often, and its name comes with it.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<Callout kind="tip">While the pairs play, sing the name back under your breath. It sticks faster.</Callout>

<OnPass>The 3rd and the 6th sound warm, the 5th open and bare, the octave like one note at two heights. A trained ear names a distance by that colour first and counts only to check.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Now you name them. Until now one note of each pair was home; here both can be anywhere.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={3} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Both notes placed, neither of them home. Count up from the lower one, both ends included, and you have the distance.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Going down sounds different from going up, even for the same distance. Guess first, then check yourself against the name.

<Callout kind="tip">A 5th down and a 5th up are the same distance. Your ear may take a while to agree.</Callout>

<OnPass>A falling pair tends to sound like arriving, a rising one like reaching. The distance is the same either way; the direction changes how it feels.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now name the pairs going down.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={4} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Down as well as up: the same distances, heard from above.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
From the inside now: sing two of those distances up from home.

<Sing exercise="sing-up-from-do" />

<OnPass>Do to mi is a 3rd, do to sol a 5th. A distance you can sing is one you will know when you hear it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Last, both notes at once, as in Together Now.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={5} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Up, down and together: the same distances, three ways to hear them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
At the start of this unit a distance was a count on the labels; now you can move a whole riff by one, as Oak's opening does a 4th higher in bar 2. Your riff takes over the opening and the ending of your song, in place of Oak's riff and its second voice. Bar 2 can be written by hand, or `.transpose()` from Major, Minor, Perfect can move it by name.

</Step>
