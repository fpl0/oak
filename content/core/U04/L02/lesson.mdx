<Step id="S1">
In Moving Melodies you moved a line with `.transpose()` and a number of half steps. This `vox` moves by a name instead: find `.transpose("5P")` in its line.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>`"5P"` puts the copy a 5th above, seven half steps. The two lines blend almost into one thicker riff, open and bare where last lesson's 3rds sounded sweet. Rock guitarists get the same hollow strength by playing a note together with its 5th.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
In Steps Apart a 3rd was three degrees, counted from both ends. Every 3rd has the same count. Does every 3rd have the same size?

<Example id="two-thirds" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Same number, different size: C up to E is four half steps, E up to G only three. The wider 3rd is called major, the narrower one minor.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Count the letters for the number, then the half steps for the size. Most numbers come in a major and a minor size. The 4th, the 5th and the octave come in one usual size, called perfect.

| Half steps | Name | In code |
|---|---|---|
| 1 / 2 | minor / major 2nd | `"2m"` / `"2M"` |
| 3 / 4 | minor / major 3rd | `"3m"` / `"3M"` |
| 5 | perfect 4th | `"4P"` |
| 7 | perfect 5th | `"5P"` |
| 8 / 9 | minor / major 6th | `"6m"` / `"6M"` |
| 10 / 11 | minor / major 7th | `"7m"` / `"7M"` |
| 12 | octave | `"8P"` |

Six half steps has a name of its own: next lesson.

<Example id="from-c" views="code,roll" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A number of half steps works, but it hides what you meant. A name says it.

<Editor exercise="name-the-distance" />

<OnPass>`.transpose("3M")` (a major 3rd up) plays the same notes as `.transpose(4)`, and now the code says what you hear. Major, minor or perfect is the **interval quality**, the exact size of an interval in half steps.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now from a name to a note, with no keyboard to look at.

<Choice exercise="spell-it" />

<OnPass>The count fixes the letter, and the sharp or flat does the fine tuning. That is why a major 3rd above A is C♯ and never D♭.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
In Steps Apart, adding 2 to every `n` kept `vox` in the key, so its 3rds came in both sizes. A named move keeps one size for every note. This riff uses only 1, 4 and 5 (C, F and G), so a major 3rd, a major 6th or a perfect 5th above stays in C major.
