<Step id="S1">
Your two voices so far sat on two lines, `mel` and `vox`. Here one line plays both notes of each pair at the same moment.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Three pairs could ring on forever. One grates and leans forward, and the pair after it sounds like a relief. Next you find out which one, and why.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The very first loop of the course had a comma in its drum line, `"bd ~ sd ~, hh*8"`, and the hi-hats played at the same time as the kick and snare. Now the comma is yours, for notes.

<Example id="pairs" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>B up to F is six half steps, exactly half an octave: a **tritone**. In C major those are degrees 7 and 4, and each leans towards a neighbour a half step away. In the last bar both get there, B up to C and F down to E. The two notes close in to a 3rd, and the pull lets go.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The ear sorts pairs into two kinds long before it knows any names.

<Choice exercise="which-one-ends-it" />

<OnPass>C with E blends and could end a song: **consonance**. C with D♭ rubs and wants to move on: **dissonance**. 3rds, 6ths, 5ths and octaves sound at rest; 2nds, 7ths and the tritone pull. The 4th sits in between, open but less settled than the 5th.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
On a staff, notes that sound together share one stem.

<Staff example="the-page" />

<Editor exercise="play-them-together" />

<OnPass>Four pairs, four beats: at rest, at rest, a pull, and the release. That last move, a dissonance stepping to a consonance, is a small version of what makes music go forward.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the pairs come from all over the key. Two notes at once are harder to pick apart than one after the other, so take your time.

<EarDrill family="fam.int.in-key" level={5} trials={6} />

<Callout kind="tip">Hum the top note first, then the bottom one. Then find each on the way down to do.</Callout>

<OnPass>Two notes at once, both named. Your ear is starting to hear inside a sound, not only along a line.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The pull you heard in the hook is not special to C. Every major key has exactly one pair like it.

<Choice exercise="where-the-pull-lives" />

<OnPass>4 and 7: F and B in C, C and F♯ in G. When you hear that pull in a song, home is usually close.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Two lessons ago a second voice needed a line of its own. Now one line can hold a pair, and you decide whether it rests or pulls. The starter's B with F lets go when the loop comes round to C with E, and `@` can hold a pair at rest to give that release time to land.

<Make exercise="clash-then-rest" />

<Recap />
</Step>
