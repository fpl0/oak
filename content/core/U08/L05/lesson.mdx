<Step id="S1">
Four lessons gave your bass its notes and its rhythm. Two things remain: hearing a bass line well enough to write it down, and choosing what it sounds like. First, one bass line under the doo-wop loop, played three ways.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>`ebass` is picked and clear, `fingerbass` rounder, its notes starting softer, and the synth bass buzzes with an edge the other two lack. Same notes, three timbres: the bass's tone colours the whole loop.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
This is how bass players learn songs: by ear, a bar at a time. The chords are written, so you already know each bar's root; listen for the note after it.

<Editor exercise="take-the-bass-down" />

<OnPass>C G, F C, A E, G D: each bar's root on beat 1, then its 5th on beat 3, below the root in bar 1 and above it after that, so the line stays low. You heard a bass line and wrote it down.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
No code and no chords to read this time: only the bass degree under each chord.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.bass" level={4} trials={4} />

<OnPass>The bass degree first, then everything else: that is the start of every transcription you will do.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Singing a bass line is how you remember it away from the keyboard.

<Sing exercise="sing-what-you-took-down" />

<OnPass>Do sol, fa do, la mi, sol re: the line you took down, in your own voice.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The same notes can sit warm and low under the chords or buzz on top of them. Two new sounds today, and one number to turn.

<Editor exercise="choose-the-tone" />

<OnPass>A lower number in `.lpf()` makes the bass darker and rounder; a higher one lets more of the buzz through. Under piano chords, somewhere from 400 to 800 usually sits right.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Everything from this unit goes on one loop: roots on the one, 5ths and octaves, approach notes, a rhythm that locks with the kick, and a tone you chose. This loop starts away from home, vi IV I V, so it is the bass that tells the ear which chord each bar stands on. It plays twice in the quiet part of your song, before the last loud part: first under Oak's drums and held chords alone, then with Oak's tune on top.

<Make exercise="your-bass-your-tone" />

<CanDo id="cd.transcribe.bass" />

<Recap />

You can now write a bass line: from the chords, from the page, or by ear, in the tone you want. Next unit: whole songs, built from parts that take turns and come back.
</Step>
