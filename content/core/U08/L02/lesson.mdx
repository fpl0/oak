<Step id="S1">
Roots alone give a loop its floor. Most bass lines do more than that, and they need only a couple of extra notes to do it.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Each bass moves more than the last: one note a bar holds the floor, two start to walk, eight bounce. The second is the sound of a lot of rock and country, the third a disco bass. All three use only each chord's root and the notes that blend with it best.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Here is the second bass in code. A bass shape works like a chord stack: write it once as a cell, and `.add()` moves it onto every root.

<Predict exercise="what-the-4-adds" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
A longer cell, `[0 4 7 4]`: each bar climbs from its root and comes back.

<Example id="up-and-back" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Seven steps up from any root is the same letter again, an octave higher; four steps up is its 5th. Over G that 5th is D, degree 2 of C. Root, 5th and octave: one cell, and `.add()` fits it to every chord.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Disco basses live on this jump.

<Editor exercise="the-octave-bounce" />

<OnPass>The root and its octave on alternate eighths, under every chord. `n` counts from 0, so the octave is 7, not 8. That bounce is a whole bass line.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
This bass is written on the bass clef only. The code already holds the chords.

<Editor exercise="root-five-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>C to G, then G to D: each bar's root and its 5th, the same cell under both chords. Half notes on the page, a two-number cell in code.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The 5th and the octave are the two notes a bass reaches for after the root. Tell them apart by ear.

<Choice exercise="which-one-jumps" />

<OnPass>The octave blends with the root, so the line sounds like one note bouncing. The 5th is a new note, so the line moves between two.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
This bass walks from each root to its 3rd, and this low the 3rd makes each chord sound less settled. The 5th or the octave settles it. After that, treat the cell like a motif: more notes, rests, the 5th below the root, or the cell turned round, with a tune on top.
