<Step id="S1">
Your bass knows which notes to play now: roots, 5ths, octaves, approach tones. This lesson is about when it plays them. Here the Band plays the four-chord loop under drums and a bass written in code.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Loop 1 rubs: the bass and the kick agree on some hits and not on others, and the low end sounds cluttered. In loop 2 every bass note starts with a kick, and the bottom of the groove snaps into place.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
`const` gives a pattern a name. Written once at the top, `hits` can be used by any line below it, as many times as you like.

<Predict exercise="how-many-together" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Two lines, one rhythm. Change it in one place and see what follows.

<Example id="change-the-rhythm" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Every line that says `hits` moves when `hits` changes: here, the kick and the bass. The middle hit lands on the “and” of 2, between the beats, and leans the groove into beat 3.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The bass is busy and the kick is not.

<Editor exercise="give-the-bass-the-kicks-rhythm" />

<OnPass>Kick and bass now start every note together, so you hear one low, round hit instead of two parts. That is a **groove lock**, and in funk and much modern pop it is the first thing a bass player listens for.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Feel the shared rhythm in your hands before you write your own.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-shared-rhythm" />

<OnPass>Three taps a bar, and the one on the “and” of 2 is the one that makes it move.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now by ear alone, with grooves you have not heard.

<EarDrill family="fam.rhy.groove-lock" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Locked or loose, you can now hear which. Oak brings these back in your warm-ups.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your kick and bass play the last two loud parts of your song, under your chords from Root–Five–Octave and the tune above them. The bass's notes came from the last three lessons; now its rhythm comes from the drums. A backbeat on top, the way your first grooves had it, finishes the groove.

<Make exercise="kick-and-bass-as-one" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.groove-lock" />

<Recap />

You can now lock any bass to any kick with one rhythm, named once. Next: taking bass lines down by ear, and choosing the sound of your bass.
</Step>
