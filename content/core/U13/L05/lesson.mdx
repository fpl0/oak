<Step id="S1">
The ballad chords from Compound Meter, back in a bar of four beats and played twice. The kick and the snare keep the same square beat both times.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In loop 2 the piano and the hats split every beat into three: the rolling feel of 6/8, inside a square bar of four. The three quick snare hits at the end of bar 4 roll into the repeat. Together they make the slow dance of a 1950s ballad.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Feel it before you code it. The count you said in 6/8, three to a beat, now turns up one beat at a time inside a bar of four.

<OnPass>Two bars echoed back, busy beats included. That is the whole idea of this lesson: a beat can split in two or in three, one beat at a time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
One new call can do the splitting for you.

<Predict exercise="count-the-hats" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Twelve hats in a bar of four beats, on the roll and on the staff. On the staff each group of three sits under a bracket with a 3.

<Example id="threes" views="code,roll,staff" />

<OnPass>Three equal notes in the time of two is a **triplet**: the bracket with a 3 says so on the staff, and in code it is a bracket of three in one step or `.ply(3)`. Swing was made of triplets all along: the long–short pair of your shuffles is the first and the third of the three, with the middle one left out.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now bring the piano into the threes.

<Editor exercise="the-piano-in-threes" />

<OnPass>`.ply(3)` after `.struct()`: three chords to every beat, locked with the hats, while the kick and the snare keep the four beats square. That is the slow-dance ballad sound.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Reading triplets. A quarter note fills one beat; a triplet shares one beat between three notes. `n` counts from 0, so degree 1 is `n` 0.

<Editor exercise="read-the-triplets" />

<OnPass>One step for each beat, and a bracket of three for each triplet: C, then E G C, then G, then E D C. In 12/8 the same bar needs no brackets at all, because there every beat already splits in three: a dotted quarter and three eighths, twice. One rhythm, two ways to write it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
By ear. The kick and the snare mark the four beats; the hats say how each one divides.

<Choice exercise="two-or-three" />

<OnPass>Two to the beat walks; three to the beat rolls. Your warm-ups will keep asking.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
Your song's bridge rolls in threes: Oak's chords play three notes to every beat, and the snare rolls three hits into beat 4 of bars 4 and 8. This loop is the same four chords, square as a march; set it rolling, and it becomes the bridge, under Oak's tune. The 6/8 trick still works here: `.velocity()` can lean on the first of each three.
