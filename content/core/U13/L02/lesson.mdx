<Step id="S1">
Your pushes moved chords in time. Here nothing moves. A funk vamp on Dm7 and G7 plays twice, with kick and bass locked together and every hit in the same place both times.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In loop 2 the snare is loud on 2 and 4 and drops to a whisper in between, while the hats and the guitar lean on some steps and not others. Nothing moved in time, yet loop 2 sounds played rather than programmed: strength alone gives a groove its shape.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The kick and the bass hit together, two of their three hits between the beats, like the bass grooves you locked to a kick in Lock with the Kick. That frame comes before anything else.

<OnPass>Three taps a bar, two of them off the beat: the frame of the groove. Everything else you heard sits in the gaps around it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Strength has its own call. Like every control, it reads its numbers against the rhythm on its left.

<Predict exercise="two-numbers-eight-hits" />

<OnPass>Two numbers share the bar between them, half each: four loud hits, then four quiet. To give every pair of hits the same loud–quiet shape, repeat the cell: `"[1 .3]*4"` is eight numbers.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Back to loop 2 from the start of the lesson, now with its code.

<Example id="the-groove" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Beats 2 and 4 hit at full strength; the three hits between them, on the “a” of 2, the “e” of 3 and the “a” of 4, play at .3. A note played so quietly that it is felt more than heard is a **ghost note**. Sixteen steps, sixteen numbers: one strength for every step, rests included.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the snare is yours to shape, on the same groove with every snare hit at full strength.

<Editor exercise="ghost-the-snare" />

<OnPass>The same five hits, and now the snare has a shape: loud on the backbeat, a whisper in between. The ghost notes fill the space without crowding it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
By ear now, on the sixteenth grid. The hats play every eighth; listen for the kick that lands where no hat does.

<Choice exercise="find-the-sixteenth-push" />

<OnPass>A kick on an “e” or an “a” is the tightest kind of syncopation: a sixteenth off the eighths. Funk drummers put it there so the groove never sits still.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The vamp is stiff again, and the Rhodes holds long chords. This time every strength is your decision. Bring back a tool from the last lesson too: a chord pushed by a sixteenth, the tight push of funk.

<Make exercise="your-funk-groove" />

<CanDo id="cd.code.ghost-notes" />

You can now give every hit of a groove its own strength and write a funk groove that breathes. Next: the push on paper, where a note that holds across the beat gets a curved line or a dot.

<Recap />
</Step>
