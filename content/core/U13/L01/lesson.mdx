<Step id="S4">
Back to the eighth-note push, with the roll open.

<Example id="the-push" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Each chord starts on the “and” after beat 4 and holds across the bar line: one eighth note before it, seven after. A chord or a note that arrives early like this and holds over the beat it belongs to is an **anticipation**; players call it a push. Accents that land between the beats, against the pulse the drums keep, are **syncopation**. You wrote such hits with `.struct()` before; now one call moves a whole line. (The first time through, bar 1 stays silent until its last eighth note, because its chord would have had to start before the music did.)</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now you write the push, on a new loop where everything starts on the beat.

<Editor exercise="push-the-rhodes" />

<OnPass>`.early(1/8)`: every chord of the Rhodes now leans into its bar, and the bass confirms it on beat 1. Same chords, same tempo, and the loop has started to move forward.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The opposite move has its own sound. A short chord after every beat is the off-beat chop of ska and reggae.

<Editor exercise="after-the-beat" />

<OnPass>`.late(1/8)`: four chords a bar, each an eighth note after its beat, each still the chord of its own bar. Early leans into the next beat; late answers the one that just went by.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
By ear now. The bass is your ruler: it plays on beats 1 and 3 in all four grooves.

<Choice exercise="on-the-beat-or-pushed" />

<OnPass>On the beat, the chords and the bass sound like one hit. Pushed, you hear two: the chord, then the bass under it. The third groove pushed by a sixteenth note only, the tight push of funk.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
Until this lesson every chord change you wrote landed on a beat. In your song's verses every chord after the first arrives early: Oak's kick, bass and chords push it together on the “and” of 4, and the tune leans across the bar line with them. This loop is square: push its Rhodes, and it becomes the groove of your song's intro, both verses and ending, with Oak's tune on top in the verses and the ending.
