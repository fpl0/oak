<Step id="S1">
You first played Ode to Joy in plain beats, because its real rhythm needed tools you did not have yet. Here is its last phrase twice: the way you know it, then as Beethoven wrote it.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>In loop 2 an E arrives a beat early and holds across the bar line, and the last bar stops walking in even steps. Those two moments make the written tune sound sung rather than counted.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Feel the ending before you count it. Hum it once under your breath as it plays.

<OnPass>The last bar has only three notes, and the first one takes more than its beat. Writing that stretched note down takes something new, in code and on the page.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
`@` gives a note a weight inside its brackets, as it has since your first drum loops.

<Predict exercise="shares-of-the-bar" />

<OnPass>Three shares of eight: three eighth notes, a beat and a half. The C after it is one eighth, and the last C takes the other half of the bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now the same two bars on the page, beside the roll.

<Example id="the-ending" views="code,roll,staff" />

<OnPass>The dot makes a note half as long again: a quarter note with a dot is a quarter plus an eighth, three eighths in all. A note written this way is a **dotted note**. In code there is no dot, only the share: `1@3` in a bar of eight.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the other change you heard, near the start of the phrase.

<Example id="bars-12-13" views="staff" />

<OnPass>Two beats: the E starts on beat 4 and the curved line carries it through beat 1 of the next bar. That curve is a **tie**. It joins two notes of the same pitch into one sound, across a beat or a bar line. It is also how a page writes a push: the E belongs to the C chord of the next bar, and it arrives a beat early.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Play from the page: every length you need is on the staff, and the notes are already in the starter.

<LeadSheet example="the-riff" />

<Editor exercise="play-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>G for a dotted quarter, then A from the “and” of 2 to the end of the bar: on the page an eighth tied to a half note, in code one note with a weight of five. The riff leans because its long note starts between the beats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Now from code to the page. The tie is a button and a key; so is the dot.

<Example id="the-lean" views="code" />

<StaffInput exercise="write-the-lean" />

<OnPass>A dotted quarter, then an eighth tied over the bar line to a half note: the G belongs to bar 2 and arrives an eighth early. Every push you write in code has this shape on paper.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
The pushed rhythm section from Pushes & Anticipations, with a tune that plods on the beats. Give the tune notes that lean and linger, and watch the dots and ties appear on the page as you write. If the snare sounds plain beside it, ghost notes are one call away.

<Make exercise="your-leaning-tune" />

<CanDo id="cd.read.ties-dots" />

You can now read and write notes that hold across a beat or a bar line, in code and on the staff. Next: a slow groove that sways in two beats, each one split in three.

<Recap />
</Step>
