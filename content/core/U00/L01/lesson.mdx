<Step id="S1">
Everything you are about to hear is four short lines of code, and each line is one player.

<Example id="loop" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Four players, the same few seconds of music, around and around until you press Stop: a **loop**. Most of what you write in Oak starts as one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Each line starts with a name and a colon: `drums`, `bass`, `chords`, `mel`. The name tells you who plays.

<OnPass>A deep bass, soft keys, a bright piano and a kit of drums. They can play the same note and still sound nothing alike. That colour of a sound is its **timbre**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Your first edit: one number in the tune.

<Editor exercise="change-one-number" />

<OnPass>One number changed, and the tune now ends on a different note. Every number in that line is a note you can move.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The panel on the right is your tutor. It sees the step you are on and the code in the editor, and it answers in plain words. Ask it about anything you hear, or why a line sounds the way it does.

A good first question: "What is each line of this code doing?"

The tutor is optional. Every step works without it, and the hints under each exercise are always there.

<Callout kind="tip">If you skipped connecting the tutor, Settings has the same card whenever you want it.</Callout>
</Step>
