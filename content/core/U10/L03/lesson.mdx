<Step id="S1">
You have seen minor's three dark notes on the roll and sung them up a scale. This lesson trains your ear to find them inside a tune, starting with a soul groove in A minor.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The tune opens on me, C, and in bar 3 leans le onto sol, F onto E: two dark notes, each heard against home on A. Hearing a note against home, not against the note before it, is how you take a minor tune down by ear.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Start with three landmarks of a minor key, each heard against a home the chords set up.

<EarDrill family="fam.deg.fet-minor" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Do is rest, sol is open, and me, right above re, gives the key its colour. These three come back in tomorrow's warm-up.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
One note apart, one mood apart. Play each card as often as you like.

<Choice exercise="me-or-mi" />

<OnPass>Card 1: C over the low A, me, a half step under mi. Everything after that first note is the same on both cards, and the first note alone sets the mood.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
In major, ti leans hard into do. In minor, te sits a whole step below and arrives more gently.

<Sing exercise="le-to-sol-te-to-do" />

<OnPass>Le sol, te do. Le sighs down onto sol, and te reaches do without the tug of ti. That softer arrival is much of the sound of minor rock and soul.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now put the ear to work on a minor phrase. Switch between the source and your code at the same bar, and slow it down when you need to.

<Editor exercise="take-it-down" />

<OnPass>Me re do, then le falling onto sol. You heard each dark note against home and wrote it down.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Back to the soul groove, with a tune that knows only 1 and 5. Me, le and te give a tune its minor colour. Chord notes on beats 1 and 3 keep it on the chords, as in Landing on Chord Tones.

<Make exercise="lean-on-the-dark-notes" />

When this unit began, minor was a word in `.scale()`. Now you can hear it: me, le and te each against home, and a minor tune told from a major one by its 3rd. Minor degrees join your warm-ups. Next: the chords of a minor key.

</Step>
