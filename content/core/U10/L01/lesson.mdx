<Step id="S1">
Every tune and chord you have written so far has lived in a major key. Here is the loop you put a floor under in Roots Down Low, bass and tune included, and then the same loop with one word changed in each line.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>At bar 5 the loop turns dark. The rhythm and the shape of every line stay the same, yet the mood goes from open to heavy, close to sad. One word in each line did that; the next two steps find out what it did to the notes.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The change at bar 5 lives in the three `.scale()` calls.

<Predict exercise="what-changed" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Take the loop away and keep only its scale: one bar in major, one bar in minor.

<Example id="two-scales" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Home stays on C. Degrees 3, 6 and 7 each drop a half step, to ♭3, ♭6 and ♭7, and everything else stays where it was. That is **natural minor**, major's dark twin. Sung, it is do re me fa sol le te: 1 is still do, and the three lowered notes take new syllables.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A scale word moves only the numbers in `n()`. A tune written in note names stays where you put it, even when the chords under it turn dark: hear how this one rubs.

<Editor exercise="darken-it-by-hand" />

<OnPass>E♭, B♭ and A♭: the tune now sits on its minor chords. Degrees in `n()` turn minor with one word; note names need their three flats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
No roll and no code now, and a tune you have never heard. Inside one loop the switch was plain; here only the sound can tell you.

<EarDrill family="fam.key.mode" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Degree 3 decides it: a half step lower and the whole key turns dark. It is also the middle note of the chord on home, so you hear it every time the music comes home. This choice comes back in tomorrow's warm-up.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Put the new scale in your voice. Me and le are the two notes that make a tune sound minor, and 1 is still do.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-dark-scale" />

<OnPass>Do re me fa sol le sol. That little fall from le to sol, a half step down, is the sigh of a minor key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The loop is dark already; only the tune still waits on home. Me and le are what make a tune sound minor. Your loop plays your song's bridge in C minor, the eight bars after the second chorus; Oak plays Cm Gm A♭ Fm there today, with G in the last bar. A first phrase that asks and a second that answers, as in Question & Answer, gives the bridge its shape.

<Make exercise="a-dark-tune" />

Now one word in `.scale()` turns any loop written in degrees dark, and you can sing the scale it makes. Next comes the Bass & form checkpoint, which opens your verse–chorus demo; after it, minor's two twins.
