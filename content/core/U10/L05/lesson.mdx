<Step id="S1">
Four chords of A minor from Minor-Key Chords, one a bar, over a bass that drives. Then the same four chords again, with another tune on top.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The first tune keeps coming back to A, the second to C, and the loop feels different under each, though not one chord changed. The note a tune rests on has a big say in which note sounds like home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Two loops, one difference: the note the tune rests on.

<Predict exercise="which-one-is-minor" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now the numbers behind the first loop.

<Example id="in-a-minor" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>i VI III VII: home, then the key's three major chords in a row, ♭6, ♭3 and ♭7. Natural minor has an older name, **Aeolian**, and this is the Aeolian loop, minor's four-chord song. Read in C, it is vi IV I V, the axis loop started on vi. The chords are the same; the tune decides which home you hear.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The loop in stacks, over the minor scale. Four numbers stand between it and the Aeolian loop.

<Editor exercise="write-the-aeolian-loop" />

<OnPass>0 5 2 6: Am F C G. You can write minor's four-chord song from its numerals.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
No chord band this time. Two clues tell you where home is: the chord a card starts on, and the note its tune rests on.

<Choice exercise="where-is-home" />

<OnPass>A loop that starts on Am with a tune that rests on A is home on A: the Aeolian loop. Start the loop on C, or let the tune rest on C, and the same chords sound like C major, I V vi IV or vi IV I V.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The roots of the loop are the loop. Sing them and you can hear it without any chords at all.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-aeolian-roots" />

<OnPass>Do, le, me, te: three of minor's own syllables in four notes. With do on A, the roots alone spell out the minor key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A groove that only rocks between Am and Dm. Once it is the Aeolian loop, it plays the first two choruses of your song, where Oak's chorus already circles Am F C G under its hook, and arranging it is what makes it sound like a record. A loop this steady suits the `.mask()` entries of Arranging in Code: a bass or a tune that comes in a few bars late.
