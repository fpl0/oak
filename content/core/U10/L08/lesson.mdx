<Step id="S1">
The loop of Raise the Seventh, i iv V i, now with a tune that climbs into home through the raised 7. Two versions; only bar 3 differs.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The first climb lurches in the middle, over a stretch wider than the steps around it. The second walks evenly into home, and the arrival sounds smoother for it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
G♯ pulls up to A, as it did in Raise the Seventh. The trouble is the step before it.

<Predict exercise="close-the-gap" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
All three forms of minor in a row, so you can compare their tops.

<Example id="three-minors" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Harmonic minor raises 7 and leaves a gap of three half steps under it. Raise 6 as well, F to F♯, and the climb from 5 to home goes by steps: that is **melodic minor**. Tunes usually use it on the way up and come back down in natural minor, G then F.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
One scale per bar, the pattern you once used to change key. Here it changes the form of minor instead.

<Editor exercise="up-melodic-down-natural" />

<OnPass>E F♯ G♯ A up, A G F E down: raised going up, natural coming down. Your tune now climbs home the way a singer would.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the three forms by ear alone, over a single low A, with no chord to give the answer away.

<Choice exercise="which-minor" />

<OnPass>Natural minor comes home from a whole step below, harmonic minor leaps into its raised 7, and melodic minor climbs smoothly through both raised notes. Tomorrow's warm-up brings all three back.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
La and ti on the way up, te and le on the way down: the same four places in the scale, two colours.

<Sing exercise="sing-up-and-down" />

<OnPass>Sol la ti do, do te le sol. Up bright, down dark, and home both times.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The chords and the bass are done; the tune still leaps into its last bar. Over E, F♯ is a passing tone, as in Passing & Neighbour Tones: it moves through and does not stay.

You now have all three forms of minor and know the job of each: natural for the colour, harmonic for the pull of V, melodic for a smooth climb home. Next: an old minor tune by ear, set as a ballad with strings and an echo.
