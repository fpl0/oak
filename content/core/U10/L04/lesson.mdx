<Step id="S1">
So far you have played minor chords without naming them. Here is a triad on every note of A minor, stacked the way you stacked C major in The Seven Chords, then a groove made from four of them.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The groove lifts in bars 3 and 4: it sounds brighter there without leaving A minor. A minor key keeps a few bright chords of its own, and the next steps find where they sit.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">

<Step id="S3">
Back to the seven chords, this time with their names.

<Example id="the-seven" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>i ii° III iv v VI VII. The chords on 1, 4 and 5 are minor, ii° is diminished, and the chords on ♭3, ♭6 and ♭7 are major: the bright corners of a dark key. The major chord on ♭7, G, sits a whole step below home. That note and its chord are the **subtonic**.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A loop that never leaves the dark. Two of the key's major chords can lift its second half.

<Editor exercise="lift-the-second-half" />

<OnPass>Am Dm G C: i iv VII III. The loop climbs out of the dark in bar 3 and stays bright on III in bar 4, before i pulls it back. The groove from the start of the lesson is now yours to write.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Chord names on a page, numerals in your head. Read each chord against A, not against C.

<OnPass>i VI VII i. The same F and G that are IV and V in C major are VI and VII here, because home is A.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The bass tells you the loop, in minor as in major. Some of these loops are minor, so expect me, le and te down there.

<EarDrill family="fam.har.bass" level={5} trials={4} />

<OnPass>In a minor key, a bass on me, le or te carries a bright chord above it: III, VI or VII. These loops come back in your warm-up.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The loop is all minor again, and where it lifts is your choice. A fingerbass on root and 5th, the shape from Root–Five–Octave, turns it into a groove you can play over for minutes. In your song, its first four bars play just before each chorus, where Oak climbs F G C E today, and the chorus comes in on Am right after.
