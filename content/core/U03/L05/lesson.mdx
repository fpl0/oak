<Step id="S1">
In Degrees in Code you moved Ode to Joy to G with one word. Here the word takes turns, so the tune changes home without stopping.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>It does. Every note of bars 3 and 4 sits lower, yet the tune ends on 1 again and sounds just as finished. The numbers on the roll never changed; only home moved.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
One more home, F, and this time you guess first.

<Predict exercise="new-home-same-tune" hum />

<OnPass>The same tune, with F as home: the numbers kept every note's place in the key, and the new key decided how high the tune sits.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
`< >` inside `.scale()` works as it does everywhere else: one item per bar. `!2` keeps a key for two bars, the length of the tune. <CodeRef fn="scale" />

<Example id="three-keys" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The numbers never change. Every note moved up two half steps from C to D, and from C to F every note moved up five: the whole tune shifts as one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now write the key plan yourself.

<Editor exercise="visit-g" />

<OnPass>`<C4:major!2 G4:major!2>`: two bars in C, two in G. The tune never changed; you wrote one plan for where home is.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Degrees work both ways: from a number and a key you can name the note without playing it.

<Choice exercise="n-two-in-d" />

<OnPass>F♯: `n` 2 is degree 3, and D major's third note is F♯. In D major every F is sharp, wherever it appears.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Your ears get the same deal as your code: the key can change from one note to the next, and the degrees do not.

<EarDrill family="fam.deg.fet-major" level={2} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Degrees 1 to 5 in keys you did not choose. The pull towards home is the same in every one of them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Your voice can move home too.

<Sing exercise="sing-it-in-a" />

<OnPass>One, two, three, two, one: the same numbers you would sing in C. Your voice found home in A and counted from there.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
One pattern in `.scale()` now sends a tune through as many keys as you like, and the band changes key with you. A 7 that leads home or a run with `..` can make the tune your own before it travels.
