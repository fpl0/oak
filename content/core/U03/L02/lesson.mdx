<Step id="S1">
Home & the Major Scale gave every scale a home and seven letters. A letter tells you which key to press, not where a note sits in the scale. This line is written in numbers that say exactly that, counted from home.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The line starts on 1 and stops on 2, one step above home, so it never quite lands until the loop brings it back to 1. The numbers show at a glance how far each note sits from home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
`n` plays the notes of the scale named in `.scale()` by their place in it, counting from 0 at home. <CodeRef fn="n" />

<Predict exercise="which-three-notes" hum />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The first seven notes of C major, one number each. Compare the code with the labels.

<Example id="seven-degrees" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The label is the note's **scale degree**: its number in the key, with home as 1. Like an array index, `n` counts from 0, so degree 5 is `n` 4.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
You played this tune from letters in Playing from the Page. A tune written in degrees says what each note does in the key, not just which key to press.

<Editor exercise="tune-by-numbers" />

<OnPass>`2 2 3 4`, then `4 3 2 1`: every degree one less, every time. Now you can say why the tune's first phrase sounded unfinished in Playing from the Page: it stopped on 2, not on 1.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
The numbers say what each note does; `.scale()` says where home is: its letter, its octave, then the kind of scale. <CodeRef fn="scale" />

<Editor exercise="home-is-g" />

<OnPass>Same numbers, new home: the whole tune moved to G with one word. In Moving Melodies you counted half steps to move a tune; now you name its home instead. The tune is the numbers, and the key is a setting.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
From here on your ears learn degrees too, starting with the two clearest.

<Choice exercise="rest-or-open" />

<OnPass>5: steady, but the music could go on from it. Only 1 sounds like the end.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Now 3 joins them. Judge each note by how it sits against home, not by how high it is.

<EarDrill family="fam.deg.fet-major" level={1} trials={6} />

<Callout kind="tip">Sing down from the note to home on numbers. Your voice counts the steps for you.</Callout>

<OnPass>1, 3 and 5 by ear. The same three numbers you can now write as `n` 0, 2 and 4.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
Now the same three degrees in your own voice.

<Sing exercise="sing-one-three-five" />

<OnPass>One, three, five and back home. Those three degrees are the bones of the key: sounded together, they make the chord that home rests on, which you will build in a later unit.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S9">
A note used to be a letter. Now it is a place in the key, counted from home. The band follows whatever key your `.scale()` names.
