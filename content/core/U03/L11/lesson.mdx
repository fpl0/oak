<Step id="S1">
Your last Make answered two bars with two more. Today a whole tune: eight bars on a new sound, `acgtr`, the acoustic guitar, over a band that changes chord every bar.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>One pause half-way wants to go on; the ending lands on home and lets you stop. Many tunes are built from halves like these: the first leaves a question open, the second closes it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
A sentence pauses at a comma and stops at a full stop. A tune does the same, and the chords under it agree.

<Example id="tune" views="code,roll,staff" />

<OnPass>Bar 4 rests on 5, over a chord that leans back towards home: a comma. Bar 8 lands on 1, over home's own chord: a full stop.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
A tune that stops on 2 or 7 sounds like a question nobody answered; landing on 1 answers it.

<Editor exercise="bring-it-home" />

<OnPass>Home in bar 8. Any way of getting there works, as long as the last note is 1.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The second half of a tune often starts like the first. The ear hears the return and knows where it is.

<Editor exercise="start-again" />

<OnPass>Bar 5 starts the second half the way bar 1 started the first. This time, bars 7 and 8 take it home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
One more round of all seven degrees before you write your own eight bars.

<EarDrill family="fam.deg.fet-major" level={3} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Every degree in the key, by ear. You now hear what you write.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The tune's ending, now in your voice.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-ending" />

<OnPass>Fa, mi, re, do: the tune's last two bars, walking home by step.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
At the start of this unit you found home by ear. Now you can write eight bars that leave it and come back, and they become both loud parts of your song, over the same eight chords Oak's tune uses there. A 7 or a 2 just before the last 1 makes the arrival clear.

</Step>
