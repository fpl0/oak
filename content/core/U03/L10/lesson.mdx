<Step id="S1">
Tunes by Number handed you a tune's degrees to write in `n`. Today you get only the sound, and your ears do the reading.

<OnPass>They are: this tune starts on home. Knowing home and the first note gives you somewhere to start writing.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Three steps, every time: find home, find the first note, then follow the line up or down. Start small.

<EarDrill family="fam.mel.dictation" level={2} trials={4} />

<Callout kind="tip">Sing each note back before you tap it. Your voice remembers the line better than your ears do.</Callout>

<OnPass>That is a melody taken down by ear. The longer tunes below work the same way, one note at a time.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
An old spiritual, from its best-known line. Its code is hidden, so your ears and your own code are all you have.

<Editor exercise="swing-low-by-ear" />

<Callout kind="tip">If it is hard to follow, slow it down, and sing each bar before you write it.</Callout>

<OnPass>Swing Low: 3 and 1, then 3, 1 and two notes below home, the last one held. In `n`: `2 0 | 2 0 -2 | -3`. Singers stretch these notes freely; Oak plays them on plain beats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Between the two tunes you write down, a short one for your voice.

<Sing exercise="echo-hallelujah" />

<OnPass>Mi, sol, la, sol: the "Hallelujah" of Michael, Row the Boat Ashore. Degrees 3 5 6 5.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the first tune. This time hints go from finding home to the first bar, if you want them.

<Editor exercise="auld-lang-syne-by-ear" />

<OnPass>Auld Lang Syne, without its first note: `0 0 0 2 | 1 0 1 2 | 0 0 2 4 | 5`. The real tune starts with one note before bar 1 and lengthens some notes at the expense of the next; both wait for a later unit.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
You can now write down a tune you have only heard. A phrase often gets an answer: the same shape again, higher or lower, often landing where the music can rest. Your four bars end your song, just before its last chord; there the band plays C and F twice, the chords your phrase was written over, and the song stops on C after it.

<Make exercise="answer-the-phrase" />

<CanDo id="cd.transcribe.melody" />

<Recap />
</Step>
