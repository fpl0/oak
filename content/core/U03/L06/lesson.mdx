<Step id="S1">
Home is G today, and the band plays in G with you. Before the first note, the staff shows something new: one sharp, on the top line.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The note on the top line is F♯, degree 7, and it leans up into the high G that opens bar 2. The sharp at the start of the staff told you so before the line began.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
In Home & the Major Scale you built G major from the pattern and needed one sharp. The keyboard shows where it falls.

<Example id="g-major" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>G major has one sharp, F♯. D major has two: F♯ and C♯. Every time you play in D, those two notes are sharp, whatever the tune.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Because a key's sharps never change, musicians write them once, at the start of every line of the staff, instead of before each note. Every F on the page is then F♯, in any octave, until the piece says otherwise.

The sharps always come in the same order: **F C G D A E B**. Each sharp key keeps the sharps of the one before and adds the next. G major has F♯. D major adds C♯. A major adds G♯, E major D♯, and so on.

<Example id="d-major" views="code,roll" />

<Callout kind="tip">The last sharp is always degree 7 of the key, a half step under home: C♯ in D major, G♯ in A major.</Callout>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now write one yourself.

<StaffInput exercise="d-major-sharps" />

<OnPass>F♯, then C♯: D major's **key signature**. One glance at the start of the staff tells you which notes are sharp for the whole piece.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the page, now with a key signature. The staff alone, no code and no roll.

<Staff example="the-page" />

<Editor exercise="page-in-g" />

<OnPass>`<[4 6 7 4] [5 4 2@2]>`: 5 7 1' 5, then 6 5 3. The note on the top line was F♯, because the key signature said so; nothing next to the note did.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
No staff this time: from the order alone.

<Choice exercise="sharps-in-a" />

<OnPass>Three: F♯, C♯ and G♯. Each step along the sharp keys adds one more, and the next lesson puts every key on one circle.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
One sign at the start of the staff now covers every F in the piece. Your line plays the eight bars where your song moves to G, over G and C chords; in note names there is no `.scale()` to add the sharp for you, so the key signature lives in your head. An F♯ that leads up to `g` lands the way 7 led home in Leading Tone & Six.
