<Step id="S1">
You have sung degrees on numbers since Degrees in Code. Numbers are good for code, but for singing "seven" takes two syllables and "one, three, five" blurs together. Here every degree also gets a short word to sing, shown under its number.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>It ends on do, the word for 1, home. Each degree has a word of its own, and every one is a single syllable you can hold on a note.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Now move home and watch where the words go.

<Example id="hook-in-c" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Do stays on home, sol on 5, in C and in A alike. These singing names are **solfège**: one syllable for each degree, and because do moves with home, it is called movable do.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The seven syllables, in order from home: do re mi fa sol la ti, for degrees 1 2 3 4 5 6 7. After ti comes do again, the 1 above.

Do is always home, in every key on the circle. So do mi sol is 1 3 5 in C, in A, in E♭: the same sound, from a new home.

The numbers do not go away: they stay on the labels, and when you sing, numbers and syllables both count. In code nothing changes: do is `n` 0, sol is `n` 4.

<Example id="do-to-do" views="code,roll" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The syllables in your voice: the 1 3 5 you know, then the two neighbours of do.

<Sing exercise="sing-on-syllables" />

<OnPass>Do mi sol, ti up into do, re down into do. Syllables you can sing quickly, each one a degree.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
All seven degrees again, as in Leading Tone & Six, and now each one has a syllable. Singing the note on a syllable helps your voice find the way to do.

<EarDrill family="fam.deg.fet-major" level={3} trials={7} />

<Callout kind="tip">Do, mi and sol sound at rest. The others lean toward one of them.</Callout>

<OnPass>Every degree by ear, each with a name to sing. Next lesson you use those names to sing a tune straight off the page.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now from your ear to the code: hear syllables, write numbers. The roll shows the target after one try.

<Editor exercise="write-what-you-sing" />

<OnPass>Do re mi re do is `0 1 2 1 0`: each syllable a degree, each degree one less in `n`.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Every note now has three names: a letter for the keyboard, a number for the code, a syllable for your voice. A line that fits your voice, like your call in Sing It Back, is one you can sing back on syllables.
