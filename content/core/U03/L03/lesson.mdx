<Step id="S1">
1, 3 and 5 are the stable notes of the key, the ones you sang in Degrees in Code. This line spends its time on the notes between them.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>2 and 4 do the waiting. Bar 1 hangs on 2 before the rest, and in bar 2 the 4 leans until it falls to 3. The 1, 3 and 5 around them let the line rest.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The opening line dipped below home once. Until now every `n` you wrote sat between 0 and 6, from home up to 7, but the scale does not stop there.

<Predict exercise="below-zero" hum />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Six notes, from under home to over it.

<Example id="under-and-over" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The labels repeat every seven numbers. `n` 7 is 1 an octave up, and counting down from 0 walks below home: `n` -1 is the 7 under it, `n` -3 the 5. `n` counts from 0, so degree 5 is `n` 4, or `n` -3 an octave lower.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
In High & Low you moved a line an octave by changing the number after each letter. In degrees, an octave is a count of numbers instead.

<Editor exercise="an-octave-down" />

<OnPass>`-3 -5 -6 -7`: 5 3 2 1 again, one home lower. Seven numbers to the octave, whatever the key.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the sound, and to the waiting you heard in the opening line.

<Choice exercise="which-one-settles" />

<OnPass>A settles: the 2 steps down to 1 and the tension is gone. A restless note moving to a stable one is a **resolution**. 2 resolves down to 1, and 4 resolves down to 3.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now find the two restless notes by ear, mixed in with the stable ones.

<EarDrill family="fam.deg.fet-major" level={2} trials={6} />

<Callout kind="tip">If the note leans, sing down to the next note and keep going home. One note of the scale down to home means 2; three notes down means 4.</Callout>

<OnPass>2 and 4 by ear. Both sound like a held breath: each one wants the note below it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Now sing the pull: each restless note, then the note it lands on.

<Sing exercise="sing-them-home" />

<OnPass>Each lean, then its landing, and home an octave up. You sang the pull you heard.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
Your lines can now reach past home in both directions, and you can hear which notes lean and which ones rest. Your line from Degrees in Code is back, and this time it plays the four bars after the first loud part of your song. A 2 that leans into a final 1 makes a landing clear.

<Make exercise="over-the-edge" />

<Recap />
</Step>
