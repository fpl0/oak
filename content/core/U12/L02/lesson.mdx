<Step id="S1">
Two roots, C and F, over one groove, played twice with a different kind of seventh chord each time. Hear the difference before anything is named.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Loop 1 could sit there all night; in loop 2 every chord wants to go somewhere. One note in each chord makes that difference, and the next step finds it. A blues is built from loop 2's kind of chord, which is why it keeps rolling and never quite sits still.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
You have written both symbols: `C7` in the blues, `C^7` in your soul loop. On screen they differ by one character.

<Predict exercise="which-note-moves" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The two chords side by side, stacked by hand so the roll shows every note, then Fmaj7 for two bars.

<Example id="half-step" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Bar 2. Cmaj7 and C7 share C, E and G; the fourth note is B in one and B♭ in the other, a half step apart. With B on top the chord rests. With B♭ it leans, and it leans toward the chord a 5th below: C7 into F, the way G7 leans into C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A major seventh chord cannot stand on every degree of a key. One chord of your soul loop now shows why.

<Editor exercise="one-note-from-outside" />

<OnPass>`G7`: G, B, D and F. On G the key's own notes make a dominant seventh, never a major seventh chord: its 7th is F, a whole step under the next G. That is the chord that leans home to C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
By ear, listen for the mood before the note. Inside a key, one rests where it is; the other leans on toward the chord a 5th below. (A blues can end on a dominant seventh, since there it is home.)

<Choice exercise="rest-or-lean" />

<OnPass>Rest or lean, told apart by ear on roots that kept changing. Oak will bring this pair back in your warm-ups, and the minor seventh chord will join it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The two 7ths, one after the other, against the same low C. Singing them puts the half step in your own throat.

<Sing exercise="ti-then-te" />

<OnPass>Do, ti, do: ti is so close under do that it barely leaves. Do, te, do: te sits a whole step under, and it would sooner fall on to la than climb back. That pull is C7 leaning into F.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A lesson ago a seventh chord was a colour. Now you can choose what it does: stay, or lean on. Your four bars are plain triads under a tune that climbs to a held note in every bar.

<Make exercise="rest-and-lean" />

<Callout kind="tip">The tune is in note names today, so it can reach any key on the keyboard. Over C7, change bar 2's held `a4` to `bb4`, the chord's own 7th; bar 3 lands back on `a4` under Fmaj7. Over G7, hold `f4`: it falls to the `e4` that starts the loop again.</Callout>

One half step decides whether a chord stays or goes. Next: the two dark sevenths, both built on the diminished triad.

</Step>
