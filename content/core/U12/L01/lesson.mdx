<Step id="S1">
In Seventh Colour you stacked one more 3rd on each chord of this loop, and it turned from pop into soul. Today those four-note chords get their names.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Same roots, same tune. In the plain loop three tune notes sat outside their triads: B over C, G over Am, F over G. With one more note stacked on, the chords take those notes in, and the edge turns into colour: softer and warmer.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
In the blues you wrote `C7`. Today's symbol, `C^7`, has one more character and one different note.

<Step id="S3">
Here are the loop's first two chords, each a triad you know with one more note on top.

<Example id="two-kinds" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>C E G B: the C major triad with B on top, eleven half steps above C and one below the next C. That is a **major seventh chord**, Cmaj7, written `C^7` in code: soft, settled, happy to stay. A C E G is the A minor triad with a ♭7 on top: a **minor seventh chord**, Am7, mellow and less sad than the plain minor triad.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build one with your hands, with the same recipe as Cmaj7, on F.

<OnPass>F, A, C and E: Fmaj7, all white keys. The E sits a half step under the F above, close to the root's octave without landing on it. That nearness is the soft glow you heard in the soul loop.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back in code, the loop from the start of the lesson, with three of its chords still plain.

<Editor exercise="name-the-sevenths" />

<OnPass>`C^7`, `Am7`, `Dm7`, `G7`. On paper the first is Cmaj7; Strudel reads `^` as "major seventh". In Seventh Colour you built this loop note by note. Now you can call every chord in it by name.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
In code the kind is in the name. By ear, one clue already works: the triad inside the chord.

<Choice exercise="major-seven-or-minor-seven" />

<OnPass>Major triad underneath: Cmaj7's kind. Minor triad underneath: Am7's kind. The next lesson's pair is harder, because both have a major triad inside.</OnPass>
</Step>
