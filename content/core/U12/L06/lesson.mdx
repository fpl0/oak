<Step id="S3">
Bar 1 is Cmaj7 stacked by hand from C, as you first met it in this unit. Bar 2 is the same chord from the left hand.

<Example id="stack-then-left" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The hand leaves out the root and lets the bass play it. That is a **rootless voicing**: the 3rd and the 7th, plus one or two notes that colour them. In its place the hand adds D, an octave and a 2nd above C. An interval wider than an octave is a **compound interval**: a 9th is a 2nd an octave up, a 10th a 3rd an octave up. That 9th is where the left-hand chord gets its shine.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The hand also turns intervals upside down. In the stack, B was a 7th above C; in the left hand B is the lowest note, under E and G.

<OnPass>A major 3rd turned over is a minor 6th; a perfect 5th turned over is a perfect 4th. Moving the lower note up an octave is an **interval inversion**. The two numbers add up to 9, major becomes minor and minor becomes major, and perfect stays perfect. C to the high E is a major 10th, a 3rd an octave up.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to the trio, with its chords in the voicing you know.

<Editor exercise="voice-it-for-the-left-hand" />

<OnPass>One call and the chords move into the middle of the keyboard. From Dm7 to G7 only one note moves: C slides down to B, the 7th of one chord becoming the 3rd of the next. The bass carries the roots, so nothing is missing.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Choice exercise="all-five-by-ear" />

<OnPass>All five kinds, by ear, on any root. A voicing changes where the notes sit, never which kind of chord it is, so these five will follow you into every voicing.</OnPass>
