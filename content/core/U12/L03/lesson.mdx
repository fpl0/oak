<Step id="S1">
The seventh chords you know are warm, mellow or restless. Two darker ones remain, and each hides inside a loop.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Both new chords pull the music onward. The first aches, then falls through E7 to A minor, a turn heard under many sad ballads. The second is a stepping stone, gone as the bass rises a half step into Dm7. Both are built on the diminished triad.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
In code, the first chord of the turn looks like a chord you already use, with two more characters on the end.

<Predict exercise="what-b5-lowers" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Bar 1 is that chord, stacked by hand from its root. Bar 2 lowers its top note once more.

<Example id="two-stacks" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Bar 1 is B D F A: the diminished triad with a ♭7 on top. That is a **half-diminished seventh**, written Bm7♭5 and in code `Bm7b5`: Bm7 with its 5th lowered. Bar 2 lowers the 7th once more, to A♭, and now every note is three half steps from the next. That is a **diminished seventh**, B°7, in code `Bo7`. The first leads into E7 and on to A minor; the second pulls up a half step, B°7 into C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Now build the first of the two with your hands.

<OnPass>B, D, F and A: Bm7♭5, all white keys. It is the chord A minor's own notes build on degree 2, so a minor key carries its ache inside it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back in code: the turn from the start of the lesson, with one wrong note in its first chord. Listen for the tune rubbing against it.

<Editor exercise="the-keys-own-chord" />

<OnPass>`Bm7b5`, `E7`, `Am7`. Every note of the first chord now belongs to A minor, and the F in the tune sits inside it. Half-diminished, then a dominant seventh, then the minor home chord: this is how a minor key comes home in a great many songs.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
By ear the question is the 5th. The first two chords stand on the same root, so you can hear that one note sink.

<Choice exercise="calm-or-hollow" />

<OnPass>A minor triad underneath: a minor seventh chord, calm. A diminished triad underneath: half-diminished, with a tritone between its root and its 5th. That tritone is what you hear as the ache.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A torch song: the piano marks every beat and the strings carry the tune, yet every chord in it is one you could already write. In your song its first four bars end the bridge, in A minor, just before the last chorus, where Oak plays Bm7♭5 into E7. One of today's chords gives it an ache, and the raised 7 from Raise the Seventh can answer it in the tune.

<Make exercise="your-dark-turn" />

<Callout kind="tip">Over E7 and over G♯°7 the tune can sing G♯, the raised 7 of harmonic minor: `n` counts from 0, so degree 7 is `n` 6. Keep it off Am7, where the chord has G.</Callout>
