<Step id="S1">
The staircase walked its seventh chords by step. These eight bars, in A minor, walk another way: each root falls a 5th, the motion under many jazz standards.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The chords travel a long way, from D round the cycle to A, while the top line only steps down: F, E, D, C, each held for two bars. It moves less than any chord under it, yet it fits every one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Two of the chords, side by side, show how one held note can fit both.

<Predict exercise="the-note-that-stays" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The first four bars of the cycle, with the line on top.

<Example id="first-four" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>3rd, 7th, 3rd, 7th. F is the 3rd of Dm7 and stays to become the 7th of G7. That 7th falls a half step to E, the 3rd of Cmaj7, which stays to become the 7th of Fmaj7. The 3rd and the 7th of a chord are its **guide tones**. Over the root they say most of what kind of chord it is; only the 5th tells Bm7 from Bm7♭5. And when roots fall a 5th, the guide tones stay put or move by a step from chord to chord, never a leap.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A minor has seven sevenths too, the same seven as C major counted from A, with E7 borrowed from harmonic minor as in Raise the Seventh.

<OnPass>i7, iv7, iiø7, V7. Home is a minor seventh chord now, the half-diminished sits on 2, and the raised 7, G♯, gives V its dominant seventh: the pull that brings the loop back to Am7.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Now the cycle's last four bars, where the thread has to find its own way home.

<Editor exercise="finish-the-thread" />

<OnPass>D, D, C, C. D is the 3rd of Bm7♭5 and the 7th of E7; it falls one step to C, the 3rd of Am7, and rests there. Four notes, each held for two bars, have carried the whole cycle home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Back to the first four chords. Sing the thread and you are holding the progression together yourself.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-thread" />

<OnPass>Fa, fa, mi, mi: two notes for four chords. Each chord changed under you while the line held still or slid one half step.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The cycle plays with its bass under a tune of one A a bar. Guide tones make a skeleton for a tune: one at the start of each bar, with your own rhythm hung on it, or a motif you vary from bar to bar, as in Motif & Variation.
