<Step id="S2">
The new method sits between the stacks and `.scale()`. Its numbers are not degrees: they point into the stack, in the order its notes are written, the first one as 0. Every stack here is written lowest note first, so 0 is the lowest.

<Step id="S3">
The hook's pattern, `0 1 2 1`, plays lowest, middle, highest, middle. Change it and listen.

<Example id="the-loop" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>One number, one note, sharing the bar like any pattern. `0 1 2` climbs, `2 1 0` falls, and `0 2 1` skips past the middle and comes back to it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Same chords, same shape, twice as many notes. This is how a pianist keeps a slow song moving.

<Editor exercise="twice-as-fast" />

<OnPass>Up and back down, twice a bar. A chord played one note at a time is an **arpeggio**: the notes of the triad, spread out in time instead of stacked. The word is Italian, from playing on a harp.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
In Stacking Thirds your chords could only strike all at once. Now each one can walk, skip or pause inside its bar. Broken, they play the four bars that open your song, over Oak's bass and its soft held chords, as the drums come in. A `~` in the pattern makes a gap, and a tune on top can fill it.
