<Step id="S1">
Bass lines live far below the treble staff: written there, a `c2` would hang eight short lines under it. Listen to this one, and watch where the page puts it instead.

<Example id="hook" views="code" autoplay />

<OnPass>The bass sits on each chord's root on beats 1 and 3 and moves to the chord's 5th on beat 4. Root and 5th carry a great many bass lines, and both sit far too low for the treble staff.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Two staves this time, one above the other. The bass and the chords each get their own.

<Example id="two-staves" views="code" />

<OnPass>The lower staff has a clef of its own, with two dots either side of its fourth line. That line is F3, and every line and space follows from it, from G2 at the bottom up to A3 at the top. This is the **bass clef**. Brace a treble staff above it and you have the **grand staff**, with middle C on a short line between the two. A pianist reads it this way, left hand below, right hand above.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The same letters as on the treble staff, in new places. The lines are worth knowing by heart.

<Staff example="four-lines" />

<Choice exercise="name-the-lines" />

<OnPass>G, B, D, F, then A on the top line: each line skips a letter, like any staff. Many players remember them as Good Boys Do Fine Always. Below the staff the short lines go on down, E2 and then C2.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Play from the page. No code to read, no roll: only the bass part, the way a bass player gets it.

<Staff example="the-page" />

<Editor exercise="play-the-bass-part" />

<OnPass>`c2 e2 g2 e2`, then `f2 a2 c3 a2`: up the C triad and back, then up the F triad and back, each under the band's own chord. Bass parts often look like this, the chord's own notes starting from its root.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
From code names to the page.

<StaffInput exercise="write-the-roots" />

<OnPass>Second short line below, bottom line, first space, the space below the staff. The four roots of the four-chord loop, where a bass player reads them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The loop of C, G, Am and F has always stood on the band's bass; now the bass is yours. It takes Oak's place under the second part of your song and under both loud parts, all three on C, G, Am and F. The starter has the right notes an octave too high, right where the chords sit, and it muddies them. Once it sits low, each bar can hold the hook's root and 5th, or a climb through the chord as in Play from the page.
