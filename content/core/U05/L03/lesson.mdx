<Step id="S1">
Every triad you have stacked so far, major or minor, could sit still at the end of a loop. Two more kinds cannot.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The top note creeps up a half step at a time, G, G♯, A, and bar 2 sounds stretched on the way. Bar 4 does not sound finished at all. It leans into bar 1, and the loop has to go round.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Some chords can end a song. Others sound like a question.

<Choice exercise="which-one-moves-on" />

<OnPass>B, D and F hangs in the air. It wants to go somewhere, and in the hook it went to C.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
That restless chord is the stack on degree 7, the one triad of the key you have not looked at closely. Here it is, followed by where it leans.

<Example id="seven-then-home" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three half steps, then three more: two minor 3rds make a **diminished triad**. Its outer notes, B and F, are the tritone, a half step short of the 5th that major and minor triads share. Bar 2 of the hook did the opposite. C, E, G♯ stacks two major 3rds, and the 5th grows to eight half steps: an **augmented triad**, bright but unsettled, as if still rising.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
In the hook, bar 2 was C major with one note raised. Build that chord yourself.

<Editor exercise="make-it-rise" />

<OnPass>C, E, G♯: two major 3rds. The raised 5th keeps its letter, G, and takes a sharp, and it rises on to A in the next chord, the way the tune does.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
All four kinds now. Ask first whether the chord could end a song: that splits the four into two pairs.

<EarDrill family="fam.chd.quality-triad" level={4} trials={8} />

<OnPass>Settled or restless, then bright or dark: two questions name all four kinds.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Spelling works as in Major & Minor, now with all four kinds.

<Choice exercise="spell-all-four" />

<OnPass>Two 3rds every time: their sizes give the kind, and the letters always skip one.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
The editor opens with your loop from Stacking Thirds, where every chord could sit still. A restless chord is useful because of what comes after it: it needs somewhere to go. In your song this loop plays the four bars that climb into the first loud part, over Oak's drums, and a tune on top can climb with the chords.
