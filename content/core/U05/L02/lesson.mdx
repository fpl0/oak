<Step id="S3">
In Major, Minor, Perfect you measured 3rds in half steps. A triad holds two of them: bottom to middle, middle to top.

<Example id="count-the-thirds" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>A up to C is three half steps, a minor 3rd; A up to C♯ is four, a major 3rd. The top 3rd changes the other way, so the outer 5th stays seven half steps. A major 3rd at the bottom with a minor 3rd on top makes a **major triad**, bright and settled. A minor 3rd at the bottom with a major 3rd on top makes a **minor triad**, darker and more inward.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Same move, new chord, and this time the tune tells you which way to go.

<Editor exercise="brighten-d" />

<OnPass>D, F♯, A: the middle note went up one key, the chord turned bright, and the rub with the tune is gone.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
From a name to three notes, on paper.

<Choice exercise="spell-the-triad" />

<OnPass>Spelled from the root, a triad always skips a letter, twice. That is why D major is D F♯ A, never D G♭ A.</OnPass>

<Step id="S7">
