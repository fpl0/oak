<Step id="S1">
Three chords you know from three-chord rock, now as dominant sevenths, in an order that blues, rock and roll and soul players have shared for over a hundred years.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Four bars at home, a step away and back, then the biggest move near the end before the form falls home and starts again. The same shape comes round every twelve bars, so after one time through you can feel where you are.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Read the changes before you hear them again. One string holds the whole form, and both the chords and the bass read it.

<Predict exercise="read-the-changes" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Now name what you felt, bar by bar.

<Example id="three-chords" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>Three chords, I7, IV7 and V7, in a fixed order: four bars of I, two of IV, two of I, then V, IV, I and V. That is the **12-bar blues**. V arrives in bar 9, the furthest point from home, and bar 12 sends you round again. One time through the twelve bars is called a chorus of the blues.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The first two lines of the blues are written. The last line is yours.

<Editor exercise="the-last-line" />

<OnPass>G7, F7, C7, G7: V, IV, I, V. With the last line in place the form is whole, and it loops on its own.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Players keep their place in a blues by ear: they know which chord is coming because they know which bar they are in.

<EarDrill family="fam.frm.blues" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Bars 1–4 home, 5–6 on IV, 7–8 home again, then V, IV, I, V. Once that shape is in your ear you can find your place in any blues.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The bass of a blues is its map. Once you have sung it, you can hear the form without counting.

<Sing exercise="sing-the-blues-roots" />

<OnPass>Twelve syllables, and the whole form is in your voice: the long wait on do, the short visit to fa, and the turn at sol that brings it home.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Twelve bars of C7 leave the riff nowhere to go; the blues form gives it something to ride on. The riff's E♭, the blue 3rd of Blue Notes, bites over C7 and settles into F7 as that chord's ♭7.

<Make exercise="your-first-blues" />

<CanDo id="cd.read.twelve-bar" />

You can now write a whole blues in one line of code and keep your place in it by ear. Next: the two changes most blues records make to these twelve bars.

</Step>
