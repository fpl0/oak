<Step id="S1">
You have two pentatonics now, one sweet and one dark. The blues lives between them. Here is an organ vamp on C and F with a riff on top, twice: the rhythm stays, the notes and the guitar change.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The second riff rubs: some of its notes sound minor against chords that stay major. That rub, more than the distortion, is what makes it sound like the blues.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The second riff's scale word is new, and so is one of its notes.

<Predict exercise="one-note-more" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
The new scale in full, up to the octave and back, over the vamp.

<Example id="six-notes" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>1 ♭3 4 ♭5 5 ♭7, sung do me fa se sol te: the minor pentatonic with ♭5 squeezed in between 4 and 5. That is the **blues scale**. Its three flattened notes, ♭3, ♭5 and ♭7, played over major chords like C and F, are the **blue notes**: a minor sound against a major one, the rub at the heart of the blues.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
A blue note does not have to stay put. Played just before the note above it, it sounds like a string bent up into place.

<Editor exercise="bend-into-the-3rd" />

<OnPass>E♭ rising into E: the blue 3rd bending into the chord's own 3rd. One note name, and the lick has the blues in it. On a guitar or a voice the bend is one smooth slide, and singers often hold it between the two keys, where a piano can only hint at it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Three scales now share home and most of their notes.

<Choice exercise="which-scale" />

<OnPass>Sweet means 3: the major pentatonic. A biting ♭3 means one of the other two, and a note slipping between 4 and 5 makes it the blues scale.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
The same blue notes can also be written as steps of C major with a flat after them. `n("2b")` is ♭3: `n` 2 is degree 3, and the `b` lowers it. This code plays C E♭ E G♭ G B♭, with E♭ and G♭ each leading straight into the plain note above. You will write steps like these later in the course; this one is only to read and hear.

<Example id="altered-degrees" views="code,roll" />
</Step>
