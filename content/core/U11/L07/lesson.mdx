<Step id="S1">
Every blues you have written so far marched. Keep the chords and the notes, change only how the eighth notes are spaced, and it walks like a different song.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The first version marches: every hat sits the same distance from the next. In the second the hats fall into pairs, a long one and a short one, and the riff follows them. Not one note changed; only their spacing did.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Strip the groove down to the hats, the kick and the snare. One call is added to the hats.

<Predict exercise="which-hats-move" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Look at one beat at a time on the roll.

<Example id="the-groove" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>The second eighth of every beat waits until two thirds of the way through it: long, then short. That feel is **swing**. A groove built on swung eighths, like the one you just heard under the blues, is a **shuffle**. The second number counts the slices the call swings: one per beat, so 4 in 4/4 and 3 in a waltz.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The notes are written even. The feel comes from the call, one per line that swings.

<Editor exercise="shuffle-the-blues" />

<OnPass>Hats and riff both lope now, and the bass and the chords did not need it: they play on the beat, where swing changes nothing.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
On paper a swung riff is written in plain eighths, and players play them long–short. Your code works the same way.

<Example id="the-page" views="staff" />

<Editor exercise="swing-from-the-page" />

<OnPass>Written straight, swung by one call: the page and the code say the same thing in the same way.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Every groove you hear from now on is straight or swung.

<EarDrill family="fam.rhy.swing" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Even hats tick; swung hats roll. A shuffle blues and most jazz swing; most rock keeps its eighths even. The hats tell you which within a bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A blues like the one you wrote last lesson, quick change and turnaround included, still marches. It plays your song's third chorus, where Oak's band already shuffles and Oak's tune keeps singing on top. How hard it swings is yours to choose, and so is how fast it walks: your `setcpm` sets the pace of the whole song.
