<Step id="S1">
The loop you saved in Hello, Loop had drums under everything. Today the drums come apart, starting with the one thing every groove has.

<Example id="groove" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Your nod lands with the kick and the snare, evenly spaced like footsteps. The hats tick twice as fast on top, but a nodding head follows the steadier step underneath.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Now put that step in your hands, the way you would tap your foot.

<Tap exercise="tap-the-pulse" />

<Callout kind="tip">Oak measures your timing against your own setup, so a little lag from your keyboard is already taken into account.</Callout>

<OnPass>That steady beat you tapped is the **pulse**. Almost every song you know has one, and the drums usually say it out loud.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
So far the first line has read `setcpm(96/4)`.

<Predict exercise="bigger-number" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
The line `setcpm(132/4)` is the speed dial for everything below it. <CodeRef fn="setcpm" />

<OnPass>The first number is the **tempo**: how many beats go by in a minute. 132 is brisk, 70 is a slow walk, and most pop sits between 90 and 130.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Strudel plays the words inside the quotes from left to right, then starts again. Watch the roll while it loops.

<OnPass>One trip through the quotes is one **bar**. The tempo line keeps every bar four beats long, so more words in the same bar sit closer together. The `/4` at the end of the tempo line is those four beats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Slow grooves have their place, but this one should move.

<Editor exercise="set-120" />

<OnPass>Same drums, 120 beats per minute: two beats every second.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
A kick on every beat is the plainest pulse there is. Dance music builds whole tracks on it.

<Editor exercise="kick-every-beat" />

<OnPass>Four kicks, one per beat. `bd*4` writes the same bar: `*` repeats a sound inside its slot, the way `hh*8` gave you eight hats.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
You started this lesson nodding to someone else's beat. Now write your own and choose its speed. Your beat plays the first four bars of your song, and the whole song takes your tempo. A second line on top, the way the hats rode over the first groove, is welcome, and `*` saves you typing a sound four times.
