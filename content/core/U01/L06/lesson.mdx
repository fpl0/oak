<Step id="S1">
Two per Beat split each beat in two. These hats go further, and toms tumble down at the end of every bar. The beat underneath is still four to a bar.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<Callout kind="tip">Drummers count this out loud as "one-e-and-a, two-e-and-a". The words keep your place when the sounds come quickly.</Callout>

<OnPass>Busier, not faster. The tempo is close to your last grooves, and your nod keeps its steady pace while the hats race above it. The toms run down through the last beat and hand you back to beat 1.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
The toms are gone and the full backbeat is back. Only the hats line is new to you.

<Predict exercise="count-the-hats" />
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Every beat now holds four hats, and each gets a word: the beat's number, then e, then &, then a. Beside the roll, the same bar is written out the way a drummer reads it.

<Example id="count-it" views="code,roll" />

<OnPass>That hat is the e of 3. Each hat in this bar is a **sixteenth note**: a quarter of a beat, four to a beat, sixteen to a bar. It is half an eighth note, the way an eighth is half a quarter.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Quarter-note hats are steady but stiff. Make them run.

<Editor exercise="sixteen-hats" />

<OnPass>Sixteen hats, four on every beat. `[hh hh hh hh]*4` writes the same bar: one beat's worth, four times over.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Toms are the drums a drummer often rolls across at the end of a bar: `ht` high, `mt` mid, `lt` low. A quick run of them on the last beat pulls the ear into the next bar. `!` repeats a sound as its own step: `ht!2` is `ht ht`.

<Editor exercise="tom-run" />

<OnPass>Two high, two mid, all inside beat 4: 4, e, &, a. Without the brackets each tom would take a slot of its own, and the bar would hold seven slots instead of four.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now by ear and by hand. The counting words help most in the busy beat.

<Tap exercise="echo-sixteenths" />
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
Two lessons ago a beat could hold two sounds. Now it holds four, and each one has a word. The starter is an eighth-note groove. A tom run with `!`, or a line on another drum machine with `.bank()`, can go on top.

<Make exercise="busy-groove" />

</Step>
