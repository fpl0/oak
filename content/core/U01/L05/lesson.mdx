<Step id="S1">
Until now every word in your bars got an equal share of time. In this slower groove one sound gets more.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>The crash: it hits on 1 and rings on through beat 2. The kick takes 3 and the snare takes 4. Two beats, one, one, and next you see how drummers write that down.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Here is the drum line of that bar written the way drummers read it, on five lines, with the roll beside it. Each drum has its own height: the kick low, the snare in the middle, cymbals at the top with x-shaped heads. The two 4s at the start say there are four beats to a bar.

<Example id="on-paper" views="code,roll,staff" />

<Callout kind="tip">The roll shows length as width. On paper, length is in the shape of the note: an open or a filled head, a stem or none, a flag or a beam. Kick and snare are drawn with stems in opposite directions so the two parts never blur together.</Callout>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
`@` and `_` both hold a sound for longer. The words in a bar share it out, and `@2` gives a word two shares. Watch how the long kick is drawn. <CodeRef fn="@" />

<OnPass>Each `sd` lasts one beat: a **quarter note**, the filled head with a plain stem. The hats in the first groove lasted half a beat each, two per beat: **eighth notes**, drawn with a flag, or joined by a beam when they come in pairs.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Every shape in this bar has already appeared in the last two steps.

<Choice exercise="read-the-bar" />

For comparison, one kick held for the whole bar:

<Example id="whole-bar" views="code,staff" />

<OnPass>The open head with a stem lasts two beats: a **half note**, `@2` in a bar of four shares. The open head with no stem lasts all four: a **whole note**, like the lone `bd@4` that takes the whole bar. Silence has shapes too: a `~` one beat long is drawn as a quarter rest.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Back to code. A held sound and a sound followed by silence look different on paper, even when you hear the same hit.

<Editor exercise="hold-the-crash" />

<OnPass>`cr@2 bd sd`: a half note and two quarter notes, the same bar you heard at the start.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Now from the page to your hands. Oak plays a click; you tap the rhythm that is written down.

<Tap exercise="tap-what-you-read" />

<OnPass>Half, quarter, quarter: you read it and played it.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
You can now write a length and read it back on paper. Your groove plays the ending of your song, its last four bars, where a crash that rings sounds like the song closing. Mix long and short: a crash that rings, a kick that waits, eighth-note hats in brackets from Two per Beat, a rest from Count to Four. Watch each change appear on paper as you go.
