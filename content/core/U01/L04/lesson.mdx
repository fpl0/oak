<Step id="S1">
The kick and snare from Backbeat, with the hats written two ways. Nothing else changes between the loops.

<Example id="hook" views="code,roll" autoplay />

<OnPass>Loop 2 moves forward. The tempo, the kick and the snare are the same, yet the groove leans ahead as if it were going somewhere. The hats are doing it. Next, you count them.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S2">
Now find out why by counting. Play both cards as often as you like.

<Choice exercise="which-one-moves" />

<OnPass>Version B: two hats in every beat, eight in the bar. The second hat of each pair lands halfway between two beats, and that is what moved the groove forward.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S3">
Version B's hats were written with square brackets, a new tool. Guess what they do to the bar before you hear it. <CodeRef fn="[a b]" />

<Predict exercise="count-the-hats" />
</Step>

<Step id="S4">
Drummers count a split beat with a word in between: 1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and. The numbers land on the beats. Each and lands exactly halfway to the next one. On paper the and is often written &: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &.

<OnPass>Brackets squeeze everything inside them into one beat: `[hh hh]` is two hats in the time of one. Splitting the beat into equal parts like this is **subdivision**, and the and is where the second half begins.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S5">
Most grooves keep the hats busier than the kick and snare. Write Version B's hats yourself.

<Editor exercise="eighth-hats" />

<OnPass>Two hats per beat, eight in the bar. `hh*8` plays the same eight; the brackets show where each beat begins.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S6">
Brackets can hold a rest too. `[~ hh]` is one beat with a silent first half and a hat on its second half: nothing on the number, a hat on the and.

A kick on an and leans the groove forward, toward the next beat.

<Editor exercise="kick-on-the-and" />

<OnPass>`bd [~ bd] ~ ~`: the silent first half of beat 2, then the kick. Hear how that kick leans toward beat 3.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S7">
No code now. Finding an and by ear is the same skill as writing one, turned around.

<EarDrill family="fam.rhy.count" level={1} trials={6} />

<OnPass>Counting the ands is how drummers, bass players and dancers find their place in a busy bar.</OnPass>
</Step>

<Step id="S8">
A beat used to be one word. Now it can hold two, and you choose which half sounds. The editor opens with your backbeat from Backbeat, and what you write here drives both loud parts of your song: split a beat somewhere, with busy hats, a kick on an and or a snare doubled on 4.
